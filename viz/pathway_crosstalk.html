Five signaling pathways, connected

A crosstalk map from the cell model's real data: proteins of five Reactome pathways, laid out top‑to‑down by compartment (where each protein acts) and interconnected by measured physical interactions (PPI). Gold ringed nodes belong to two pathways at once — the hand‑off points.

How to read it. Columns are the five pathways; rows are compartments from outside (top) to nucleus (bottom). Signaling canonically runs downward — a membrane receptor (EGFR) relays through the RAS proteins (HRAS, KRAS, NRAS, GRB2) into both the MAPK and PI3K arms, converges on ERK (MAPK1/MAPK3), and ends at nuclear targets; WNT crosstalks near the nucleus via PP2A (PPP2*). 41 proteins shown (14 shared), 113 within‑pathway and 21 across‑pathway PPI edges.
Honest scope. Vertical position = observed compartment, not a proven flow; edges are measured but undirected physical interactions — real crosstalk and shared machinery, not a proven directional hand‑off. The arrow marks the textbook cascade direction for these pathways, not something derived from the edges. A descriptive near‑field map, not a phenotype predictor.
