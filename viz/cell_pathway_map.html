The whole cell as interconnected pathways

The cell's biology at its coarsest honest resolution: the 25 top-level Reactome systems (node size = number of genes), linked by how much they interconnect — a chord's thickness is the fraction of proteins two systems share (Jaccard ≥ 0.08), corroborated by cross‑system PPI. These systems span 9,682 annotated genes of the cell.

What you're seeing. Each node is a major cell system; a chord means the two systems share machinery. The strongest links are textbook — DNA replication–DNA repair; DNA repair–Cell cycle; DNA replication–Cell cycle; Signal transduction–Development; Responses to stimuli–Development — which is the sanity check that the map is real. 42 interconnections above the threshold.
Honest scope. Interconnection = shared proteins + physical contact — DESCRIPTIVE crosstalk, not causal signal flow and not directional. The biggest hubs (Signal transduction, Metabolism, Gene expression) connect broadly partly because they are huge and full of multifunctional proteins, and Reactome's own annotation choices shape the overlaps. Dark / orphan genes are not here — they have no pathway (that's what the orphan‑network view adds). A map of who‑shares‑and‑touches‑whom across the cell, not a wiring diagram of control.
