The cell is robust, not brittle — knock a part out and it mostly heals and reroutes. That's the first way it declines to be software: it doesn't break the way code breaks.

How good is it, really

Graded by what you'd use it for

Broad, coherent, reproducible — and every claim traces to the evidence that earned it.

as a research decision-support tool
Genuinely useful for triage, grounding and hypothesis-ranking. Doesn't replace the bench.
D
as a discoverer of new biology
Chance. And no cleverness fixes it — it's gated by measurements that don't exist yet.

Its rarest quality isn't a score. It's that it knows which of these it's doing on any given question — and trusts you with the answer. Trust it exactly as far as it says it trusts itself.

A mystery of life isn't a problem to solve — it's a reality to experience.

We built the most rigorous map we could, and its most faithful feature turned out to be its edge: the line past which the cell stops being a problem and goes back to being what it always was underneath the equations. The 55% we can trust doesn't conquer the cell — it makes the rest visible, instead of papering over it with a confident lie. The map keeps drifting into fantasy the moment it stops touching the living thing. So we keep going back and looking.
