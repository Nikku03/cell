# *M. tuberculosis* persistence — McFadden & Hingley-Wilson

**Their area.** Mycobacterial persistence and drug tolerance; single-cell mycobacterial behaviour.

## Where the offer would sit

The TB relapse case (document 01, Maiello/Fortune/Flynn/Lin) produced two results that are not
specific to macaques:

1. **The spread of residual burdens across lesions does not move relapse probability.** Only the
   total does, unless burden and drug escape covary across sites. That is an exact algebraic
   statement, verified to 3 × 10⁻¹⁶.
2. **Lesion count and per-lesion dissemination probability are structurally unidentifiable** from
   relapse counts alone — the likelihood depends on them only through n·log(1−p). Flat to
   10⁻¹⁶ along that curve. No sample size fixes it.

Both apply to any system where sterilisation is asked of multiple semi-independent sites, which is
the shape of TB.

## What would be needed

| What | Why |
|---|---|
| A single-cell or per-site residual-burden distribution | §1: converts the identity into a number |
| Any paired measurement of burden **and** local drug exposure/killing | Sets Cov(q,N) — the one quantity the answer turns on, and as far as we can find, unmeasured by anyone |
| A biphasic time-kill curve with both slopes stated | The Van Bambeke analysis (document 02) applies directly and gives a free upper bound on the persister formation rate |

## The question, as a question

*Is there any dataset — yours or anyone's — in which residual mycobacterial burden and local drug
exposure were measured on the same sites? That covariance is the single number that decides whether
mean-field estimates of sterilisation understate or overstate the risk, and we can show it is
exactly what the answer depends on.*
