T4  THE SECRETORY CHAIN  (does multi-step transit change the tail?)
=================================================================================================
  A protein transits ER -> Golgi -> surface. Against a single effective step matched on
  the SAME mean transit time, does the number of stages change the late tail?
    stages  CV of transit        P(t>2)        P(t>3)        P(t>5)
         1         1.0000    1.3534e-01    4.9787e-02    6.7379e-03
         2         0.7071    9.1578e-02    1.7351e-02    4.9940e-04
         3         0.5774    6.1969e-02    6.2322e-03    3.9308e-05
         5         0.4472    2.9253e-02    8.5664e-04    2.6691e-07
         8         0.3536    9.9998e-03    4.7500e-05    1.6640e-10

  a three-stage chain against one step, at five times the mean transit: 6.7379e-03 against 3.9308e-05, a factor of 171.4
  Multi-step trafficking is a NOISE FILTER on transit time: the coefficient of variation
  falls as 1/sqrt(stages), and the late tail falls by orders. A model that collapses the
  secretory pathway into one effective step OVERSTATES how often a protein arrives late by
  that factor. This is residence.py's question asked of trafficking, and unlike there the
  answer is that the structure matters a great deal.

=================================================================================================
T5  WHAT TRAFFICKING COSTS IN PARAMETERS
=================================================================================================
  transport reactions in Recon3D                     4230
  of which enzyme-catalysed, so carrying a kcat      2337
  as a share of all enzyme-catalysed reactions       39.4%

  The asymmetry that matters: a diffusion coefficient follows from size and viscosity to
  within a factor of a few, so space is nearly free to parameterise. A transport rate is a
  pump's or a carrier's turnover number -- active, protein-mediated, and derivable from no
  physics at all. Every one of these must be measured exactly like a metabolic kcat.
  So of the two things usually filed together under 'spatial biology', the one that is
  safe to ignore is cheap to parameterise, and the one that is not is expensive.
