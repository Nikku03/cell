POST-RUN READING.  Hand-written.  G3's WITHDRAWAL VERDICT IS NOT ACCEPTED AS PRINTED.
=================================================================================================

G3 printed the withdrawal reading -- that the sensitive set grows as the cell is forced off its
optimum, so recon.py's metabolic sparsity was an artefact of optimality. Three defects in how that
number was produced mean it cannot be accepted, and all three are mine.

ONE, THE COUNT IS CENSORED. The probe examines the top 40 reactions by dual magnitude. Two of the
three programmes return exactly 40 -- every reaction probed came back sensitive -- so for those the
count is a LOWER BOUND and the true value is unknown. A table whose entries pile up on the edge of
the instrument is reporting the instrument.

TWO, THE REGULATION COSTS NOTHING, SO NOTHING IS BEING CONSTRAINED. Growth runs
28.854821, 28.854790, 28.854729, 28.854605, 28.854451, 28.854297 across the whole sweep -- a
change of 0.002% while 5,903 of 5,938 enzyme floors are active. G1 passed on its letter, that the
floors bind, and failed on its intent, that the cell is thereby made suboptimal. The proteome
budget was never scarce in this formulation -- wholecell.py measured its shadow price as exactly
zero -- so committing 85% of it to a fixed programme costs almost nothing. This knob does not make
a growth-maximiser into a regulated cell; it makes it into a growth-maximiser wearing floors.

THREE, THE THRESHOLD IS FAR BELOW WHAT COULD MATTER. A reaction is counted sensitive at
|d log mu / d log k| > 1e-9. The whole-cell work established that ribosome speed scores 0.977 and
the best individual enzyme 7.75e-04; a sensitivity of 1e-9 could not move a factor-of-two answer
if every rate in the cell had it. Counting at that threshold measures how many reactions are not
exactly zero, which is a different question from how many matter.

WHAT IS NEVERTHELESS REAL, AND WORTH KEEPING. The mechanism the sweep exposes is genuine even if
the count is not: pinning an enzyme at a programme-set floor converts v_j <= kcat_j E_j into a hard
cap at a fixed value, so a cell allocating protein by programme rather than by need has many more
reactions sitting at their enzyme-imposed ceiling than an optimiser does. That is a real
consequence of regulation and it is the right mechanism to measure. The measurement just has to be
made with an uncensored probe, at a threshold that means something, and with a knob that actually
costs the cell something.

G5's finding stands independently and was the first thing the run caught: at phi = 0 the count
here is 10 against recon.py's 8, which passes G2's factor-of-two bar, but the OVERLAP is 1/8. Same
sparsity, almost entirely different reactions, because this formulation binds a proteome budget and
per-reaction enzyme variables where recon.py bound flux limits. A matching count made of different
reactions is a different result, which is why G5 exists.

THE RERUN, and what it changes: probe 150 rather than 40 to remove the censoring, report the count
at thresholds of 1e-3, 1e-4 and 1e-6 rather than 1e-9 so the number means something, and record
the magnitude distribution rather than a bare count. Until then recon.py's conclusion is neither
confirmed nor withdrawn -- it is untested, which is the honest state.
