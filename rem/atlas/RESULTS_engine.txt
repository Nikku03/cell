H2b  THE SCALING LAW -- how cost at FIXED accuracy grows with system size
=================================================================================================
  criterion: 1% relative error in Var(total ON count), a global observable whose meaning
  does not change with N. Cheapest configuration of each route that reaches it.
       N       route 1 r-ball        route 2 width                   HYBRID
       6            126 (r=5)            126 (w=5)            176 (r=1,L=2)
       8            382 (r=6)            510 (w=7)            432 (r=2,L=2)
      10           1534 (r=8)           2046 (w=9)            560 (r=2,L=2)
      12           4094 (r=9)          8190 (w=11)            688 (r=2,L=2)

  growth from N=6 to N=12:  route 1 x32.5   route 2 x65.0   hybrid x3.9
  Route 2 grows as 2^N exactly -- 126, 510, 2046, 8190 is 4x per two variables. Route 1
  grows nearly as fast. The hybrid grows LINEARLY, and it LOSES at N = 6: at small N
  exactness is cheap and the history machinery is not worth its overhead. That crossover
  is what makes the rest credible; a method that won everywhere would be suspect.
  H2b: PASS -- the hybrid scales fundamentally better, which is the only claim a hybrid can earn

H7  WHERE DOES IT STILL FAIL?
=================================================================================================
  unsigned minimum over all hybrid points: 5.577e-03  -- NOT quotable, it is the
  zero crossing of the r=1 branch that H2a identified.
  CONVERGED hybrid accuracy (H  r=2, L=8, cost 2.765e+04): 7.090e-03 tail
  Against the 1% bar: MET on the converged value.
  The residual is carried by the same thing history.py R5 priced: the controller's path
  between sample times. Halving that residual means halving dt, which doubles L, which
  squares the cost -- the hybrid moves the ceiling, it does not remove it.
