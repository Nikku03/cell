WHAT ACCURACY SHOULD THE ENGINE TARGET? PRICED AGAINST WHAT IT ALREADY LOSES

=================================================================================================
A0  THE CORRECTION THAT COMES FIRST
=================================================================================================
  whatdata inverted a law fitted INSIDE a simulation to get an accuracy per class count,
  and reported it as the useful new object. It was not new. signed.py MEASURED it, on
  held-out yeast promoters, and the column has been sitting beside the cap column this
  ledger has quoted throughout:

       C   MEASURED floors from identity    whatdata SIMULATED
       1                            6.29                 0.930
       2                            2.61                 0.480
       4                            2.09                 0.247
       8                            1.52                 0.127
      16                            1.45                 0.066
      64                            0.19                 0.017

  The simulated column is between 7 and 11 times too small at every point. It is
  WITHDRAWN. whatdata's claim that C = 64 demands 1.7% of a noise floor is wrong: the
  measured demand is 0.19 floors, 19%. The finding whatdata's simulation DID establish --
  that the ladder criterion returns a resolution and not a structural count -- stands,
  because that came from the estimator's behaviour and not from the fitted constant.

A1  THE CEILING GATE: IS THE OBSERVABLE EVEN DETERMINED TO THAT PRECISION?

  accurate the class map should be, price the choice at zero: sweep the pruning budget
  and see how far the reported tail moves on its own.

  controller block: 10 controllers by out-degree, 200 target rows,
  window L = 6, dt = 0.5. TRRUST sha 9b909319ccc8.

        budget             tail   log10 tail   certificate       kept
         1e-01       0.0000e+00         -inf        1.0000          0
         1e-02       0.0000e+00         -inf        1.0000          0
         1e-03       3.7208e-98       -97.43        1.0000      19531
         1e-04       3.7208e-98       -97.43        1.0000      19531
         1e-05       3.7208e-98       -97.43        1.0000      19531

  A1 IS DEGENERATE AND IS SUPERSEDED, NOT DELETED. Two of those rows retained NO paths
  at all -- a tail of exactly zero is an absent number, not a small one -- and the other
  three returned the IDENTICAL tail from the IDENTICAL retained set, because
  engine_budget carries a hard cap of 2e7/n retained paths and that cap, not the budget,
  decided every one of them. The sweep did not vary what it claimed to vary, and the
  'spread' it produced was infinity manufactured from two absent rows. Ledger U, in this
  module's own gate, in the module that opened by correcting another for the same thing.

=================================================================================================
A1b  SWEEPING THE QUANTITY THAT ACTUALLY BINDS: THE RETAINED-PATH CAP
=================================================================================================
  If the reported tail is still moving as the retained set grows, the pruning error is
  not converged and the increments show its size. If it has stopped moving, it has
  converged and comparing it with the class ladder is legitimate.

  THE BUDGET IS SET TO ZERO FOR THIS SWEEP, AND THAT IS NOT A DETAIL. The first attempt
  swept the cap at a fixed budget of 1e-3 and four of six rows returned NO paths. That is
  a real interaction and not a crash: the per-level budget is ABSOLUTE, so a small cap
  retains less mass, the retained mass falls below the budget at the next level, and the
  pruner then drops everything. Cap and budget were confounded and the sweep measured
  their interaction rather than the cap. With the budget at zero the cap alone decides.

      path cap       kept             tail   log10 tail      step
           100        100       1.5395e-99       -98.81          
           300        300       2.9663e-99       -98.53     +0.28
          1000       1000       7.3706e-99       -98.13     +0.40
          3000       3000       1.2285e-98       -97.91     +0.22
         10000      10000       2.6793e-98       -97.57     +0.34
         19531      19531       3.7208e-98       -97.43     +0.14

  movement between the two largest retained sets: 0.14 orders of magnitude.
  movement across the whole retained-set sweep:   1.38 orders of magnitude.
  The certificate column is not used for this and cannot be: the pruner certifies the
  MASS it dropped, which runs to essentially 1 in every row above, and realkinetics
  measured that the mass certificate misses the tail by a factor of 1.9e29.

A3  WHERE THE LEDGER'S OWN CAP TABLE SITS ON THAT LADDER
=================================================================================================
  What a class map costs depends on how many controllers share a class, not on C alone.

    ledger row                          controllers     C  ctrl per class
    class count C = 64                           13    64            0.20
    + hub demotion + factored block             140    64            2.19
    pattern (exact)                               3     3            1.00

  AND THERE IS THE THING THE CAP TABLE HIDES. At the C = 64 row the cap is THIRTEEN
  controllers, and sixty-four classes over thirteen controllers is about one class each.
  SO THE CLASS MAP MAKES NO APPROXIMATION AT ITS OWN OPERATING POINT: with each
  controller alone in its class the projection is exact and costs ZERO accuracy.
  BE PRECISE ABOUT WHAT THAT DOES AND DOES NOT MEAN, because the loose version of this
  sentence is wrong. It does NOT mean the representation buys nothing -- it still buys
  the cap, 13 controllers against the exact pattern's 3, because the per-gene factor is
  a product of slot() terms rather than a 2^(k(L+1)) pattern table. What it means is that
  the ACCURACY cost of classing is zero there, so the 0.19 floors signed.py measured at
  C = 64 is not being paid at the operating point the cap describes. The accuracy is
  paid only where controllers SHARE a class -- the 140-controller row, at 2.2 per class,
  which on the ladder above sits between C = 5 and C = 10.

=================================================================================================
A4b  THE SAME RANKING DONE AS A SCALING LAW, WHICH REVERSES IT
=================================================================================================
  The pruner's LAST STEP is not its error. Its error is how far the tail still has to
  travel, and the sweep shows it rising and not levelling off. It rises for a structural
  reason: retaining more paths can only ADD mass, so every tail this engine reports is a
  LOWER BOUND on the tail it is approximating.

    log10 tail  =  -100.01 + 0.608 * log10(retained paths)      R^2 = 0.997
    measured over 2.00 to 4.29 decades of retained paths

    the pruning error reaches the class ladder's whole span of 5.08 orders
    at 10^12.7 retained paths.
    the full path set this engine is approximating is 10^21.1 paths.

  A4b AS PREDECLARED: the crossing at 10^12.7 lies BELOW the full set of
  10^21.1, by 8.4 decades. PRUNING IS THE BINDING TERM. The point
  comparison in A4 was wrong in the way this build order has been wrong three times
  before: it ranked an approximation at a point instead of by its scaling law.

  AND THE HONEST SIZE OF THE EXTRAPOLATION. The slope is measured over about two decades
  of retained paths and the full set is nineteen decades further out. This is NOT a
  prediction of the true tail. The claim it supports is narrower and does not need the
  far end: the tail is still rising at the largest set that can be enumerated, and it
  only has to keep rising for a few more decades -- a tiny fraction of the way -- before
  the pruner's error exceeds every representation choice inside the engine. Nothing
  measured here suggests it stops.

  SO THE ANSWER TO 'WHAT ACCURACY SHOULD WE TARGET', IN ORDER:
    1. A BOUND ON THE OBSERVABLE, not on the dropped mass. The pruner certifies mass and
       the tail is not a function of mass; realkinetics measured that gap at 1.9e29 and
       this module measures its consequence: the reported tail is a lower bound still
       rising at 0.6 orders per decade of retained paths.
    2. THEN the class map. Its whole ladder is worth 5.08 orders, and at the operating
       point of the C = 64 cap row it is worth NOTHING, because 64 classes over 13
       controllers is one class each.
  Choosing C first optimises the smaller term. That is what this build order has been
  doing for several modules, including the one that asked this question.

=================================================================================================
A5  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
=================================================================================================
  1. Measured at 10 controllers and L = 6, which is what can be enumerated. The
     140-controller row cannot be run; its ratio is placed on the ladder by analogy and
     that step is an assumption, not a measurement.
  2. The retained-set sweep measures how far the tail MOVES, a lower bound on the
     pruning error, not a bound on it. The true error could be larger and nothing here
     bounds it -- that is the open problem, restated in the unit that matters.
  3. base and gain are fixed at the module defaults. realkinetics recorded that base
     moves the tail eight to nine orders for a change of 0.5, so the ABSOLUTE tail is not
     a physical prediction. Every number here is a DIFFERENCE at fixed base, which is why
     the comparison between error terms survives that even though the tail itself does
     not.
