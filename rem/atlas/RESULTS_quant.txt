=================================================================================================
TURBOQUANT HERE: WHAT QUANTIZATION CAN AND CANNOT DO
=================================================================================================
  TurboQuant, arXiv:2504.19874: random rotation makes coordinates near-independent, then
  an optimal scalar quantizer per coordinate; within ~2.7x of the distortion lower bound.

=================================================================================================
Q0  THE CEILING GATE, BEFORE ANY IMPLEMENTATION
=================================================================================================
  Our cost is the NUMBER of entries, 2^(k_i(L+1)) and 2^(|C|(L+1)). Quantization changes
  BITS PER ENTRY. At a fixed byte budget, entry capacity scales as 64/b.
        b  capacity x   bits of exponent   controllers at L=8
        8         8.0               3.00                 0.33
        4        16.0               4.00                 0.44
      3.5        18.3               4.19                 0.47
      2.5        25.6               4.68                 0.52
        1        64.0               6.00                 0.67

  against the shortfalls this session has MEASURED:
    reach a 1e-13 event in a year on 1e4 cores                     28.2 bits
    exact joint over 100 binary species, in a year                129.6 bits
    go from the affordable |C|=3 to the network's 122 at L=8     1071.0 bits
    quantization at 2.5 bits supplies                              4.68 bits
  Q0: quantization buys HALF A CONTROLLER. It is not a fix for state complexity and no
  quantizer can be -- the problem is exponential in coupled variables, quantization is a
  constant on the entries. The tail tier is bounded by TIME, not memory, so it does not
  apply there at all. Everything below is about what it IS good for.

  test object: exact stationary distribution of the closed circuit, 5184 states
  (padded to 8192), solver residual 8.7e-15
  dynamic range of the entries: 1.743e+06  -- heavy-tailed, which is the
  regime a random rotation exists for.

  Q4: the choice of space is the operative engineering decision and it is not close.
  A quantizer optimised for mean-squared error on the RAW table spends its bits on the
  large entries, which is exactly where the tail is not. Quantizing log-probabilities
  spends them evenly in orders of magnitude. Linear quantization also produces NEGATIVE
  probabilities, which have to be clipped before the table is a distribution at all.

Q6  WHAT IT CANNOT DO
=================================================================================================
  1. It cannot change an exponential. 4.68 bits against shortfalls of 28 to 1071.
  2. It does not apply to the tail tier at all, which is bounded by the NUMBER of
     trajectories, not by memory.
  3. The rotation that makes it work destroys the structure the other routes exploit. A
     randomly rotated basis mixes every gene into every coordinate, so r-balls, bounded
     width and controller history -- all of which depend on locality in the gene basis --
     cannot be applied to a rotated representation. Quantization composes with those
     methods only as a final storage layer, never as an alternative to them.
  4. Our observables are not rotation-invariant. 'Which genes are ON' is a statement in
     the gene basis; after rotation it is a dense linear functional, and the conjunctive
     events this engine exists for are defined in the original coordinates.
