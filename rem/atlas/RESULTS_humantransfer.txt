DOES THE YEAST PROMOTER RESULT TRANSFER TO HUMAN CELLS?
=================================================================================================
  ENCODE CRISPRi RNA-seq, K562. 48 transcription factors silenced, each with two
  biological replicates. HGNC map sha256[:32] 6f43d6ff43aa9fdfa5fb2f20a20a7cac.
  TRRUST provides the regulator sets; 2816 of its genes are quantified here.
  carried: 153 target genes that are EXPRESSED and have >= 2 of these factors as
  TRRUST regulators; 57 have >= 3, most-regulated has 7.
  400 (gene, regulator) pairs measured twice each.

=================================================================================================
H1  THE REPLICATE NOISE FLOOR, MEASURED BEFORE ANY MODEL
=================================================================================================
  RMS replicate-to-replicate difference in the centred response : 0.3112 log2
  NOISE FLOOR, the noise in ONE replicate's response            : 0.2200 log2
  The quantity every model below is scored against is a SINGLE replicate's response, so
  the floor is the single-replicate noise. Stated explicitly because the yeast module got
  exactly this wrong -- it scored against a two-replicate MEAN while dividing by the
  single-replicate figure -- and had to be corrected by an audit.
  spread of the response itself: 0.3513 log2   (signal-to-noise 1.60)

H2  THE BASELINE THAT MUST FAIL -- AND IT IS THE GATE THAT WAS WRONG
=================================================================================================
    model                                         held-out RMSE  floors vs identity
    no information at all (predict zero)                 0.3520                0.18
    the GENE only, blind to which regulator              0.3360                0.11
    the (gene, regulator) pair -- IDENTITY               0.3116                0.00

  H2 as predeclared FAILS: knowing the gene beats knowing nothing by only 0.07 floors.
  But H2 could not have passed WHATEVER the data said, and that is a defect in the gate
  rather than a verdict on the data. Each gene's response is CENTRED ACROSS THE PANEL
  before anything is fitted, which removes the gene's own level by construction -- so a
  gene-level predictor has almost nothing left to predict. A bar no evidence can clear is
  ledger P, and this one was built into the preprocessing.

  THE QUESTION H2 SHOULD HAVE ASKED -- is there reproducible signal here at all?
    correlation between the two replicates across all 400 pairs: r = 0.6284
  There is. The data are not dead; the baseline was.

=================================================================================================
H3  DOES IT MATTER WHICH REGULATOR YOU PERTURB?
=================================================================================================
  Held-out prediction comparison, as predeclared: regulator-blind 0.3360 against
  identity 0.3116, an advantage of 0.11 floors. Taken at face value that
  says the regulators ARE interchangeable and the yeast result does not transfer.

  IT IS THE WRONG INSTRUMENT, and the reason is arithmetic rather than biological. The
  blind model predicts from the MEAN of 2.61
  noisy measurements while the identity model predicts from ONE, so the blind model's
  predictor is quieter by roughly sqrt(K). With K this small that advantage in noise
  cancels most of identity's advantage in signal, and the comparison measures the two
  together. In the yeast experiment the groups were large and this did not bite.

  THE RIGHT INSTRUMENT IS A VARIANCE COMPONENT, which separates them: within a target
  gene, how much of the response variance is attributable to WHICH regulator, after the
  replicate noise the averaging leaves behind is subtracted? Bootstrapped over genes.

    between-regulator variance   0.05301      sd 0.2302 log2
    95% CI on that sd            [0.1987, 0.2590] log2
    IN FLOORS                    1.05   CI [0.90, 1.18]
    for scale, the between-GENE variance is 0.04200 -- regulators of one gene differ by
    MORE than genes differ from each other.

  H3: the regulators of a human gene are NOT interchangeable -- the between-regulator variance excludes zero.

=================================================================================================
H3b  THE SAME ESTIMATOR ON THE YEAST DATA, SO THE TWO ARE COMPARABLE
=================================================================================================
                 grouping unit               floor  sd of the effect              in floors
    HUMAN        target gene                0.2200            0.2302      1.05 [0.90, 1.18]
    YEAST        sequence context           0.0823            1.3049    15.86 [9.32, 21.36]

  ratio yeast/human: 5.67x in absolute log2, 15.15x in floors.

  THE VERDICT, IN TWO PARTS, BECAUSE THE ANSWER IS NOT ONE WORD.
  QUALITATIVELY IT TRANSFERS. A human gene's regulators are demonstrably not
  interchangeable; the effect excludes zero and is larger than the variation between
  genes. Every count-like summary in this build order assumes the opposite, and that
  assumption is refuted on human data as it was on yeast.
  QUANTITATIVELY IT DOES NOT. The effect is 15 times smaller relative to measurement noise than in
  yeast. Since the entire regulator-identity effect spans about ONE noise floor here, the
  yeast bar -- 'within one floor of identity' -- is met by almost any class resolution,
  including C = 1. The specific requirement of 64 classes out of 404, on which every cap
  in this build order rests, is NOT reproduced on human data and is not currently
  testable there: this panel cannot distinguish class resolutions at all.

H6  WHAT DIFFERS BETWEEN THE TWO EXPERIMENTS
=================================================================================================
  1. Yeast measured SITES designed into a promoter; this measures PERTURBATIONS of factors
     in their native network. A knockdown effect includes everything downstream of that
     factor, not only its direct action on the gene -- so this test is, if anything,
     biased TOWARD regulators looking interchangeable, since indirect effects are shared.
  2. Yeast varied the number of sites; here exactly one factor is perturbed at a time, so
     the count-versus-identity question becomes which-versus-whether, which is the half
     that matters for the engine's per-gene factor.
  3. TRRUST's regulator sets are literature-curated and incomplete, so a gene's true
     regulator set is larger than the one used here.
  4. One cell line, one assay, and CRISPRi silences transcription rather than removing
     protein, so factors acting post-translationally are under-represented.

=================================================================================================
H7  WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE
=================================================================================================
  It settles whether a HUMAN gene's response depends on WHICH of its regulators is hit,
  on this panel, which is the assumption every count-like summary in this build order
  makes. It does not settle the temporal half of the engine's summary, it does not
  validate any cap directly, and it cannot say anything about factors absent from the
  panel or edges absent from TRRUST.
