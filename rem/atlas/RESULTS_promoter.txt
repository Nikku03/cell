VALIDATING THE COUNT SUMMARY AGAINST REAL PROMOTER MEASUREMENTS
=================================================================================================
  Sharon et al. 2012, Nature Biotechnology 30:521-30, doi:10.1038/nbt.2205, found via
  PubMed. Data from NCBI GEO GSE37851 (GSM929011/GSM929012), sha256[:32] 6a68ee26db7a1d3f4813ae29ed4e69bd.
  6016 designed yeast promoters with both replicates and a parsed context.
  Design varies site COUNT and site IDENTITY independently, which is what makes it the
  right experiment: if expression is a function of how many sites there are, promoters
  with the same count but different factors should be interchangeable.

=================================================================================================
V1  THE REPLICATE NOISE FLOOR, MEASURED BEFORE ANY MODEL IS FITTED
=================================================================================================
  replicate Pearson r on log2 expression : 0.9974
  RMS replicate-to-replicate difference   : 0.1646 log2 units
  noise in a SINGLE replicate             : 0.1164 log2
  NOISE FLOOR, the noise in y = (l1+l2)/2 : 0.0823 log2
  The first version of this module used the per-replicate figure as the floor, which is
  sqrt(2) too large, because y is the MEAN of two replicates and not one of them. Every
  'floors' number in this module and in signed/poscount/multiplier was understated by
  1.414x. Checked empirically: std(l1 - y) = 0.0823.
  Correcting it makes every REFUTATION stronger and every VALIDATION margin tighter; no
  gate verdict in the arc changes, which was checked before the change was made.
  No model difference smaller than this is a result.

V5  THE AXIS A COUNT ALSO DISCARDS: POSITION AND ORIENTATION
=================================================================================================
  The same paper reports a ~10 bp periodic dependence of expression on site location. A
  count discards position too, so if position carries more than identity then
  count-versus-identity is not the operative question.
    identity only                    1.1641
    identity + orientation           1.1945   gain -0.37 floors
    identity + position (10 bp bins) 1.3774   gain -2.59 floors
    fallback rates: identity 11.1%, +orientation 13.9%, +position 25.7%
    Both refinements make it WORSE, and the fallback column says why: finer groups are
    sparser, so the held-out promoter more often has no training group and drops to the
    context mean. This does NOT show position is unimportant -- the paper reports a 10 bp
    periodicity -- it shows a group-mean estimator cannot exploit position at this sample
    size. The count-versus-identity comparison is unaffected: both sides are coarse.

=================================================================================================
V6  WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT VALIDATE
=================================================================================================
  1. These are SYNTHETIC yeast promoters in two sequence contexts, measured at steady
     state in one growth condition. They are the cleanest available separation of count
     from identity; they are not human promoters and not a time course.
  2. The engine's summary is over controller HISTORY -- how many controllers were active
     and for how long. This data has no time axis, so it tests the count-versus-identity
     half of that claim and says nothing about the temporal half.
  3. Site occupancy is designed, not measured. A designed site is not necessarily a bound
     factor, so this tests the promoter's response to SITES rather than to bound TFs.
  4. Whatever the verdict, it bounds only what a count can do on THIS observable --
     mean expression. summary.py's K7 showed that a symmetric observable can be blind to
     identity even when the mechanism is not.
