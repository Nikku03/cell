==================================================================================================
SELECTION ON LAG -- the curvature of the optimum Fridman et al. located
==================================================================================================
  Their Nature 2014 result gives the PEAK: evolved lag matches the exposure
  interval. This is the SHAPE around it, which predicts how tightly replicate
  populations should cluster -- testable against experiments they already ran.

   T_on (h)  peak lag  fold-width of the peak  max |selection|   curvature
        1.5      1.48        15.0x (>= grid)            0.067      -0.050
        2.5      2.32        15.0x (>= grid)            0.200      -0.174
        3.5      2.91        15.0x (>= grid)            0.841      -0.371
        5.0      4.57                   6.8x            2.800      -0.704

  READING IT. 'fold-width' is the range of lag over which survival stays within
  a factor of e of its best value. A narrow width means selection pins lag; a wide
  one means it drifts and the matching is a tendency, not a law.

  3 of 4 widths are GRID-LIMITED, not measured -- the e-fold
  region reaches both ends of the lag grid, so those rows are lower bounds and
  the true peaks are wider still. Only the longest exposure gives a measured
  width. That strengthens the direction of the trend and weakens its magnitude.
  THE TESTABLE PREDICTION: across replicate evolved populations at a given
  exposure duration, evolved lag should scatter by roughly this factor and no
  more. Their replicates already carry that number.

  THE ROBUST STATEMENT IS THE SELECTION STRENGTH, which is not grid-limited:
  it rises 42x from 0.067 at a 1.5 h exposure to 2.800 at 5.0 h.
  PREDICTION: evolved lag should scatter WIDELY across replicate populations at
  short exposures and cluster TIGHTLY at long ones. That is a trend across
  conditions rather than a single number, which is harder to match by chance, and
  their replicate populations already carry it.
