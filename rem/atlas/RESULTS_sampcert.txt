=================================================================================================
CAN THE CERTIFICATE BE VERIFIED WITHOUT SCANNING EVERY STATE?
=================================================================================================
  If verifying costs a full scan it costs what exact DP costs, and no speed-up of the
  approximation can rescue that. This prices the three ways out.

  10 controllers, n = 1024 states, L = 6, 200 targets. sha 9b909319ccc8.
  one real pruning level: 3,000 parents x 1024 children = 3,072,000 candidates.
  keeping the top 3,000 by bound, the FULL-SCAN certificate over the 3,069,000 dropped candidates is 1.306979e-76.

=================================================================================================
C0  THE CEILING GATE: HOW EVEN IS THE SUM BEING SAMPLED?
=================================================================================================

      heaviest fraction   share of the certificate
                 0.001%                      0.17%
                 0.010%                      1.73%
                 0.100%                     13.77%
                 1.000%                     54.10%
                10.000%                     93.37%

  C0: the heaviest 0.1% carries 13.77% of the sum -- the sum is even enough that uniform sampling has a chance.
  MY PREDECLARED EXPECTATION WAS WRONG, AND THE REASON IS WORTH KEEPING. I expected the
  certificate sum to inherit the tail's concentration -- pathbound measured the top 1%
  of PATHS carrying 91% of the TAIL. It does not. The tail is concentrated because a
  few paths have large ON-probability; the certificate is a sum over the DROPPED
  candidates, which are precisely the ones that do not, so it is dominated by the many
  rather than the few. Concentration in the observable does not transfer to the
  residual, and I assumed it would.

=================================================================================================
C3  THE AGGREGATE BOUND: EXAMINE k CHILDREN PER PARENT, BOUND THE REST WITHOUT TOUCHING
=================================================================================================
  For each parent, take its k heaviest children and bound every other child in ONE term:
  their total remaining mass times the largest ON-probability any child could have. That
  largest is itself a box bound over the child space -- max over c of g.v_c is bounded by
  sum_j max(g_j,0)*max_c v_c[j] -- which costs O(targets) per parent and touches NO child.

         k   children touched     certificate   / full scan

         2              6,000    2.419057e-74       185.088

=================================================================================================
C2b  THE CIRCULARITY IN C1 AND C2, AND THE VERSION THAT DOES NOT HAVE IT
=================================================================================================
  C1 and C2 sampled from the DROPPED set. Knowing which candidates are dropped means
  having ranked them all by the bound -- which is the full scan. Their saving is illusory
  as stated: they cheapen the verification of a partition whose construction already paid
  the cost. My setup's defect, not sampling's.

  THE NON-CIRCULAR VERSION: select by path MASS, which needs no bound evaluations -- the
  child ordering under any parent is that column of the transition matrix, so one sort of
  the columns gives every parent's heaviest children and a global top-k is a merge. Then
  certify that partition by importance sampling, evaluating the bound only where sampled.

  selecting by mass instead of by bound: the full-scan certificate for THAT partition is
  1.421285e-76, against 1.306979e-76 for the bound-selected one (1.09x).

     bound evals  vs full scan  estimate/true   95% UCB/true
              30       0.0010%          0.997          2.807
             306       0.0100%          1.044          1.424
           3,069       0.1000%          0.988          1.063
          30,690       1.0000%          0.994          1.013

  C2b: a valid 95% upper bound within 2x costs 306 bound evaluations against
  3,072,000 for the full scan -- a factor of 10,039. The scan IS avoidable,
  and the thing that avoids it is the cheap proxy plus sampling, NOT the aggregate
  bound this module was built expecting to work.

  AND THE GUARANTEE IS WEAKER IN KIND, WHICH MUST NOT BE GLOSSED. The full scan gives an
  EXACT sum. This gives a 95% confidence upper bound. That is a real demotion -- a
  certificate that holds one time in twenty is not the same object -- and whether it is
  acceptable is a decision about what the engine's guarantee is for, not a measurement.

=================================================================================================
C4  THE VERDICT, AND C5 WHAT IT DOES NOT SETTLE
=================================================================================================
  Read C3's last column against C1's and C2's. A certificate is a guarantee; the sampling
  gates produce confidence statements and only the aggregate bound produces a certificate
  at all, so if C3 stays close to the full scan at small k the scan is avoidable and if it
  does not, item 2 is unresolved and the approximation cannot beat exact DP.

  NOT SETTLED.
  1. One pruning level at one width. The aggregate term's looseness compounds across
     levels and that is not measured here.
  2. Sorting the transition matrix's columns once is O(n^2 log n), paid before any of this
     helps. At n = 1024 that is cheap; it is not free at every width.
  3. This asks only whether the SCAN can be avoided. exactcert already showed the bound
     being verified is 25 orders looser than the tail, and nothing here changes that.
