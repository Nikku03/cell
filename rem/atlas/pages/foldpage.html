How a chromosome folds

Two metres of DNA fold inside each cell by a few simple rules. We tested each rule on real measurements, then built a simulator from the rules alone and checked it against DNA it had never seen.

LoopsA motor pulls, stop signs hold.A ring-shaped motor (cohesin) reels DNA through itself. It stops at CTCF proteins whose sites point into the loop, so a loop sits between two signs facing each other.
ZonesLike sticks with like.Active, open DNA gathers with other open DNA, and closed with closed. This needs no motor.
ChangeActivity moves first.When a cell gets a signal, genes switch and the DNA opens. Loops and zones are redrawn afterwards.
EffectLoops work by contact.A loop brings a distant switch next to a gene. Genes follow the switches they are looped to.
