CASE A -- TB relapse in macaques (Maiello, Fortune, Flynn, Lin; doi:10.1128/iai.00177-25)
=================================================================================================
  THEIR GAP, IN THEIR WORDS: 'complete sterilization or very low Mtb burden is protective
  against SIV-induced TB relapse but cannot be predicted by PET CT'.

  A1 EXACTNESS  worst relative error over 200 random lesion sets: 1.515e-15

CASE C -- PerSort purity on a 1% subpopulation (Srinivas, Baliga;
          doi:10.1128/mSystems.01127-20)
=================================================================================================
  THEIR NUMBERS: dim cells ~1% of the population; sorting '93% efficient', i.e. ~7%
  inappropriately sorted. Both are correct as measured. The question is only whether the
  second transfers to a gate on the first -- ledger defect S, made concrete.

  C-CONTROL  at a balanced mixture (pi = 0.5), the formula returns purity 0.9300,
     which reproduces their stated 93%. The figure is right where it was measured.

  C1 PURITY OF THE DIM GATE as a function of prevalence and misclassification rate:
        eps \ pi        0.004        0.01         0.05          0.50
        0.001         0.8005       0.9098       0.9813       0.9990
        0.005         0.4442       0.6678       0.9128       0.9950
        0.010         0.2845       0.5000       0.8390       0.9900
        0.030         0.1149       0.2462       0.6299       0.9700
        0.070         0.0507       0.1183       0.4115       0.9300
     At pi = 0.01, eps = 0.07: closed form 0.118321, Monte-Carlo (1e7 cells) 0.118293 +- 0.000364
     agreement 0.08 sigma  PASS (bar 3 sigma)
     READING IT: a 7% error rate that is harmless on a balanced mixture leaves a 1% gate
     mostly full of the majority population. The direction cuts BOTH ways and the second
     is the interesting one -- see C2.

  C2 ATTENUATION -- what an impure gate does to a measured difference:
     observed difference = true difference x 0.117561
     Monte-Carlo 0.117069 +- 0.000364, agreement 1.35 sigma  PASS (bar 3 sigma)
     CONSEQUENCE, AND IT IS IN THEIR FAVOUR: at pi=0.01, eps=0.07 any real dim-vs-lit
     difference they measured is DILUTED by 8.5x. Their -4.17 log2 fold change
     in 16S rRNA would then be a floor, not the effect size. The correction makes their
     result larger, not smaller -- provided eps for the dim gate is what the control says.

  C3 A CEILING THAT NEEDS NO MODEL AT ALL.
     dim cells ~1% of the population; ~5% of cells survive 5x MIC INH/RIF.
     Even if EVERY dim cell survives, dim cells are at most 20.0% of the survivors.
     For dim cells to account for a given share of survivors, their survival advantage
     over lit cells would have to be:
        share   50%  requires dim:lit survival ratio       99.0x
        share   90%  requires dim:lit survival ratio      891.0x
        share   99%  requires dim:lit survival ratio     9801.0x
     THE OFFER: this is an arithmetic identity, not a model, and it says the translationally
     dormant subpopulation cannot by itself be the tolerant subpopulation. Either a large
     survival ratio is real and measurable, or a second route to tolerance exists outside
     the dim gate. Both are testable with sorts they already run.

CASE D -- lag-time optimisation (Fridman, Balaban; doi:10.1038/nature13469)
=================================================================================================
  Already computed; see RESULTS_selection.txt. Reproduced their lag-matching optimum at
  4 of 4 exposure durations from first principles, then produced the curvature around it:
  selection strength rises 42x from a 1.5 h to a 5.0 h exposure. The prediction is that
  evolved lag should scatter widely across replicate populations at short exposures and
  cluster tightly at long ones -- a trend across conditions, in replicates they already ran.
  Three of four peak widths were grid-limited; those rows are lower bounds, not measurements.

WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED
=================================================================================================
  * No group's raw data was in hand. Every number above is a published one, quoted in
    CITATIONS with its source sentence, or a swept variable.
  * Case A's headline finding is a NEGATIVE: under the independent-bacillus model the
    spread of residual burdens does not move the relapse probability at all. What does move
    it is the covariance between burden and drug escape, which nobody has measured.
  * Case B does not fit their data; it shows what their data cannot fit.
  * Case C's purity number is only as good as the misclassification rate for the dim gate,
    and the prevalence at which their 7% was measured is UNRETRIEVED. The table is given
    across eps for that reason.
