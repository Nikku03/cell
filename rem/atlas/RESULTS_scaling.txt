S8 IS WHAT MAKES THE REST READABLE.  With the answer gradient replaced by a dense random vector,
K grows as N^1.062 with R2 0.9997 and K/N rises from 0.58 to 0.77.  The method can produce a
linear K(N).  So the sublinear curves below are falsifiable statements, not an artefact of a
criterion that is easy to satisfy.

WHAT K(N) DOES.  Median over five independent circuits per size:

    N        growth only    + composition    + composition + death flux
    12            8               4                    5
    72           22              16                   10
    272          37              21                    7
    1056         38              30                    4

With only a growth rate, K rises logarithmically (R2 0.881, power-law exponent 0.372).  With
growth plus composition it is cleanly logarithmic (R2 0.992, exponent 0.403).  With the full
observable set K does not grow at all over an 88-fold range in N -- the log fit has R2 0.002 and
the power-law exponent is 0.004.  K/N falls from 0.42 to 0.0038.

S5, the matched control, is emphatic: at N = 1056 the projection needs a median of 4 measurements
where random selection needs 763, a factor of 190.

=================================================================================================
S7's PREDECLARED READING IS REFUTED, AND THE REASON MATTERS
=================================================================================================
S7 predeclared that if the number of components carrying 99% of the unconstrained gradient grows
with N, then K must grow too.  It does not.  That count rises 9, 15, 26, 43, 67, 124, 187, 286
across the sweep while K stays flat at 4 to 12.  The predeclared coupling was wrong, and the
reason is that the two quantities measure different things: the 99% count measures how SPREAD the
residual is, while K measures how far its MAGNITUDE sits above a fixed absolute target.  A
residual can be spread over hundreds of components and still be small enough to need almost no
measurements.

=================================================================================================
THE DIAGNOSTIC THAT WAS MISSING, AND IT QUALIFIES THE HEADLINE
=================================================================================================
Measured after the run, because without it K(N) would be over-claimed:

    N      ||g||   ||g_null|| before any measurement   ratio to target   K
    12     20.07              3.231                        12.94         5
    72      8.36              2.847                        11.41        10
    272     7.63              1.725                         6.91         7
    1056    5.69              1.086                         4.35         4

    ||g|| ~ N^-0.304        ||g_null|| ~ N^-0.268

So larger circuits are INTRINSICALLY EASIER in this family: the residual starts 12.9 times above
the criterion at N = 12 and only 4.4 times above it at N = 1056.  Part of the flat K is that the
starting point is closer to the target, not that the selection rule gets better.  That dilution is
real -- in a larger network with the same tail depth no single rate dominates -- but it is a
property of the network family and it must be stated beside the curve.

THE CAVEAT THIS EXPOSES, WHICH IS NOT YET TESTED.  Switching rates here are drawn independently
for all m(m-1) ordered pairs, so every type couples to every other and the network becomes DENSER
as it grows.  Real biological networks are sparse.  In a dense network individual rates dilute by
construction, which is very likely where N^-0.30 comes from.  A sparse family, where each type
couples to a fixed number of others, has rates growing like m rather than m^2 and does not dilute
the same way.  Until that is measured, the honest claim is:

    K(N) is flat to logarithmic for a DENSELY COUPLED network family over 12 to 1056 rates,
    with the caveat that such networks dilute per-rate sensitivity as they grow.

Whether it survives a sparse topology is the next measurement, not an assumption.
