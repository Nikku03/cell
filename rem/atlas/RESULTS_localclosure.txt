LOCAL CLOSURE: CAN AN r-BALL FACTORISATION REPLACE THE JUNCTION TREE?
=================================================================================================
  statedim left treewidth > 40 by N = 512 on every topology but a cascade. Lifting a tree
  decomposition from G to G^r cannot help -- S8(b) showed treewidth is already past 40 at
  r = 1. So this route gives up exact marginalisation instead: factorise on r-balls, cost
  linear in N with no treewidth term, and pay for it in accuracy.
  tail-legal threshold from tail_err = 20.23 sqrt(MI):  MI < 2.4435e-07

L7  IS THE DEPENDENCE GRAPH REALLY G^r ON A HUB?  (added after L3, labelled)
=================================================================================================
  L3's whole verdict rests on this. The 3-ball around SP1 is 2,724 of 2,861 genes only if
  two of SP1's 484 targets are genuinely dependent above tau. statedim's S5 checked a
  cascade and a random graph and never checked a hub.

  (a) does a hub's OUT-degree dilute the dependence between its targets?
     out-degree  MI(hub,target)  MI(target,target)  frac > tau
              4      3.4954e-03         1.3473e-04        1.00
              8      3.8718e-03         1.5461e-04        1.00
             14      3.4682e-03         1.3550e-04        1.00
    NO. Sibling dependence is flat in out-degree -- a hub with 14 targets correlates them
    exactly as strongly as one with 4. Spreading influence over more targets does not
    weaken it, because the hub's own state is shared by all of them equally.

  (b) does the TARGETS' in-degree dilute it? (regulators at low index, targets at high)
     in-degree  MI(hub,target)  MI(target,target)   dilution  frac > tau
             1      3.4925e-03         1.5004e-04       1.0x        1.00
             2      8.3942e-04         3.9415e-05       3.8x        1.00
             4      1.8660e-04         6.9629e-06      21.5x        1.00
             6      7.8112e-05         2.6434e-06      56.8x        1.00
             8      3.8731e-05         1.1689e-06     128.4x        1.00
            10      2.1906e-05         6.8337e-07     219.6x        1.00
            12      1.5027e-05         4.8879e-07     307.0x        1.00
    YES, but nowhere near enough. Dependence falls roughly as the SQUARE of in-degree --
    307x from in-degree 1 to 12 -- and is STILL above tau at in-degree 12.
    TRRUST's mean in-degree is 8403/2861 = 2.9, where sibling dependence sits at roughly
    82x above tau. Real regulation is nowhere near the dilution this would need.
  L7: the graph-power assumption SURVIVES on a hub. L3's ball sizes are not overestimates,
  and its verdict stands.
