P5's PRINTED VERDICT IS WRONG, AND THE GATE'S OWN TEXT SAYS SO.  The code compared ||g|| between
families and concluded "both families dilute comparably".  ||g|| is not the quantity that governs
K -- ||g_null||, the residual the measurements must actually reduce, is.  On that column:

    sparse   ||g_null|| ~ N^+0.032        ratio to target 14.1 to 17.2, FLAT across 20 to 800
    dense    ||g_null|| ~ N^-0.268        ratio to target 12.9 falling to 4.35

So the sparse family shows NO dilution and the dense family shows exactly the N^-0.27 the
predeclared reading named.  The gate's stated condition was met; its implementation checked the
wrong column.  The correct verdict is the one predeclared: the dense result was substantially a
dilution artefact, and the SPARSE curve is the one to trust.

THE RESULT, ON THE FAMILY THAT DOES NOT DILUTE:

       N     20    30    50    80   125   200   320   500   800
    K med     5     9    13    15    15    19    21    18    28
    K/N    0.25  0.30  0.26  0.19  0.12  0.10  0.07  0.04  0.035

    K ~ N^0.365   (R2 0.834)        K ~ 5.00 ln N - 5.41   (R2 0.888)        linear R2 -0.747

K grows -- unlike the dense family, where it did not -- but sublinearly, and the linear fit is
worse than useless.  This is the honest scaling law, because it is measured where the starting
difficulty is constant.

WHY IT CAN BE BELIEVED.  P7 passes: replacing the answer gradient with a dense random vector on
this same family gives alpha = 0.947 at R2 0.9990, with K/N holding near 0.48 against the 1 - M/N
= 0.60 a fully unconstrained target would need.  The machinery produces a linear K(N) when the
target is genuinely dense, so alpha = 0.365 is a falsifiable measurement.  P8: at N = 800 the
projection needs 28 measurements where random selection needs 506, a factor of 18.

P9, THE CONNECTIVITY DEPENDENCE, IS ITSELF A RESULT AND IS NOT WHAT I WOULD HAVE GUESSED:

    c = 2   M/N 0.500   alpha 0.298
    c = 3   M/N 0.400   alpha 0.355
    c = 5   M/N 0.286   alpha 0.507

alpha RISES with connectivity.  Denser coupling makes the specification problem harder, not
easier.  That looks like it contradicts the dense family's alpha = 0.004, and the contradiction is
the point: the dense family is not an extension of this trend, it is the case where dilution
flatters the curve.  Read together, the two say that the flat dense K was an artefact and the real
dependence is a sublinear exponent that grows with how densely the network is wired.  Whether
alpha reaches 1 at very high connectivity cannot be read off the dense family for exactly that
reason, and is NOT measured here.

WHAT THIS EXTRAPOLATES TO, WITH THE EXTRAPOLATION LABELLED AS SUCH.  The data span N = 20 to 800.
A whole cell at 6e5 rates is 2.9 orders beyond the largest circuit measured, and nothing here
justifies that reach -- these are what the fitted laws say, not predictions:

    c = 2, alpha 0.298   ->  K ~ 115        c = 3, alpha 0.365  ->  K ~ 314
    c = 5, alpha 0.507   ->  K ~ 1258       log fit, c = 3      ->  K ~ 61

At an intermediate N = 1e4, one order beyond the data rather than three, the same laws give 34 to
158.  The consistent statement across every fit is that the requirement is HUNDREDS to a few
THOUSAND targeted measurements, not hundreds of thousands.  The measurement wall is not where the
rate count is.

WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED.  This is a linear multi-type branching process with a single scalar
target.  Real networks have interactions, feedback, and many simultaneous questions asked of one
parameter set; K for a set of targets is bounded by the sum of individual K's and could be much
smaller, but that is unmeasured.  P10 also shows the instance spread is wide -- at N = 320 the
five circuits needed 4 to 52 -- so K is a distribution, not a number, and the median is what is
fitted above.
