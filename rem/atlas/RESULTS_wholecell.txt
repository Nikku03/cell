W8  WHAT IS ABSENT
=================================================================================================
  Modelled: metabolism (10,600 reactions), the metabolic proteome, ribosome allocation,
  and the translation cost of the whole proteome including non-metabolic genes.
  NOT modelled, at all:
    - DNA replication and the cell cycle
    - signal transduction and regulation -- the model has no controller
    - splicing, RNA processing, export
    - protein folding, chaperones, quality control
    - secretion, trafficking, the endomembrane system
    - the cytoskeleton and mechanics
    - organelle biogenesis; mitochondria appear only as compartment labels
    - membrane potential and electrochemical gradients beyond stoichiometry
    - every spatial degree of freedom -- the cell is one well-mixed bag
    - cell-to-cell variation; this is one deterministic allocation
  1884 of 19900 genes (9.5%) have a
  mechanism in this model. The other 90.5% appear only as
  a mass and a ribosome burden. That is the honest measure of how much of a cell this is.

=================================================================================================
POST-RUN READING, SECOND RUN (corrected units).  Hand-written.
=================================================================================================

MY PREDICTION WAS FALSIFIED, AND NOT FOR THE REASON I EXPECTED.  The previous reading predicted
the rerun would land near 0.03/h.  It gives 0.3538/h, a factor of twelve out.  The units were not
still wrong -- the prediction was.  I predicted what a human cell DOES, a 24-hour doubling, while
the model computes MAXIMAL growth under its constraints.  A growth-maximising metabolic network
with no regulation grows as fast as its constraints allow, and W8 already recorded that this model
has no controller.  I then compared its answer to a regulated cell anyway.  The right reading of
0.3538/h is "the fastest this constraint set permits", which is not a prediction about human cells
and must never be quoted as one.

THE UNIT FIX ITSELF WORKED, and the sweep proves it where it previously proved the opposite.
Dividing every catalytic rate in human metabolism by ten now moves growth by 17% (ratio 0.8345)
where before it moved it by 0.6%.  Enzymes are no longer free.  Ribosome speed doubled gives
exactly 1.9902 -- growth proportional to translation capacity, as the binding constraint requires.

A SECOND MODELLING ERROR REMAINS, AND IT IS VISIBLE IN ONE NUMBER: the ribosome is 53.8% of total
protein, against a few per cent in real cells.  The cause is that the full ribosome mass is charged
against a PROTEIN budget.  A mammalian 80S ribosome is about 4.3 MDa of which only roughly a third
is protein; the rest is rRNA, which is not made by ribosomes and does not compete for the protein
budget.  Ribosomes are therefore about three times cheaper than this model makes them, which
inflates their share and depresses growth.  Fixing it is the next rerun.

W6's kcat ROWS ARE AT THE NOISE FLOOR AND MUST NOT BE READ AS SENSITIVITIES.  All eight report
-0.000542, identical to six decimals and NEGATIVE -- a faster enzyme cannot reduce growth.  The
bisection resolves mu to 1e-6 absolute, which on mu = 0.354 is 2.8e-6 relative, and a 1% kcat
perturbation moves growth by about 5e-6 relative.  The signal is barely twice the tolerance, so
these are bisection noise wearing a sensitivity's clothes.  The next run tightens the tolerance
for the probes and reports the floor explicitly.

WHAT IS ABOVE THE NOISE, AND IT IS THE POINT:

    k_elong, ribosome speed                    +1.0108
    proteome budget                            +0.0211
    k_dp of the non-metabolic proteome         -0.0200
    any individual metabolic kcat              below the resolution of the method

Growth tracks ribosome speed with an exponent of one.  The next two parameters are fifty times
smaller, and every one of 5,938 individual enzyme turnover numbers is smaller still.  The
whole-cell answer is set by ALLOCATION, not by any single enzyme.

The negative sign on the third is worth stating plainly: making non-metabolic proteins less stable
REDUCES growth, because they must then be resynthesised continuously and that consumes the same
ribosome capacity growth needs.  That is protein degradation entering a whole-cell answer through
the translation constraint -- and expression.py proved degradation rates are exactly what RNA-seq,
proteomics and ribosome profiling together cannot see.  The blind direction and the whole-cell
bottleneck are the same quantity, reached from two independent directions.

W7's uniform verdict is now worth more than it was.  The limiting layer stays "translation
capacity" across all eight variants, but the model is demonstrably responsive this time rather
than stuck in a corner, so this is evidence rather than an artefact.
