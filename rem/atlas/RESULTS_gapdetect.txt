  separation 1.1x (bar > 5x)
  GD2 FAIL -- gate on the SEPARATION; which step gates is measured, not assumed

  DIAGNOSIS, and it is a structural statement rather than a parameter complaint. Both variants
  move the tail by a comparable amount (1.43 and 1.61 orders) no matter how the rates are set,
  and making mRNA turnover 12x faster than protein decay did not separate them -- it raised both.
  The reason: in a LINEAR two-stage circuit mRNA -> protein, EVERY step lies on the causal path
  to the observable, so every waiting-time shape reaches the tail. There is no non-gating step to
  find. Section 7.2's 0.11-order figure requires a step genuinely OFF that path, and whether such
  a step exists is a property of the CIRCUIT's topology, not of the step. So the actionable form
  of the rule is not "model the gating step's shape and use exponentials elsewhere" -- it is
  "find out whether your circuit has any off-path steps at all, because in a linear cascade it
  has none, and then every shape assumption is load-bearing.

  CLAIM (a) IS WITHDRAWN, and the reason is a SECOND BLIND SPOT worth more than the claim was.
  A spread statistic measures DRIFT, not ACCURACY. A fitter that is wrong by the same factor at
  every condition -- one iteration of bisection returns the bracket's geometric midpoint whatever
  the data -- produces fitted values that are uniformly wrong and therefore perfectly consistent,
  so the spread is exactly 0. The detector cannot see it, and no threshold on spread ever will.
  So section 7.3's detector has two blind spots, not one: a missing route below the detection
  limit, AND any error that is condition-INDEPENDENT. The second is not mentioned in the spec and
  is arguably the more dangerous, because a systematically miscalibrated fitter looks exactly
  like a structurally perfect model.
