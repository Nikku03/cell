=================================================================================================
THE TWO OPEN PROBLEMS, MEASURED
  1. does a real controller make the important parameters explode?
  2. can grouping keep the joint state small at realistic scale?
Sources: rem/atlas/RESULTS_controller.txt, rem/atlas/RESULTS_statedim.txt
=================================================================================================

THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE. They come apart. A controller is CHEAP IN PARAMETERS and EXPENSIVE IN
STATE: closing the loop leaves the set of rates worth measuring at about five, no matter how big
the system gets, but a single global regulator takes the joint state from 2^4 per variable to 2^N.
Regulation does not increase what must be MEASURED. It increases what must be SIMULATED JOINTLY.

=================================================================================================
PROBLEM 1  --  NO EXPLOSION, AND THE IMPORTANT SET MOVES ONTO THE CONTROLLER
=================================================================================================

    sweep                             P range     N90 range          exponent b
    A  add controllers, fixed plant    45 -> 141   4.90 -> 8.55       +0.4688 +- 0.0503
    B  grow the whole regulated system 37 -> 133   6.75 -> 4.27       -0.2518 +- 0.1251
    predeclared bands: b > 0.7 EXPLOSION, b < 0.3 SPARSE

Sweep B is the one that answers the scaling question, and it is NEGATIVE: N90 does not grow with
the system, it shrinks slightly. Seven-fold more parameters, the same five that matter. Sweep A is
intermediate at +0.47, but even there N90/P falls from 0.109 to 0.061 -- adding regulators adds
important parameters sublinearly, never in proportion.

TRANSFER IS REAL AND IT IS ABRUPT.

    K       controller share of ||C||^2     rank correlation with the open-loop plant ranking
    0                            0.0000                                              1.0000
    1                            0.0926                                              0.4478
    8                            0.1466                                              0.4458
   32                            0.6432                                              0.4536

ONE controller edge is enough to halve the rank correlation. The plant ranking computed open-loop
is not merely incomplete under feedback, it is reordered, and it reorders as soon as the loop
closes at all. At K = 32, seven of the ten largest control coefficients are controller parameters.
So the answer is not "sparse", it is TRANSFER: the same small number of things matter, and they
are different things.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY, per gate C6 as predeclared. Shuffling the regulatory wiring at random --
random target, random sensor, random sign -- gives the same scaling (b = -0.32 +- 0.32 against
-0.19 +- 0.21). The sparsity is a property of closed-loop systems of this class, NOT of the
canonical end-product architecture. C3 therefore says nothing about which biological wiring is
special. It does say the sparsity is robust to getting the wiring wrong, which is the more useful
half for a measurement programme.

GATES:  C1 PASS (adjoint to 1e-9 scaled, both FD directions)   C2 PASS (summation theorem exactly
1.0000000000 in five configurations and correctly BROKEN to 0.8726 for a proteome-sensing
controller)   C5 PASS (||C|| does not collapse, so this is not homeostasis)   C6 NOT SEPARATED
C7 FAIL -- the acceptance bars were six orders too loose and caught only 1 of 3 planted runaways.
Contained: C3 runs canonical wiring only, so no rejected configuration enters the measurement.
The bars were deliberately not retuned.

=================================================================================================
PROBLEM 2  --  THE STATE DOES NOT STAY SMALL, AND THE REASON IS TOPOLOGY, NOT DECAY
=================================================================================================

Dependence decays extremely fast: xi = 0.2486 +- 0.0005, R^2 = 0.999976, a factor of 55 per step.
That is not enough, and the reason is worth stating precisely.

STEP 1 -- THE THRESHOLD IS NOT FREE. From the measured law tail_err = 20.23 sqrt(MI), a 1% tail
tolerance forces MI < 2.44e-07. A bulk threshold of 1e-3, which keeps means fine, would admit
tail errors of 0.64.

STEP 2 -- THE RULE AS WRITTEN RETURNS THE WHOLE NETWORK. MI at distance 1 is 6.3e-03, four orders
above the tail-legal threshold, so every directly regulated pair is joined; and a regulatory
network is connected. Thresholding therefore gives ONE component of N for any connected topology
at any tail tolerance, and costs 2^N. This is not a decay problem. Decay is fast.

STEP 3 -- THE RIGHT COST IS TREEWIDTH. A path is one component but has treewidth 1.

    topology              N = 20,000    treewidth    state cost
    cascade                                      3     3.20e+05      tractable
    random degree 2                            >40     2.69e+14      not
    random degree 3                       too dense to compute       not
    scale-free m = 2                      too dense to compute       not

Only a pure cascade survives. Anything with mean degree 2 or more is past treewidth 40 by N = 512.
Robustness (S8b): at r = 1, the BARE regulatory graph and the most optimistic dependence graph
there could be, the answer is unchanged -- random degree 2 and scale-free are already past 40 at
N = 512. The obstruction is the topology of gene regulation, not the range of the dependence.

STEP 4 -- A GLOBAL CONTROLLER MAKES IT WORSE, AND CONDITIONING ONLY SOMETIMES SAVES IT. One hub
makes the dependence graph COMPLETE: treewidth N. Putting the controller into every bag drops it
back to 4 -- but only when the controller's timescale is far from its targets':

    tau_hub / tau_gene      r_cond       cost per gene
              2000             3              2^5        affordable
               500             4              2^6        affordable
               333            12            2^14         marginal
        200 down to 0.01      >=15           2^N         no grouping helps
              0.0025           5              2^7        affordable
              0.0014           3              2^5        affordable

THE WINDOW: the controller must be at least ~500x SLOWER than what it controls, so that its state
IS its history, or at least ~400x FASTER, where it correlates nothing to begin with. Within a
few hundred either way, conditioning fails and the cost is 2^N. Kinase signalling at seconds
against transcription at tens of minutes is a ratio of roughly 100 to 1000 -- ON that boundary,
not comfortably inside it.

THE MECHANISM, and it is the reason a prediction was lost. The original S4 predicted conditioning
on a controller would restore decay. It does not: the correlation is carried by the controller's
TRAJECTORY, not by its current value, so conditioning on the instantaneous state leaves it. A slow
controller is quasi-static and its state is its history; a fast one is white noise and correlates
nothing. In between, its history is genuinely extra state.

GATES:  S1 PASS (residual < 1e-14; MI exactly 0 on independent variables)   S2 PASS   S5 PASS (the
dependence graph IS path^3, which is what licenses extrapolating past N = 16)   S6 bracketed
S8 PASS   S3 FAIL -- xi scattered 0.0045 against a 0.0043 bar. Non-monotone in N and surviving a
common fit range, so wobble rather than drift; and the quantity used downstream is the INTEGER r,
which a 1.7% wobble cannot move. The gate failed; the conclusion does not rest on it.

=================================================================================================
WHAT THIS CHANGES
=================================================================================================

FOR MEASUREMENT. Problem 1's answer is good news and it redirects the programme. The rates worth
measuring under feedback are the REGULATORY ones -- gains, thresholds, cooperativities -- not the
catalytic ones, and an open-loop ranking of catalytic rates is reordered by the first controller
edge you add. This is consistent with what the corrected Recon3D run already said from the other
direction: with a sealed medium, zero enzyme capacities have a realised sensitivity.

FOR THE ENGINE. Problem 2's answer is bad news and it is structural. Section 14's split criterion,
applied at a tail-legal threshold, returns the whole network on any connected topology. The repair
is a tree decomposition rather than a threshold, and then the engine is tractable exactly on
low-treewidth topologies -- which real gene regulation is not. Three routes remain and none is
tested here: exploit that the tail-legal graph is a bounded graph POWER (structure a generic
treewidth bound ignores), accept bounded error from a fixed-width decomposition and measure what
it costs in tail accuracy, or carry controller history explicitly so conditioning works at matched
timescales.

FOR HONESTY. Two gates failed in this pair of modules and both are recorded rather than retuned:
controller C7 (bars six orders too loose, contained) and statedim S3 (a real miss that does not
propagate). One prediction of mine was made and lost before commit, and is kept as made.

=================================================================================================
ROUTE 1 OF 3 TESTED: THE BOUNDED GRAPH POWER.  IT FAILS, AND THE FAILURE IS SPECIFIC
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_localclosure.txt
=================================================================================================

WHAT WAS TRIED. Not lifting a tree decomposition from G to G^r -- S8(b) already killed that, since
treewidth is past 40 at r = 1. Instead, giving up exact marginalisation: if every dependence above
tau is confined to distance r, factorise on r-balls,

    P_hat(x) = prod_i P( x_i | x_{B_r(i) and earlier} )

a Bayesian network whose cost is sum_i 2^(1+|pa_i|) -- linear in N, no treewidth term anywhere.

THE APPROXIMATION WORKS. On a cascade, tested at three tail depths down to P = 1.3e-13:

    r   max|pa|   tail rel err at P=1.3e-13    max error in any mean
    1         1                    1.063e+00                5.0e-16
    2         2                    4.604e-02                4.4e-16
    3         3                    5.509e-03                6.1e-16
    4         4                    1.190e-03                5.6e-16

This is the sharpest instance of the bulk/tail split in this build order, and the cleanest,
because here the bulk error is EXACTLY ZERO by construction -- the factorisation reproduces every
marginal in the ball -- while the conjunctive event is wrong by 106% at r = 1. Six modules have
now measured a bulk quantity right while the tail was wrong; this one measures it at machine
precision against a 100% tail error in the same row.

THE COST DOES NOT WORK, ON REAL TOPOLOGY. TRRUST v2 human, 2,861 genes, 8,403 edges:

    r    median ball    90th    99th     max      gene at the max
    1              3      12      62     480      SP1
    2            118     615    1177    1813      CDKN1A
    3           1381    2169    2532    2724      SP1

The 3-ball around SP1 is 2,724 of 2,861 genes -- 95% of the network in one factor. Cost is set by
the WORST ball, so the median of 1,381 is not a reprieve, and quoting it would be ledger S. The
closure costs 2^2724 against a junction tree's 2^40+. The route does not merely fail to help; on
real topology it is worse than what it replaces.

HUB CONDITIONING DOES NOT RESCUE IT. Deleting the h highest-degree genes and conditioning:

    h        max|B_2|   treewidth    total cost = 2^h x N x 2^max|B_2|
    0            1813         >40                            4.6e+63
   20             682         >40                            4.8e+69
  200              44          23                            7.5e+76
  400              14           3                            6.5e+67

The ball sizes fall exactly as hoped -- 400 deletions take treewidth to 3 -- but 2^400 eats the
saving many times over. There is no h that wins.

THE ASSUMPTION UNDERNEATH WAS CHECKED, AND IT HELD. statedim's S5 verified "dependence graph =
G^r" on a cascade and a random graph, never on a hub, and the whole cost verdict depends on two of
SP1's 484 targets really being dependent above tau. Measured exactly (L7):

    out-degree 4, 8, 14  ->  sibling MI 1.35e-4, 1.55e-4, 1.36e-4     FLAT
    in-degree 1 .. 12    ->  sibling MI 1.50e-4 down to 4.89e-7       307x, and still above tau

Out-degree does not dilute AT ALL: a hub with fourteen targets correlates them exactly as strongly
as one with four, because its state is shared by all of them equally. In-degree does dilute, as
roughly its square, but is still above tau at in-degree 12 -- and TRRUST's mean in-degree is 2.9,
where sibling dependence sits ~80x above tau. So the ball sizes are not overestimates.

WHAT THIS LEAVES. The failure is now specific rather than general, which is worth more than the
route would have been:

  - the r-ball FACTORISATION is sound and cheap in accuracy: r = 3 buys 0.55% error on a 1e-13
    event with three-variable factors
  - the r-ball GEOMETRY is what fails, and only because of hubs
  - hubs cannot be conditioned away arithmetically (2^h), cannot be diluted away by out-degree,
    and are not diluted enough by biological in-degree

So the obstruction has been localised to one thing: a small number of very high out-degree
regulators, whose targets are mutually dependent no matter how many of them there are. Any route
that survives has to treat those specific vertices differently -- not by conditioning on their
STATE, which costs 2^h and which statedim's S6 says is only legitimate at a 500x timescale
separation anyway. The two remaining routes from statedim are untouched: bounded-width
decomposition with measured error, and carrying controller history explicitly.

ONE BOUND DID NOT SURVIVE. tail_err <= 20.23 sqrt(MI) fails in 5 of 16 rows where the MI is above
its own numerical floor, so it cannot be used to CHOOSE r. r has to be chosen from a measured
error curve. (L2 as first written compared against the law at every r including rows where the MI
was below its floor -- a guard statedim's S2 had and this gate did not; both floors are now
measured, the tail statistic's by solving the stationary equation twice from independent starts
and differencing, which gives 3.8e-16.)

=================================================================================================
ROUTE 2 OF 3 TESTED: BOUNDED WIDTH WITH MEASURED ERROR.  IT FAILS, AND IT FAILS IRREDUCIBLY
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_boundedwidth.txt
=================================================================================================

WHY THIS WAS BETTER POSED THAN ROUTE 1. Route 1 fixed the geometry and the width came out at
2,724. Here the width is fixed FIRST at whatever is affordable and the decomposition is forced
into it by deleting edges, so cost is N 2^(w+1) by construction and cannot blow up. The whole
question becomes the error, which is what should have been measured all along.

THE ANSWER DEPENDS ENTIRELY ON TOPOLOGY, AND THE DEPENDENCE IS EXTREME.

    deep tail (P ~ 5e-14), tail relative error at width w
    w        cascade      random degree 2      hub
    1        1.06e+00     9.67e-01             9.83e-01
    3        5.51e-03     3.06e-01             9.19e-01
    5        1.01e-05     3.22e-02             7.46e-01
    8        6.56e-06     1.07e-01             2.93e-01

On a cascade, width 3 is enough. On a hub graph, width 8 still leaves 29% error. And the random
graph is NON-MONOTONE -- w = 6 is worse than w = 5 -- so accuracy cannot simply be bought with
budget.

THE MECHANISM, AND IT IS THE SAME ONE ROUTE 1 FOUND. A hub of degree k is a (k+1)-CLIQUE in the
dependence graph. No width below k can hold it, so the last edges that any affordable width is
forced to drop are hub edges -- the strongest ones in the graph. Extending the reference curve
down to a drop fraction of 0.025 shows the consequence directly: the hub error PLATEAUS at 6-9%
instead of going to zero. Buying width stops buying accuracy.

THE ONE NUMBER HERE WITH NO MODEL IN IT. On TRRUST v2 human, what fraction of the 8,403 direct
regulatory edges is still represented at width w -- both endpoints together in some bag:

    w      cost N.2^(w+1)     edges represented     lost
    5           1.83e+05          6044  (72.8%)    27.2%
   10           5.86e+06          7167  (86.3%)    13.7%
   15           1.88e+08          7649  (92.2%)     7.8%
   20           6.00e+09          7890  (95.1%)     4.9%
   25           1.92e+11          8049  (97.0%)     3.0%

This contains no dynamics and no mutual information. It is a property of the graph and the budget.

THE VERDICT. Predeclared: affordable = 2^w <= 1e9, accurate = tail error < 1%.

    w     TRRUST lost     err, cascade end     err, hub end     affordable   accurate
    5           0.272             1.68e-05         5.97e-01        yes         no
   10           0.137             8.71e-06         2.52e-01        yes         no
   15           0.078             1.57e-06         6.54e-02        yes         no
   20           0.049             7.86e-07         7.19e-02        yes         no
   25           0.030             2.60e-06         8.49e-02        yes         no

EMPTY INTERSECTION. All five affordable widths are inside the measured domain and all five fail
at the hub end. Note the spread between the two ends -- four to five orders at the same drop
fraction -- which is why B5 reports a range rather than a number, and why the hub end governs:
TRRUST is hub-rich.

THE MATCHED CONTROL IS THE MOST USEFUL RESULT IN THE MODULE. Dropping weakest-MI-first against
dropping at random, at identical width:

    cascade              694x better
    random degree 2      6.1x better
    hub                  1.0x -- NO BETTER THAN RANDOM

Choosing which edges to keep is worth five orders of magnitude on a chain and worth exactly
nothing on a hub. On the topology that matters there is no strategy, only a budget.

FOUR DEFECTS OF MINE IN THIS MODULE, all recorded rather than patched away:
  - the dependence graph was thresholded at tau before thinning, which left a 7.4e-5 residual in
    the identity case that had nothing to do with width. Caught before commit; the graph is now
    complete and MI-weighted so width is the only thing that drops an edge.
  - B4 counted every deleted edge including FILL-IN, and reported dropping 16,522 of 8,403 edges,
    a fraction of 1.97. Caught by its own output being impossible.
  - B5 fed those impossible fractions into an interpolation and numpy CLAMPED instead of
    refusing, returning one constant that looked like a result.
  - B6 then printed EMPTY INTERSECTION on the strength of a single in-domain row, and after the
    first fix printed UNDETERMINED while counting rows that were unaffordable and therefore could
    never have been in the intersection. The verdict is the same as the first one; it is now
    supported by its own table instead of asserted over it.

=================================================================================================
WHERE THE TWO ROUTES LEAVE THE PROBLEM
=================================================================================================
Both failed, and they failed at the same place, which is worth more than either would have been
alone. Route 1 showed hubs are unaffordable: the 3-ball around SP1 is 95% of the genome. Route 2
shows they are IRREDUCIBLY unaffordable: a degree-k hub is a (k+1)-clique, so no width below k
represents it, error plateaus rather than falling, and choosing edges cleverly is worth nothing.

The obstruction is now one object, characterised three ways:
    a hub's targets are mutually dependent regardless of out-degree      (localclosure L7)
    its ball is the network                                              (localclosure L3)
    its clique cannot be fitted into any affordable width                (boundedwidth B2, B6)

Route 3 -- carrying controller history explicitly -- is the only one left, and statedim S6 already
says what it has to beat: conditioning on a controller's STATE works only at a 500x timescale
separation, and it is the trajectory rather than the state that carries the dependence. Route 3
is the one that attacks that directly, and it is untested.

=================================================================================================
ROUTE 3 OF 3 TESTED: CARRY THE CONTROLLER'S HISTORY.  IT WORKS, AT A PRICE, AND ONLY FOR ONE
CLASS OF QUESTION.  Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_history.txt
=================================================================================================

WHY IT COULD NOT FAIL THE WAY THE OTHERS DID. In a star the targets ARE conditionally independent
given the controller's whole path -- that is what "the hub drives them and they do not touch each
other" means. So route 3 was never going to hit a structural obstruction. It could only be
expensive, and the measurement is how expensive.

The controller's path is carried exactly from the transition semigroup. At L = 0 the construction
reproduces statedim S6's I(i;j|h) to every digit -- 1.464892e-02 both times, on the same system --
which is the cross-module identity check, not a new number.

WHAT ACTUALLY LIMITS IT, and it was not what I expected. Adding lags at fixed spacing PLATEAUS:

    L        0      1      2      4      6      8     10
    err   0.244  0.073  0.028  0.015  0.014  0.014  0.014

More lags extend the window backwards and buy nothing after L = 4. The missing information is the
path BETWEEN samples. Holding the window fixed at W = 1.2 and refining the resolution instead:

    dt      0.300  0.200  0.150  0.120  0.100  0.086  0.075
    L           4      6      8     10     12     14     16
    cost       64    256   1024   4096  16384  65536 262144
    err     6.5e-2 3.5e-2 2.1e-2 1.4e-2 1.0e-2 7.5e-3 5.8e-3

    fitted:  tail error ~ dt^1.753,  cost = 2^(W/dt + 2)

ACCURACY IS POLYNOMIAL IN THE RESOLUTION WHILE COST IS EXPONENTIAL IN IT. Inverting:

    1e-2  needs L = 12,  cost/gene 1.6e+04
    1e-3  needs L = 44,  cost/gene 9.9e+13
    1e-4  needs L = 165, cost/gene 2.5e+50

One per cent is reachable. Every further decade costs about nine orders of magnitude.

IT IS THE CONTROLLER'S HISTORY SPECIFICALLY, NOT MORE CONDITIONING (R6). Carrying the same number
of lags of an ordinary target instead of the hub leaves 480x more residual dependence at every L.

AND IT AGREES WITH statedim S6 FROM THE OTHER SIDE (R3). The gain from carrying history is 54x and
89x at timescale ratios 1 and 0.1 -- exactly where state-conditioning failed -- and 0 to 4x at the
extremes where state-conditioning already worked. Two modules, two directions, same shape.

THE DELIVERABLE: HOW MUCH ACCURACY, AT WHAT COMPUTE, FOR EACH ROUTE
=================================================================================================
Costs are doubles per gene; the genome-scale column assumes 20,000 genes at 8 bytes.

  ROUTE 1  local closure on r-balls                       (rem/atlas/localclosure.py)
    best accuracy reachable   arbitrary on chain-like topology; r=3 gives 5.5e-3 at a 1e-13 event
    compute                   N 2^|B_r|;  |B_3| = 7 on a cascade  ->  20 MB at genome scale
    on REAL topology          |B_3| around SP1 is 2,724 of 2,861 genes  ->  2^2724.  UNREACHABLE

  ROUTE 2  bounded-width decomposition                    (rem/atlas/boundedwidth.py)

    compute                   N 2^(w+1), fixed by construction
    local questions           w = 4 is exact for a 4-target event  ->  5 MB at genome scale
    global questions          error GROWS with controller degree at fixed width:

                              all five affordable widths fail the 1% bar at the hub end

  ROUTE 3  explicit controller history                    (rem/atlas/history.py)
    best accuracy reachable   1e-2 practical; 1e-3 at 9.9e13 per gene; 1e-4 never

                              k = 4, 6, 8, 10  ->  3.6e-3, 5.3e-3, 6.7e-3, 7.9e-3 at L = 10

                              acquires targets, and the only one that reaches 1% on a global
                              question at genome scale

=================================================================================================
THE ANSWER IS A MATCHING, NOT A WINNER
=================================================================================================
The three routes are not competitors at the same job, and two of my own gates went wrong by
assuming they were -- R8 was written three times, once with an observable that got harder as the
variable swept, once with one that fitted inside a single bag and flattered route 2.

    LOCAL question   -- does this pathway carry flux, will these four genes fire together
                     bounded width, w ~ 4-6, about 5 MB, exact or near it
    GLOBAL question  -- collective fluctuation across everything a regulator touches
                     controller history, L ~ 12, about 2.6 GB, 1% and no better
    CHAIN-LIKE part  -- metabolism, linear cascades
                     r-balls, r ~ 3, about 20 MB, arbitrary accuracy

Nothing reaches 0.1% on a global question at any compute this decade. That is the ceiling, and it
is set by a specific and now well-characterised object: a controller whose targets are mutually
dependent regardless of how many there are, whose ball is the whole network, whose clique fits in
no affordable width, and whose trajectory -- not its state -- carries the dependence.

All three routes named in statedim are now tested. Two failed against that object and the third
buys 1% by paying exponentially for path resolution.

=================================================================================================
THE HYBRID ENGINE: ALL THREE ROUTES COMBINED, AND MEASURED
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_engine.txt
=================================================================================================

THE CONSTRUCTION.  P_hat(x) = sum_a P(a) prod_i P( x_i | x_pa(i), a )

Controller history supplies the strata a; r-balls on the graph MINUS the controllers supply the
parent sets; bounded-width thinning caps them. It is a strict generalisation of all three routes,
and H1 verifies that by reducing it to each EXACTLY -- 0.0, 1.4e-17, 1.4e-17 -- so the comparison
is against real rivals rather than against the engine's own reimplementation of them.

Test system: a controller over N targets that ALSO regulate each other. Star plus chain. That is
what a real network is and what no single route handles.

WHAT A POINT COMPARISON SAYS: NOTHING. On N = 8, route 2 at width 7 holds every variable in one
bag and is EXACT for cost 510, beating every hybrid configuration. On any system small enough to
solve exactly, the exact method wins. This is reported as uninformative rather than suppressed.

WHAT THE SCALING SAYS, and this is the result. Cheapest configuration reaching 1% relative error
in Var(total ON count) -- a global observable whose meaning does not change with N:

    N        route 1          route 2           HYBRID
    6      126 (r=5)        126 (w=5)      176 (r=1,L=2)     <- the hybrid LOSES here
    8      382 (r=6)        510 (w=7)      432 (r=2,L=2)
   10     1534 (r=8)       2046 (w=9)      560 (r=2,L=2)
   12     4094 (r=9)      8190 (w=11)      688 (r=2,L=2)

    growth from N=6 to N=12:   route 1  x32.5    route 2  x65.0    hybrid  x3.9

Route 2 grows as 2^N exactly -- 126, 510, 2046, 8190 is a factor of four per two variables. The
hybrid grows LINEARLY. At N = 12 it is already six to twelve times cheaper, and the gap widens
without limit.

THE CROSSOVER IS THE POINT. The hybrid LOSES at N = 6, because at small N exactness is cheap and
the history machinery is not worth its overhead. A method that won at every size would be suspect.

THE MECHANISM, MEASURED ON REAL TOPOLOGY (H5). TRRUST v2 human, maximum residual 2-ball after
removing the h highest-degree genes:

    controllers removed      3      13      38     122     281
    max residual 2-ball   1564     848     423      96      25

localclosure L3 measured that same ball at 2,724 of 2,861 genes with the hubs left in. Removing
122 controllers -- 4% of the network -- collapses it to 96. That collapse IS the engine.

IT IS THE CONTROLLERS SPECIFICALLY (H6). Carrying the same number of lags of a random gene instead
of the controller gives 17x worse error at every L. And the controllers are DETECTED by degree
over a wide threshold band (H4), not hand-picked, which is what has to be true for a network
nobody has annotated.

WHERE IT STILL FAILS (H7). Converged accuracy 7.09e-3, which meets the 1% bar. The residual is the
controller's path between sample times, exactly what history.py R5 priced: halving it doubles L
and squares the cost. The hybrid moves the ceiling; it does not remove it.

FOUR DEFECTS IN THIS MODULE, and the last is a pattern:
  - hybrid_cost derived "is gene i regulated by a controller" from the target-only adjacency,
    but the controller is outside that index range, so it returned False for every gene and
    zeroed the history term. Caught before commit.
  - the component sweeps stopped at cost ~150 while hybrids ran to 28,000. Extended to equal
    cost, route 1 at r=6 and route 2 at w=7 both beat every hybrid point. The first run's
    "23.9x improvement, 8 of 8" was an artefact of under-resourcing the rivals.
  - the best hybrid point was a ZERO CROSSING. Signed error runs -1.26e-1, -2.53e-2, +5.58e-3,
    +1.78e-2; the quoted 5.58e-3 was the crossing and the converged value is 1.78e-2. The Var
    error was monotone throughout, which is what exposed it -- two observables disagreeing about
    which configuration is best is the signature of a cancellation. H7 then quoted that same
    cancellation two sections after H2a identified it.
  - THE PATTERN: a comparison at ONE system size cannot rank approximations, because wherever
    the exact method is affordable it wins. This build order made that mistake three times --
    history R7 on a six-target star where exactness costs 128, R8 written three times over the
    same issue, and engine H2. The rule, stated now so it is not made a fourth time: AN
    APPROXIMATION IS RANKED BY A SCALING LAW, NEVER AT A POINT.

THE ENGINE, AT GENOME SCALE

Extrapolating the measured scaling with 20,000 genes, 122 detected controllers, r = 2, L = 8:

    exact junction tree      2^20000                          impossible
    route 1 alone            2^2724 (SP1's ball)              impossible
    route 2 alone            48 GB at w=20, still 4.9% of direct edges lost, 1% bar not met

=================================================================================================
RETRACTION: "THE ENGINE, AT GENOME SCALE ... HYBRID ~14 MB" IS WRONG BY ~48 ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE
=================================================================================================
The section above ends by extrapolating the hybrid engine to 20,000 genes at about 14 MB, 1% on
both local and global questions. That number is withdrawn. Two defects in engine.hybrid_cost,
both invisible on every test system this build order used and both fatal on real topology.

(1) HISTORY BITS DO NOT ADD ACROSS CONTROLLERS -- THEY MULTIPLY. hybrid_cost charges (L+1) bits
    to any gene a controller touches. A gene regulated by k_i controllers needs k_i*(L+1) bits,
    because the conditioning strata are the JOINT controller histories. Every star test system
    in this build order gave every gene exactly one controller, so k_i was identically 1 and the
    error could not appear. On TRRUST at controller threshold 25:

        |C|     k_i mean   median   max   frac k>=2   frac k=0
          3         0.39        0     3       0.108      0.749
         13         0.85        0    11       0.208      0.546
         38         1.37        1    17       0.303      0.393
        122         1.88        1    17       0.417      0.224
        281         1.98        1    11       0.460      0.142

    41.7% of genes have two or more controllers and the maximum is 17.

(2) THE CONTROLLER BLOCK IS PRICED AT ZERO. P(a), the joint over the controllers' own histories,
    never enters the cost at all. That makes the functional degenerate: designating every gene a
    controller drives the reported cost towards zero. Priced honestly the block is
    2^(|C|*(L+1)) -- at |C| = 122 and L = 8 that is 2^1098.

    corrected, TRRUST, r = 2, w = 6, L = 8
        |C|      engine.py reports     corrected per-gene      controller block
          3              3.877e+07              1.604e+12                  2^27
         13              6.005e+07              8.256e+31                 2^117
         38              6.906e+07              1.839e+55                 2^342
        122              5.386e+07              9.503e+55                >2^400

WHAT SURVIVES. The star-topology results are correct FOR STAR TOPOLOGY, where k_i = 1 by
construction: the scaling law (route 1 x32.5, route 2 x65.0, hybrid x3.9 from N=6 to N=12), the
reduction identities, the matched controls, and the accuracy curves. What does not survive is the
extrapolation of that cost to a real network, and with it the claim that the engine runs at
genome scale. hybrid_cost is kept under its own name, with the retraction written into the file
above it, so that which results are affected stays visible.

HOW IT WAS FOUND. Not by a gate. Eight independent adversarial reviewers were asked, before the
TRRUST module was written, to find ways the measurement could be invalid; two of them
independently identified both defects, and a third pointed out that the cost functional was
degenerate. Every gate in engine.py passed on the star system and would have passed again on
TRRUST, because none of them priced a gene with two controllers.

=================================================================================================
THE CLOSED CIRCUIT, AND THE PIPELINE MADE EXECUTABLE
Sources: rem/atlas/RESULTS_circuit.txt, rem/atlas/RESULTS_trrust_engine.txt
=================================================================================================

ONE CIRCUIT THROUGH FOUR LAYERS, SOLVED EXACTLY. ligand -> receptor(membrane) -> active ->
kinase -> TF -> promoter -> mRNA -> protein, the protein acting back on the kinase as a
phosphatase, the receptor trafficking membrane -> endosome -> recycled or degraded. 5,184 states,
17 reactions. Each layer uses the representation its physics asks for: compartment occupancies for
trafficking, activity states for signalling, a two-state controller for the TF, a promoter switch
feeding birth-death mRNA and protein.

The six-stage rule is the gate structure, in order, and each stage did work:

X1 PHYSICS AUDIT -- FAILED FIRST, AND THE FAILURE WAS REAL. Sealed with no ligand and no
   synthesis, the system retained 92.6% of its mass. Unbound membrane receptor had no exit at
   all: binding needs ligand, nothing else removed it, so Rm was immortal. The fix is
   CONSTITUTIVE INTERNALISATION, which is documented receptor biology. The audit found a missing
   biological pathway, not a broken test. Sealed mass 9.26e-01 -> 1.9e-13.

X2 IDENTITY -- three, each against something computed another way: ligand zero gives
   P(protein>0) = 2.4e-20; feedback off reproduces an independently built open-loop generator to
   0.0; the engine at full width reproduces the exact marginal to 0.0.

X3 EXACT BENCHMARK against an analytic identity. k_md*E[M] = k_tx*P(G=1, M<cap) holds to 1.3e-13.
   The NAIVE form without the truncation term is off by 12.9% -- an identity has to know about
   the lattice it is checked on.

X4 RARE TAIL. P(protein saturated AND receptor fully internalised) = 3.60e-2 against 3.41e-2 from
   the product of marginals, a ratio of 1.055. The layers are dependent, so a conjunctive question
   spanning trafficking and expression is not answerable from the two marginals separately -- but
   at 5.5% this dependence is weak, and that is stated rather than inflated.

X5 SCALING, with the timescale ratio measured rather than assumed:
       tau_TF = 0.713, tau_promoter = 0.400, ratio 1.78

   statedim S6 found controller history helps only at ~500x separation either way, so this
   circuit sits deep in the MATCHED regime -- the expensive one. The route is being tested where
   it is predicted to do worst, which is the honest place to test it.

       k        2        3        4         5        6
       width    6       14       30        62      126        = 2^(k+1) - 2
       history  128     768     4096     20480      --

   Bounded width wins by two orders of magnitude and both grow exponentially. Consistent with S6:
   at matched timescales, carrying the controller's history buys nothing worth its price.

X6 VALIDATION -- direction only. Faster receptor internalisation monotonically weakens output
   (E[P] 0.859 -> 0.507 as k_int goes 0.1 -> 8.1), which is the documented direction. Stated
   plainly in the module: this is weak, many wrong models would also pass it, and real validation
   needs a measured time-course fitted on one condition and PREDICTED on another.

A DEFECT CAUGHT BEFORE IT WAS REPORTED, for once. X5's first run swept L <= 6 and dt >= 0.06 -- a
window of 0.36 against a TF correlation time of 0.713 -- and printed "not reached" for k >= 4.
Widened, the history route reaches the bar at k=4 for 4,096. It still loses by 137x, but losing
and not-reaching are different results. This is the fourth appearance of the under-resourced-rival
error in this build order and the first that did not survive to a report.
