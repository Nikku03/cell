=================================================================================================
THE TWO OPEN PROBLEMS, MEASURED
  1. does a real controller make the important parameters explode?
  2. can grouping keep the joint state small at realistic scale?
Sources: rem/atlas/RESULTS_controller.txt, rem/atlas/RESULTS_statedim.txt
=================================================================================================

THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE. They come apart. A controller is CHEAP IN PARAMETERS and EXPENSIVE IN
STATE: closing the loop leaves the set of rates worth measuring at about five, no matter how big
the system gets, but a single global regulator takes the joint state from 2^4 per variable to 2^N.
Regulation does not increase what must be MEASURED. It increases what must be SIMULATED JOINTLY.

=================================================================================================
PROBLEM 1  --  NO EXPLOSION, AND THE IMPORTANT SET MOVES ONTO THE CONTROLLER
=================================================================================================

    sweep                             P range     N90 range          exponent b
    A  add controllers, fixed plant    45 -> 141   4.90 -> 8.55       +0.4688 +- 0.0503
    B  grow the whole regulated system 37 -> 133   6.75 -> 4.27       -0.2518 +- 0.1251
    predeclared bands: b > 0.7 EXPLOSION, b < 0.3 SPARSE

Sweep B is the one that answers the scaling question, and it is NEGATIVE: N90 does not grow with
the system, it shrinks slightly. Seven-fold more parameters, the same five that matter. Sweep A is
intermediate at +0.47, but even there N90/P falls from 0.109 to 0.061 -- adding regulators adds
important parameters sublinearly, never in proportion.

TRANSFER IS REAL AND IT IS ABRUPT.

    K       controller share of ||C||^2     rank correlation with the open-loop plant ranking
    0                            0.0000                                              1.0000
    1                            0.0926                                              0.4478
    8                            0.1466                                              0.4458
   32                            0.6432                                              0.4536

ONE controller edge is enough to halve the rank correlation. The plant ranking computed open-loop
is not merely incomplete under feedback, it is reordered, and it reorders as soon as the loop
closes at all. At K = 32, seven of the ten largest control coefficients are controller parameters.
So the answer is not "sparse", it is TRANSFER: the same small number of things matter, and they
are different things.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY, per gate C6 as predeclared. Shuffling the regulatory wiring at random --
random target, random sensor, random sign -- gives the same scaling (b = -0.32 +- 0.32 against
-0.19 +- 0.21). The sparsity is a property of closed-loop systems of this class, NOT of the
canonical end-product architecture. C3 therefore says nothing about which biological wiring is
special. It does say the sparsity is robust to getting the wiring wrong, which is the more useful
half for a measurement programme.

GATES:  C1 PASS (adjoint to 1e-9 scaled, both FD directions)   C2 PASS (summation theorem exactly
1.0000000000 in five configurations and correctly BROKEN to 0.8726 for a proteome-sensing
controller)   C5 PASS (||C|| does not collapse, so this is not homeostasis)   C6 NOT SEPARATED
C7 FAIL -- the acceptance bars were six orders too loose and caught only 1 of 3 planted runaways.
Contained: C3 runs canonical wiring only, so no rejected configuration enters the measurement.
The bars were deliberately not retuned.

=================================================================================================
PROBLEM 2  --  THE STATE DOES NOT STAY SMALL, AND THE REASON IS TOPOLOGY, NOT DECAY
=================================================================================================

Dependence decays extremely fast: xi = 0.2486 +- 0.0005, R^2 = 0.999976, a factor of 55 per step.
That is not enough, and the reason is worth stating precisely.

STEP 1 -- THE THRESHOLD IS NOT FREE. From the measured law tail_err = 20.23 sqrt(MI), a 1% tail
tolerance forces MI < 2.44e-07. A bulk threshold of 1e-3, which keeps means fine, would admit
tail errors of 0.64.

STEP 2 -- THE RULE AS WRITTEN RETURNS THE WHOLE NETWORK. MI at distance 1 is 6.3e-03, four orders
above the tail-legal threshold, so every directly regulated pair is joined; and a regulatory
network is connected. Thresholding therefore gives ONE component of N for any connected topology
at any tail tolerance, and costs 2^N. This is not a decay problem. Decay is fast.

STEP 3 -- THE RIGHT COST IS TREEWIDTH. A path is one component but has treewidth 1.

    topology              N = 20,000    treewidth    state cost
    cascade                                      3     3.20e+05      tractable
    random degree 2                            >40     2.69e+14      not
    random degree 3                       too dense to compute       not
    scale-free m = 2                      too dense to compute       not

Only a pure cascade survives. Anything with mean degree 2 or more is past treewidth 40 by N = 512.
Robustness (S8b): at r = 1, the BARE regulatory graph and the most optimistic dependence graph
there could be, the answer is unchanged -- random degree 2 and scale-free are already past 40 at
N = 512. The obstruction is the topology of gene regulation, not the range of the dependence.

STEP 4 -- A GLOBAL CONTROLLER MAKES IT WORSE, AND CONDITIONING ONLY SOMETIMES SAVES IT. One hub
makes the dependence graph COMPLETE: treewidth N. Putting the controller into every bag drops it
back to 4 -- but only when the controller's timescale is far from its targets':

    tau_hub / tau_gene      r_cond       cost per gene
              2000             3              2^5        affordable
               500             4              2^6        affordable
               333            12            2^14         marginal
        200 down to 0.01      >=15           2^N         no grouping helps
              0.0025           5              2^7        affordable
              0.0014           3              2^5        affordable

THE WINDOW: the controller must be at least ~500x SLOWER than what it controls, so that its state
IS its history, or at least ~400x FASTER, where it correlates nothing to begin with. Within a
few hundred either way, conditioning fails and the cost is 2^N. Kinase signalling at seconds
against transcription at tens of minutes is a ratio of roughly 100 to 1000 -- ON that boundary,
not comfortably inside it.

THE MECHANISM, and it is the reason a prediction was lost. The original S4 predicted conditioning
on a controller would restore decay. It does not: the correlation is carried by the controller's
TRAJECTORY, not by its current value, so conditioning on the instantaneous state leaves it. A slow
controller is quasi-static and its state is its history; a fast one is white noise and correlates
nothing. In between, its history is genuinely extra state.

GATES:  S1 PASS (residual < 1e-14; MI exactly 0 on independent variables)   S2 PASS   S5 PASS (the
dependence graph IS path^3, which is what licenses extrapolating past N = 16)   S6 bracketed
S8 PASS   S3 FAIL -- xi scattered 0.0045 against a 0.0043 bar. Non-monotone in N and surviving a
common fit range, so wobble rather than drift; and the quantity used downstream is the INTEGER r,
which a 1.7% wobble cannot move. The gate failed; the conclusion does not rest on it.

=================================================================================================
WHAT THIS CHANGES
=================================================================================================

FOR MEASUREMENT. Problem 1's answer is good news and it redirects the programme. The rates worth
measuring under feedback are the REGULATORY ones -- gains, thresholds, cooperativities -- not the
catalytic ones, and an open-loop ranking of catalytic rates is reordered by the first controller
edge you add. This is consistent with what the corrected Recon3D run already said from the other
direction: with a sealed medium, zero enzyme capacities have a realised sensitivity.

FOR THE ENGINE. Problem 2's answer is bad news and it is structural. Section 14's split criterion,
applied at a tail-legal threshold, returns the whole network on any connected topology. The repair
is a tree decomposition rather than a threshold, and then the engine is tractable exactly on
low-treewidth topologies -- which real gene regulation is not. Three routes remain and none is
tested here: exploit that the tail-legal graph is a bounded graph POWER (structure a generic
treewidth bound ignores), accept bounded error from a fixed-width decomposition and measure what
it costs in tail accuracy, or carry controller history explicitly so conditioning works at matched
timescales.

FOR HONESTY. Two gates failed in this pair of modules and both are recorded rather than retuned:
controller C7 (bars six orders too loose, contained) and statedim S3 (a real miss that does not
propagate). One prediction of mine was made and lost before commit, and is kept as made.

=================================================================================================
ROUTE 1 OF 3 TESTED: THE BOUNDED GRAPH POWER.  IT FAILS, AND THE FAILURE IS SPECIFIC
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_localclosure.txt
=================================================================================================

WHAT WAS TRIED. Not lifting a tree decomposition from G to G^r -- S8(b) already killed that, since
treewidth is past 40 at r = 1. Instead, giving up exact marginalisation: if every dependence above
tau is confined to distance r, factorise on r-balls,

    P_hat(x) = prod_i P( x_i | x_{B_r(i) and earlier} )

a Bayesian network whose cost is sum_i 2^(1+|pa_i|) -- linear in N, no treewidth term anywhere.

THE APPROXIMATION WORKS. On a cascade, tested at three tail depths down to P = 1.3e-13:

    r   max|pa|   tail rel err at P=1.3e-13    max error in any mean
    1         1                    1.063e+00                5.0e-16
    2         2                    4.604e-02                4.4e-16
    3         3                    5.509e-03                6.1e-16
    4         4                    1.190e-03                5.6e-16

This is the sharpest instance of the bulk/tail split in this build order, and the cleanest,
because here the bulk error is EXACTLY ZERO by construction -- the factorisation reproduces every
marginal in the ball -- while the conjunctive event is wrong by 106% at r = 1. Six modules have
now measured a bulk quantity right while the tail was wrong; this one measures it at machine
precision against a 100% tail error in the same row.

THE COST DOES NOT WORK, ON REAL TOPOLOGY. TRRUST v2 human, 2,861 genes, 8,403 edges:

    r    median ball    90th    99th     max      gene at the max
    1              3      12      62     480      SP1
    2            118     615    1177    1813      CDKN1A
    3           1381    2169    2532    2724      SP1

The 3-ball around SP1 is 2,724 of 2,861 genes -- 95% of the network in one factor. Cost is set by
the WORST ball, so the median of 1,381 is not a reprieve, and quoting it would be ledger S. The
closure costs 2^2724 against a junction tree's 2^40+. The route does not merely fail to help; on
real topology it is worse than what it replaces.

HUB CONDITIONING DOES NOT RESCUE IT. Deleting the h highest-degree genes and conditioning:

    h        max|B_2|   treewidth    total cost = 2^h x N x 2^max|B_2|
    0            1813         >40                            4.6e+63
   20             682         >40                            4.8e+69
  200              44          23                            7.5e+76
  400              14           3                            6.5e+67

The ball sizes fall exactly as hoped -- 400 deletions take treewidth to 3 -- but 2^400 eats the
saving many times over. There is no h that wins.

THE ASSUMPTION UNDERNEATH WAS CHECKED, AND IT HELD. statedim's S5 verified "dependence graph =
G^r" on a cascade and a random graph, never on a hub, and the whole cost verdict depends on two of
SP1's 484 targets really being dependent above tau. Measured exactly (L7):

    out-degree 4, 8, 14  ->  sibling MI 1.35e-4, 1.55e-4, 1.36e-4     FLAT
    in-degree 1 .. 12    ->  sibling MI 1.50e-4 down to 4.89e-7       307x, and still above tau

Out-degree does not dilute AT ALL: a hub with fourteen targets correlates them exactly as strongly
as one with four, because its state is shared by all of them equally. In-degree does dilute, as
roughly its square, but is still above tau at in-degree 12 -- and TRRUST's mean in-degree is 2.9,
where sibling dependence sits ~80x above tau. So the ball sizes are not overestimates.

WHAT THIS LEAVES. The failure is now specific rather than general, which is worth more than the
route would have been:

  - the r-ball FACTORISATION is sound and cheap in accuracy: r = 3 buys 0.55% error on a 1e-13
    event with three-variable factors
  - the r-ball GEOMETRY is what fails, and only because of hubs
  - hubs cannot be conditioned away arithmetically (2^h), cannot be diluted away by out-degree,
    and are not diluted enough by biological in-degree

So the obstruction has been localised to one thing: a small number of very high out-degree
regulators, whose targets are mutually dependent no matter how many of them there are. Any route
that survives has to treat those specific vertices differently -- not by conditioning on their
STATE, which costs 2^h and which statedim's S6 says is only legitimate at a 500x timescale
separation anyway. The two remaining routes from statedim are untouched: bounded-width
decomposition with measured error, and carrying controller history explicitly.

ONE BOUND DID NOT SURVIVE. tail_err <= 20.23 sqrt(MI) fails in 5 of 16 rows where the MI is above
its own numerical floor, so it cannot be used to CHOOSE r. r has to be chosen from a measured
error curve. (L2 as first written compared against the law at every r including rows where the MI
was below its floor -- a guard statedim's S2 had and this gate did not; both floors are now
measured, the tail statistic's by solving the stationary equation twice from independent starts
and differencing, which gives 3.8e-16.)

=================================================================================================
ROUTE 2 OF 3 TESTED: BOUNDED WIDTH WITH MEASURED ERROR.  IT FAILS, AND IT FAILS IRREDUCIBLY
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_boundedwidth.txt
=================================================================================================

WHY THIS WAS BETTER POSED THAN ROUTE 1. Route 1 fixed the geometry and the width came out at
2,724. Here the width is fixed FIRST at whatever is affordable and the decomposition is forced
into it by deleting edges, so cost is N 2^(w+1) by construction and cannot blow up. The whole
question becomes the error, which is what should have been measured all along.

THE ANSWER DEPENDS ENTIRELY ON TOPOLOGY, AND THE DEPENDENCE IS EXTREME.

    deep tail (P ~ 5e-14), tail relative error at width w
    w        cascade      random degree 2      hub
    1        1.06e+00     9.67e-01             9.83e-01
    3        5.51e-03     3.06e-01             9.19e-01
    5        1.01e-05     3.22e-02             7.46e-01
    8        6.56e-06     1.07e-01             2.93e-01

On a cascade, width 3 is enough. On a hub graph, width 8 still leaves 29% error. And the random
graph is NON-MONOTONE -- w = 6 is worse than w = 5 -- so accuracy cannot simply be bought with
budget.

THE MECHANISM, AND IT IS THE SAME ONE ROUTE 1 FOUND. A hub of degree k is a (k+1)-CLIQUE in the
dependence graph. No width below k can hold it, so the last edges that any affordable width is
forced to drop are hub edges -- the strongest ones in the graph. Extending the reference curve
down to a drop fraction of 0.025 shows the consequence directly: the hub error PLATEAUS at 6-9%
instead of going to zero. Buying width stops buying accuracy.

THE ONE NUMBER HERE WITH NO MODEL IN IT. On TRRUST v2 human, what fraction of the 8,403 direct
regulatory edges is still represented at width w -- both endpoints together in some bag:

    w      cost N.2^(w+1)     edges represented     lost
    5           1.83e+05          6044  (72.8%)    27.2%
   10           5.86e+06          7167  (86.3%)    13.7%
   15           1.88e+08          7649  (92.2%)     7.8%
   20           6.00e+09          7890  (95.1%)     4.9%
   25           1.92e+11          8049  (97.0%)     3.0%

This contains no dynamics and no mutual information. It is a property of the graph and the budget.

THE VERDICT. Predeclared: affordable = 2^w <= 1e9, accurate = tail error < 1%.

    w     TRRUST lost     err, cascade end     err, hub end     affordable   accurate
    5           0.272             1.68e-05         5.97e-01        yes         no
   10           0.137             8.71e-06         2.52e-01        yes         no
   15           0.078             1.57e-06         6.54e-02        yes         no
   20           0.049             7.86e-07         7.19e-02        yes         no
   25           0.030             2.60e-06         8.49e-02        yes         no

EMPTY INTERSECTION. All five affordable widths are inside the measured domain and all five fail
at the hub end. Note the spread between the two ends -- four to five orders at the same drop
fraction -- which is why B5 reports a range rather than a number, and why the hub end governs:
TRRUST is hub-rich.

THE MATCHED CONTROL IS THE MOST USEFUL RESULT IN THE MODULE. Dropping weakest-MI-first against
dropping at random, at identical width:

    cascade              694x better
    random degree 2      6.1x better
    hub                  1.0x -- NO BETTER THAN RANDOM

Choosing which edges to keep is worth five orders of magnitude on a chain and worth exactly
nothing on a hub. On the topology that matters there is no strategy, only a budget.

FOUR DEFECTS OF MINE IN THIS MODULE, all recorded rather than patched away:
  - the dependence graph was thresholded at tau before thinning, which left a 7.4e-5 residual in
    the identity case that had nothing to do with width. Caught before commit; the graph is now
    complete and MI-weighted so width is the only thing that drops an edge.
  - B4 counted every deleted edge including FILL-IN, and reported dropping 16,522 of 8,403 edges,
    a fraction of 1.97. Caught by its own output being impossible.
  - B5 fed those impossible fractions into an interpolation and numpy CLAMPED instead of
    refusing, returning one constant that looked like a result.
  - B6 then printed EMPTY INTERSECTION on the strength of a single in-domain row, and after the
    first fix printed UNDETERMINED while counting rows that were unaffordable and therefore could
    never have been in the intersection. The verdict is the same as the first one; it is now
    supported by its own table instead of asserted over it.

=================================================================================================
WHERE THE TWO ROUTES LEAVE THE PROBLEM
=================================================================================================
Both failed, and they failed at the same place, which is worth more than either would have been
alone. Route 1 showed hubs are unaffordable: the 3-ball around SP1 is 95% of the genome. Route 2
shows they are IRREDUCIBLY unaffordable: a degree-k hub is a (k+1)-clique, so no width below k
represents it, error plateaus rather than falling, and choosing edges cleverly is worth nothing.

The obstruction is now one object, characterised three ways:
    a hub's targets are mutually dependent regardless of out-degree      (localclosure L7)
    its ball is the network                                              (localclosure L3)
    its clique cannot be fitted into any affordable width                (boundedwidth B2, B6)

Route 3 -- carrying controller history explicitly -- is the only one left, and statedim S6 already
says what it has to beat: conditioning on a controller's STATE works only at a 500x timescale
separation, and it is the trajectory rather than the state that carries the dependence. Route 3
is the one that attacks that directly, and it is untested.

=================================================================================================
ROUTE 3 OF 3 TESTED: CARRY THE CONTROLLER'S HISTORY.  IT WORKS, AT A PRICE, AND ONLY FOR ONE
CLASS OF QUESTION.  Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_history.txt
=================================================================================================

WHY IT COULD NOT FAIL THE WAY THE OTHERS DID. In a star the targets ARE conditionally independent
given the controller's whole path -- that is what "the hub drives them and they do not touch each
other" means. So route 3 was never going to hit a structural obstruction. It could only be
expensive, and the measurement is how expensive.

The controller's path is carried exactly from the transition semigroup. At L = 0 the construction
reproduces statedim S6's I(i;j|h) to every digit -- 1.464892e-02 both times, on the same system --
which is the cross-module identity check, not a new number.

WHAT ACTUALLY LIMITS IT, and it was not what I expected. Adding lags at fixed spacing PLATEAUS:

    L        0      1      2      4      6      8     10
    err   0.244  0.073  0.028  0.015  0.014  0.014  0.014

More lags extend the window backwards and buy nothing after L = 4. The missing information is the
path BETWEEN samples. Holding the window fixed at W = 1.2 and refining the resolution instead:

    dt      0.300  0.200  0.150  0.120  0.100  0.086  0.075
    L           4      6      8     10     12     14     16
    cost       64    256   1024   4096  16384  65536 262144
    err     6.5e-2 3.5e-2 2.1e-2 1.4e-2 1.0e-2 7.5e-3 5.8e-3

    fitted:  tail error ~ dt^1.753,  cost = 2^(W/dt + 2)

ACCURACY IS POLYNOMIAL IN THE RESOLUTION WHILE COST IS EXPONENTIAL IN IT. Inverting:

    1e-2  needs L = 12,  cost/gene 1.6e+04
    1e-3  needs L = 44,  cost/gene 9.9e+13
    1e-4  needs L = 165, cost/gene 2.5e+50

One per cent is reachable. Every further decade costs about nine orders of magnitude.

IT IS THE CONTROLLER'S HISTORY SPECIFICALLY, NOT MORE CONDITIONING (R6). Carrying the same number
of lags of an ordinary target instead of the hub leaves 480x more residual dependence at every L.

AND IT AGREES WITH statedim S6 FROM THE OTHER SIDE (R3). The gain from carrying history is 54x and
89x at timescale ratios 1 and 0.1 -- exactly where state-conditioning failed -- and 0 to 4x at the
extremes where state-conditioning already worked. Two modules, two directions, same shape.

=================================================================================================
THE DELIVERABLE: HOW MUCH ACCURACY, AT WHAT COMPUTE, FOR EACH ROUTE
=================================================================================================
Costs are doubles per gene; the genome-scale column assumes 20,000 genes at 8 bytes.

  ROUTE 1  local closure on r-balls                       (rem/atlas/localclosure.py)
    best accuracy reachable   arbitrary on chain-like topology; r=3 gives 5.5e-3 at a 1e-13 event
    compute                   N 2^|B_r|;  |B_3| = 7 on a cascade  ->  20 MB at genome scale
    on REAL topology          |B_3| around SP1 is 2,724 of 2,861 genes  ->  2^2724.  UNREACHABLE
    verdict                   fails on any hub-rich graph, at any accuracy, at any cost

  ROUTE 2  bounded-width decomposition                    (rem/atlas/boundedwidth.py)
    best accuracy reachable   EXACT for events inside one bag; ~6% floor for hub-mediated ones
    compute                   N 2^(w+1), fixed by construction
    local questions           w = 4 is exact for a 4-target event  ->  5 MB at genome scale
    global questions          error GROWS with controller degree at fixed width:
                              k = 4, 6, 8, 10  ->  2e-16, 9.3e-3, 2.8e-2, 4.3e-2 at w = 4
    on REAL topology          w = 20 costs 48 GB and still loses 4.9% of direct regulatory edges;
                              all five affordable widths fail the 1% bar at the hub end
    verdict                   the cheapest route by far for LOCAL questions; cannot reach 1% on

  ROUTE 3  explicit controller history                    (rem/atlas/history.py)
    best accuracy reachable   1e-2 practical; 1e-3 at 9.9e13 per gene; 1e-4 never
    compute                   N 2^(L+2), INDEPENDENT of controller degree
                              L = 12  ->  1.6e4 per gene  ->  2.6 GB at genome scale for 1%
    global questions          error FLAT in controller degree:
                              k = 4, 6, 8, 10  ->  3.6e-3, 5.3e-3, 6.7e-3, 7.9e-3 at L = 10
    verdict                   the only route whose error does not degrade as a controller
                              acquires targets, and the only one that reaches 1% on a global
                              question at genome scale

=================================================================================================
THE ANSWER IS A MATCHING, NOT A WINNER
=================================================================================================
The three routes are not competitors at the same job, and two of my own gates went wrong by
assuming they were -- R8 was written three times, once with an observable that got harder as the
variable swept, once with one that fitted inside a single bag and flattered route 2.

    LOCAL question   -- does this pathway carry flux, will these four genes fire together
                     bounded width, w ~ 4-6, about 5 MB, exact or near it
    GLOBAL question  -- collective fluctuation across everything a regulator touches
                     controller history, L ~ 12, about 2.6 GB, 1% and no better
    CHAIN-LIKE part  -- metabolism, linear cascades
                     r-balls, r ~ 3, about 20 MB, arbitrary accuracy

Nothing reaches 0.1% on a global question at any compute this decade. That is the ceiling, and it
is set by a specific and now well-characterised object: a controller whose targets are mutually
dependent regardless of how many there are, whose ball is the whole network, whose clique fits in
no affordable width, and whose trajectory -- not its state -- carries the dependence.

All three routes named in statedim are now tested. Two failed against that object and the third
buys 1% by paying exponentially for path resolution.

=================================================================================================
THE HYBRID ENGINE: ALL THREE ROUTES COMBINED, AND MEASURED
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_engine.txt
=================================================================================================

THE CONSTRUCTION.  P_hat(x) = sum_a P(a) prod_i P( x_i | x_pa(i), a )

Controller history supplies the strata a; r-balls on the graph MINUS the controllers supply the
parent sets; bounded-width thinning caps them. It is a strict generalisation of all three routes,
and H1 verifies that by reducing it to each EXACTLY -- 0.0, 1.4e-17, 1.4e-17 -- so the comparison
is against real rivals rather than against the engine's own reimplementation of them.

Test system: a controller over N targets that ALSO regulate each other. Star plus chain. That is
what a real network is and what no single route handles.

WHAT A POINT COMPARISON SAYS: NOTHING. On N = 8, route 2 at width 7 holds every variable in one
bag and is EXACT for cost 510, beating every hybrid configuration. On any system small enough to
solve exactly, the exact method wins. This is reported as uninformative rather than suppressed.

WHAT THE SCALING SAYS, and this is the result. Cheapest configuration reaching 1% relative error
in Var(total ON count) -- a global observable whose meaning does not change with N:

    N        route 1          route 2           HYBRID
    6      126 (r=5)        126 (w=5)      176 (r=1,L=2)     <- the hybrid LOSES here
    8      382 (r=6)        510 (w=7)      432 (r=2,L=2)
   10     1534 (r=8)       2046 (w=9)      560 (r=2,L=2)
   12     4094 (r=9)      8190 (w=11)      688 (r=2,L=2)

    growth from N=6 to N=12:   route 1  x32.5    route 2  x65.0    hybrid  x3.9

Route 2 grows as 2^N exactly -- 126, 510, 2046, 8190 is a factor of four per two variables. The
hybrid grows LINEARLY. At N = 12 it is already six to twelve times cheaper, and the gap widens
without limit.

THE CROSSOVER IS THE POINT. The hybrid LOSES at N = 6, because at small N exactness is cheap and
the history machinery is not worth its overhead. A method that won at every size would be suspect.

THE MECHANISM, MEASURED ON REAL TOPOLOGY (H5). TRRUST v2 human, maximum residual 2-ball after
removing the h highest-degree genes:

    controllers removed      3      13      38     122     281
    max residual 2-ball   1564     848     423      96      25

localclosure L3 measured that same ball at 2,724 of 2,861 genes with the hubs left in. Removing
122 controllers -- 4% of the network -- collapses it to 96. That collapse IS the engine.

IT IS THE CONTROLLERS SPECIFICALLY (H6). Carrying the same number of lags of a random gene instead
of the controller gives 17x worse error at every L. And the controllers are DETECTED by degree
over a wide threshold band (H4), not hand-picked, which is what has to be true for a network
nobody has annotated.

WHERE IT STILL FAILS (H7). Converged accuracy 7.09e-3, which meets the 1% bar. The residual is the
controller's path between sample times, exactly what history.py R5 priced: halving it doubles L
and squares the cost. The hybrid moves the ceiling; it does not remove it.

FOUR DEFECTS IN THIS MODULE, and the last is a pattern:
  - hybrid_cost derived "is gene i regulated by a controller" from the target-only adjacency,
    but the controller is outside that index range, so it returned False for every gene and
    zeroed the history term. Caught before commit.
  - the component sweeps stopped at cost ~150 while hybrids ran to 28,000. Extended to equal
    cost, route 1 at r=6 and route 2 at w=7 both beat every hybrid point. The first run's
    "23.9x improvement, 8 of 8" was an artefact of under-resourcing the rivals.
  - the best hybrid point was a ZERO CROSSING. Signed error runs -1.26e-1, -2.53e-2, +5.58e-3,

=================================================================================================
THE FOURTH OBSTRUCTION ATTACKED: THE CONTROLLER BLOCK
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_block.txt
=================================================================================================

THE REFRAMING THAT MADE IT TRACTABLE. Every gene's factor depends only on the k_i controllers that
regulate IT -- median 1 on TRRUST. So the engine's sum_a P(a) prod_i f_i(a_C(i)) is a CONTRACTION
over a factor graph, not an enumeration, and if P(a) factorised over controllers it would separate
completely with no block term at all. The block exists ONLY because controllers are correlated
with one another. That turns an architectural problem into a measurable one.

E2  THE MOST ATTRACTIVE PRONG IS CONDITIONAL, NOT FREE. Writing P(a) as a dynamic Bayesian network
    over time slices is linear in L instead of exponential -- but it is exact if and only if the
    controllers' sampled trajectory is Markov in themselves, which holds if and only if they are
    an AUTONOMOUS subsystem. Feedback from targets is the knob, and it turns the error on sharply:

        target->controller feedback   0.00    0.05     0.20     0.50     1.00     2.00
        non-Markovianity / tau         0.0     1.0     16.4    106.8    463.5   1787.3

    On circuit.py, where protein feeds back onto the kinase, the same statistic runs 288 to 2,273
    times tau. So a closed loop -- the thing that makes a cell a cell -- is exactly what breaks
    the temporal factorisation.

E3  THE BLOCK CANNOT BE DELETED. Pairwise mutual information between controllers' histories:

        controller coupling    0.00      0.05      0.20      0.80      1.50
        max pair MI / tau       0.0     227.1    3317.9   38917.2  102158.5

    Any nonzero coupling puts it hundreds to hundreds of thousands of times above the tail-legal
    threshold. Real controller subnetworks are dense -- mean degree 9.5 inside TRRUST's top 76 --
    so this route is closed.

E4  WHAT THE CONTRACTION ACTUALLY BUYS. Eliminating slice by slice, the separator between
    consecutive time slices is the WHOLE controller slice, because every controller at t has a
    child at t+1. Slice-internal treewidth does not help that separator.

        flat table                    2^(|C|(L+1))
        slice-by-slice contraction    (L+1) . 2^(2|C|)
        sampled                       S conditional solves

    At |C| = 122, L = 8 that is 2^1098 -> 2^244 x 9, a saving of 2^854. LINEAR in L instead of
    exponential -- real and enormous -- and still EXPONENTIAL in the number of controllers.

E5  SAMPLING IS THE ONLY THING THAT BREAKS THE EXPONENTIAL.

        strata     mode      signed error in Var(targets ON)
           512     exact     6.6e-16
             1     trunc    -1.7e-01     <- the baseline that must fail, and does
             8    sample    -1.4e-02 +- 3.4e-02
            32    sample     3.3e-04 +- 1.7e-02
           128    sample     3.2e-03 +- 6.1e-03
           512    sample     8.7e-04 +- 4.9e-03
            32     trunc    -3.8e-02

    Importance-weighted sampling beats greedy truncation at equal strata count by two orders, and
    a few hundred samples hold ~1%. The cost is independent of |C| and of L.

    BUT THE PER-SAMPLE COST IS NOT ONE, and saying so is part of the result. Each sampled stratum
    still needs the conditional target distribution given that history, lifted here from the exact
    block because on a system this small the exact block exists. A deployed engine needs a
    conditional solve per sample. The honest claim is S CONDITIONAL SOLVES in place of
    2^(|C|(L+1)) TABLE ENTRIES: the exponential in |C| is genuinely broken, the constant is not
    free, and this module does not measure it.

E7  THE CAP, STATED AS A CAP. Against a budget of 1e12 entries, using the measured TRRUST
    controller-subnetwork structure:

        |C|         3      9     15     20     30     76    122
        contracted  6e2   2e6   1e10   1e13   1e19   5e46   3e74
        affordable  yes   yes    yes     no     no     no     no
        edges cov  11.3% 21.9%  28.8%  32.7%  39.7%  57.8%  68.5%

    The contraction moves the cap from |C| = 1 -- which is where trrust_engine left it -- to about
    |C| = 19, roughly a third of the human network's regulatory edges. That is a real gain and it
    is not a solution.

THE HONEST HEADLINE. The fourth obstruction is REDUCED, not removed. Exact contraction buys a
factor of 2^854 in the exponent and takes the affordable controller count from 1 to about 19.
Beyond that only sampling works, and sampling converts an exponential cost into a variance whose
per-sample constant this module has not priced. And the temporal factorisation -- the prong that
would have made the whole thing linear -- is exact only for controllers with no feedback from what
they control, which is to say only for systems that are not closed loops.

=================================================================================================
RETRACTION: "THE CAP MOVES FROM |C| = 1 TO ABOUT 19" IS FALSE, AND THE BLOCK WAS THE WRONG TARGET
=================================================================================================
The section above reports that contracting the controller block moves the affordable controller
count from 1 to about 19, covering roughly a third of TRRUST's regulatory edges. That is withdrawn.

WHAT WENT WRONG. E7's affordability column compared the BLOCK term alone, (L+1)*2^(2|C|), against
the 1e12 budget. The engine's cost is per-gene PLUS block, and the per-gene term -- 2,861 tables
of 2^(1+|pa_i|+k_i(L+1)) -- was priced at zero in that comparison. It is not small:

    per-gene alone, L = 8:   |C|=1  4.56e6      |C|=3  3.27e11     |C|=4  5.21e13  OVER BUDGET
                             |C|=2  7.17e8                         |C|=19 7.14e44

    The real cap at L = 8 is |C| = 3, not 19.

AND THE CEILING GATE, WHICH IS WORSE AND SHOULD HAVE RUN FIRST. Price the block at ZERO and ask
whether the cap moves. That is one line and it bounds what any block work can ever buy:

    L                0     1     2     3     4     6     8
    flat block      39    19    12     8     6     4     3
    contracted      19    19    13     8     6     4     3
    block FREE      53    22    13     8     6     4     3

At L >= 3 the FREE column EQUALS the FLAT column. Setting the block cost to literally zero does
not move the cap, so no representation of P(a) whatsoever -- contraction, sampling, tensor-train,
anything -- can help at the history depths the engine actually needs. At L = 0 the contraction is
a REGRESSION, 39 -> 19, because (L+1)2^(2|C|) exceeds the flat table it replaces. The single real
gain is +1 at L = 2.

WHAT THIS MEANS. The fourth obstruction as I named it -- the controller block -- is not the
binding constraint at realistic history depth. What binds is k_i, the number of controllers
regulating ONE gene, through the per-gene term 2^(k_i(L+1)). I attacked the wrong term, and a
one-line ceiling check run before the attack would have said so.

WHAT SURVIVES FROM THE MODULE. The measurements, which were not affected by the cost accounting:
  E2 the temporal DBN factorisation is exact iff the controllers are autonomous; non-Markovianity
     goes 0.0, 1.0, 16.4, 106.8, 463.5, 1787.3 times tau as target-to-controller feedback rises
     from 0 to 2, and runs 288-2273 on circuit.py's closed loop. A closed loop is what breaks it.
  E3 the block cannot be deleted: any nonzero controller coupling puts pairwise history mutual
     information 227 to 102,159 times above the threshold.
  E5 importance-weighted sampling of the strata beats greedy truncation by two orders at equal
     count, and the 1-stratum baseline fails as it must. That is a fact about representing P(a);
     it is now a fact of no consequence for the cap.

FOUR FURTHER DEFECTS CONFIRMED BY THE SAME REVIEW, all mine:
  - E4 and E7 are STRING ARITHMETIC ("2^" + str(2*c)), not executed code. No elimination was run
    to produce them.
  - engine_from_block documents an 'indep' mode and raises ValueError on it; no temporal/DBN mode
    exists at all despite E2 being about one; E6 is predeclared in the docstring and absent from
    main().
  - cost_real's cap=400 is printed as a value: trrust_engine's "cost max 2.58e+120" is the clamp
    2^400, not a cost.
  - the k_i figures "median 1, max 17, 41.7% with k_i >= 2" come from the top-122 by TOTAL degree
    with undirected neighbour counting -- the definition already retracted as defect C4. The
    module's own corrected table prints k max = 42 at |C| = 76.

HOW IT WAS FOUND. Not by a gate. A pre-mortem of five adversarial lenses, run before the module
was written and returning after it was run, recomputed the cost from the TSV and found the missing

=================================================================================================
ATTACKING k_i: THE TERM WAS NEVER THE PROBLEM, THE REPRESENTATION WAS
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_summary.txt
=================================================================================================

K0  THE CEILING GATE, RUN FIRST THIS TIME. block.py skipped it and spent a module on a term whose
    ceiling was zero. Priced at its best case, k_i work buys AT MOST +1 controller at every
    history depth, and truncating to one controller per gene already captures the whole ceiling:

        L                  0     1     2     3     4     6     8
        k_i as measured   39    19    12     8     6     4     3
        k_i FREE (= 0)    39    19    13     9     7     5     4

    Together with block.py's result -- pricing the block at zero moves the cap by nothing at
    L >= 3 -- BOTH terms sit at the wall simultaneously. The cost is exponential in |C| by two
    independent routes and removing either leaves the other. There is no local fix.

THE FIX IS A CHANGE OF FORM, NOT OF TERM. Both terms are exponential because the controller
history is carried as a PATTERN, 2^(L+1) distinguishable values per controller. If a gene's
response depends on that history only through a low-dimensional SUMMARY, both collapse at once:

        representation          per-gene              block
        pattern                 2^(k_i(L+1))          2^(|C|(L+1))
        per-controller count    (L+2)^(k_i)           (L+2)^|C|
        total count             k_i(L+1)+1            |C|(L+1)+1

    resulting cap on TRRUST      L=0    L=1    L=2    L=3    L=4    L=6    L=8
        pattern                   39     19     12      8      6      4      3
        per-controller count      39     25     19     16     15     --     --
        TOTAL COUNT              419    419    419    419    419    419    419

    419 is the search bound, not a limit. Pattern at L=8 caps at |C| = 3, covering 12.8% of
    TRRUST's regulatory edges; the count summary covers 93.3%.

K2  CONTROLLER IDENTITY DOES NOT MATTER -- ONLY HOW MANY, AND FOR HOW LONG. Per-controller count
    does no better than total count at any size tested, and sometimes worse. That is a stronger
    statement than the cost saving, and it is what licenses the collapse.

K3  THE ERROR DEGRADES WITH HISTORY DEPTH AND IS FLAT IN CONTROLLER COUNT, which is exactly the
    right shape, because |C| is what must grow and L is what can stay small:

        L                1      2      3      4         nC              2      3      4
        summary/pattern  1.08   1.29   2.39   3.81      summary/pattern 1.23   1.29   1.27

    At L <= 2 the count summary is within 1.3x of the full pattern's error at every controller
    count tested. At L = 4 it is 3.8x worse and fails the 1% bar. Controller coupling and
    target-to-controller feedback degrade it mildly (1.25 -> 1.95 as feedback goes 0 -> 2).

K7  THE ADVERSARIAL CASE IS TESTED, AND PARTLY INCONCLUSIVE. Generators whose targets read a
    SPECIFIC controller pair -- AND and XOR gates, where "two on" is ambiguous -- should kill a
    count summary. It survives both on the symmetric observable. But that is evidence about the
    OBSERVABLE as much as the summary: Var(total ON) cannot see which controller drove which. A
    target-specific observable was therefore added, and on the XOR system it turned out to be
    UNREADABLE, because its no-conditioning baseline PASSES at -6.7e-3. The baseline check has to
    run per observable rather than once per system; here it did, and it stopped a conclusion.

A DEFECT THAT MADE THE FIRST SUFFICIENCY TEST UNFAILABLE. It replaced each pattern's conditional
by its group average and summed, weighting by that pattern's own probability. That telescopes back
to sum_a w_a m_a -- the exact answer. Every summary scored 1e-15 INCLUDING "no history at all",
because every summary WAS the exact answer by construction. The approximation has to bite in the
PRODUCT over genes, where the engine's does.

THE HONEST HEADLINE. The obstruction was never k_i and never the block. It was carrying controller
history as a PATTERN. Replacing the pattern with a count makes both terms polynomial, lifts the
affordable controller count from 3 to beyond the search bound, and takes edge coverage from 12.8%
to 93.3% -- at the price of about 1.3x the pattern's error, provided the history stays shallow
(L <= 2). Whether a real promoter integrating several transcription factors is count-sufficient is
an empirical question about promoters that these synthetic systems cannot settle.

=================================================================================================
THE COUNT SUMMARY IS REFUTED ON REAL PROMOTER DATA
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_promoter.txt
=================================================================================================

WHAT WAS AT STAKE. summary.py's whole result rested on count-sufficiency: if a gene's response
depends on its regulators only through HOW MANY are active rather than WHICH, both exponential
terms in the engine's cost collapse to polynomial, the affordable controller count goes from 3 to
past the search bound, and TRRUST edge coverage goes from 12.8% to 93.3%. summary.py said plainly
that whether a real promoter is count-sufficient is an empirical question its synthetic systems
could not settle. It is settled now, and the answer is no.

THE DATA. Sharon et al. 2012, Nature Biotechnology 30:521-30, doi 10.1038/nbt.2205, found via
PubMed; NCBI GEO GSE37851, two replicates. 6,016 designed yeast promoters with both replicates,
varying site identity, number, strength, orientation and position INDEPENDENTLY. Within one
sequence context, two-site promoters span 38 distinct factor multisets -- if expression were a
function of site count those 38 would be interchangeable.

THE NOISE FLOOR, MEASURED BEFORE ANY MODEL WAS FITTED. Replicate Pearson r on log2 expression
0.9974; RMS replicate difference 0.165 log2; noise in the averaged response 0.116 log2. Nothing
smaller than that is a result.

THE ANSWER.

    model                          held-out RMSE (log2)      fallback
    context only, no site info                   1.9570          0.6%
    context + site COUNT                         1.8960          2.6%
    context + factor IDENTITY                    1.1641         11.1%

    identity advantage over count: 0.7319 log2 = 6.3 NOISE FLOORS

The count model beats a model that knows nothing about the sites at all by 0.52 floors -- which is
to say, by nothing. Knowing how many binding sites a promoter has is worth almost no more than
knowing only its sequence context. Knowing WHICH factors bind is worth 6.8 floors. A ridge
regression agrees: 1.9408 context-only, 1.9055 with count, 1.1299 with per-factor counts.

It is not an average hiding a subpopulation. Broken down by site count, the identity advantage is
5.65, 6.56 and 8.37 floors at one, two and three sites -- it grows with the number of sites, which
is the direction that matters for the engine.

WHY, AND IT IS OBVIOUS IN HINDSIGHT. Single-site promoters in one context, mean log2 shift against
the context mean: MATALPHA2 -3.79, GAL4 +2.33, ADR1 -1.61, MIG1_2 -1.55, CBF1 -1.46, GCN4 +1.44.
These factors are activators and repressors. Counting them together adds a repressor to an
activator and calls the sum "two sites". No count can survive that, and the synthetic generators
in summary.py could not have revealed it because their regulation was signed uniformly.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT KILL.

  KILLED: count-sufficiency as a general property of promoters, and with it summary.py's headline
  that the cap goes from 3 to past 419 and coverage from 12.8% to 93.3%. That collapse required an
  assumption which is false on measurements.

  NOT KILLED, and worth separating: a SIGNED count -- activators minus repressors, or a count per
  sign class -- was not tested and is the obvious next candidate. It is still polynomial: two or
  three counts instead of one, k_i(L+1)+1 becoming a small product. The refutation here is of the
  UNSIGNED count, which is what summary.py measured.

  NOT TESTED: the temporal half of the claim. The engine's summary is over controller HISTORY --
  how many were active and for how long. This data is steady-state with no time axis, so it speaks
  only to count-versus-identity, not to how much of the trajectory a summary can discard.

TWO DEFECTS OF MINE IN THIS MODULE.
  - V2 was specified as "the baseline must be worse than both site models" and implemented against
    the COUNT model alone. Since the count model is barely better than no site information, V2
    reported FAIL and would have blocked reading the entire module. That conflates "the data
    cannot test this" with "the count is useless". The data test it decisively; it is the count
    that is useless, and that belongs in V3.
  - V5 shows adding position or orientation to identity makes the predictor WORSE, by 0.26 and
    1.83 floors. That is not evidence that position does not matter -- the same paper reports a
    10 bp periodicity. It is the group-mean estimator running out of data: fallback rises from
    11.1% to 15.4% and 34.7% as the groups get finer. Reported with the fallback rates beside it.

WHERE THE LINE IS, AND IT IS NOT A GENTLE SLOPE. Between T3 and T4. The MEAN behaviour of a whole
cell is a cluster job today -- under three hours on ten thousand cores. The conjunctive rare
events this build order has spent itself on -- does any lesion fail to sterilise, does any cell
survive, does a memory element ever flip -- sit 15 to 20 orders of magnitude further out.

WHY THE CONCLUSION SURVIVES ITS OWN ASSUMPTIONS. If the event count is wrong by 10x, T2 and T3
move by 10x and stay feasible while T4 stays impossible by 14 orders instead of 15. If the
compiled SSA rate is wrong by 100x, the same. NO plausible error in any input moves the T3/T4
boundary. That is why a costing built on ranges still gives a firm answer.

THE POINT THIS MAKES ABOUT THE WHOLE BUILD ORDER. Hardware does not close the gap. Going from ten
thousand cores to an exascale machine buys about five orders of magnitude against a shortfall of
fifteen to twenty. What closes it is a change of ALGORITHM -- rare-event sampling, or a
representation whose cost is not exponential in the number of coupled variables. This session has
now tested four such representations. r-balls die on hubs; bounded width has a 6% floor on
hub-mediated dependence; controller history is exact only when controllers have no feedback from
what they control, which is to say only for systems that are not closed loops; and the count
summary that would have made the cost polynomial is refuted on real promoter data, because
activators and repressors cannot be added together.

So the honest position is: the uncompressed whole cell is affordable for means and unaffordable
for tails by a margin no machine will close, every obvious compression has been tried and
measured, and the one that survives contact with data has not been found yet. The signed count is
the nearest untested candidate.


=================================================================================================
QUANTIZATION (TURBOQUANT, arXiv:2504.19874) -- THE CEILING GATE ANSWERS IT IN ONE LINE
=================================================================================================
TurboQuant randomly rotates a vector, which makes its coordinates near-independent and Gaussian,
then applies an optimal scalar quantizer per coordinate, landing within ~2.7x of the distortion
lower bound. It reduces the BITS PER VALUE. Our cost is the NUMBER of values: 2^(k_i(L+1)) and
2^(|C|(L+1)).

So the ceiling gate is arithmetic. At a fixed byte budget, b bits buys log2(64/b) bits of
exponent. At L = 8 one extra controller costs 9 bits of exponent.

    quantization at 2.5 bits supplies                              4.68 bits  =  0.52 controllers
    reach a 1e-13 event in a year on 1e4 cores                     28.2 bits
    exact joint over 100 binary species, in a year                129.6 bits
    go from the affordable |C| = 3 to the network's 122 at L = 8  1071.0 bits

QUANTIZATION BUYS HALF A CONTROLLER. No quantizer can do better, because the problem is
exponential in the number of coupled variables and quantization is a constant factor on the
entries. And the tail tier is bounded by the NUMBER OF TRAJECTORIES rather than by memory, so
quantization does not apply to it at all. This is the same ceiling-gate discipline that block.py
cost an entire module by skipping: price the term at zero first and see whether the cap moves.

WHAT IT IS ACTUALLY GOOD FOR, measured rather than assumed. Every tier that ALREADY FITS gets
more of itself. The sampled-block route from block.py is exactly such a tier -- memory-bound,
cost independent of |C| and L -- and there quantization is worth having: 512 strata of 32 entries
each, 32x fewer bytes per stratum at 4.3e-2 mean tail error, 8x at 8.3e-4. It composes with the
other routes as a final storage layer and never as an alternative to them, because the random
rotation destroys exactly the locality in the gene basis that r-balls, bounded width and
controller history all depend on, and because "which genes are ON" is not a rotation-invariant
observable.

AND ONE METHODOLOGICAL RESULT, which is the more useful half. Q4 was predeclared with a
prediction attached: that quantizing log-probabilities would beat quantizing probabilities,
because a mean-squared-error quantizer spends its bits on the large entries and the tail is not
there. The first run appeared to refute it violently, returning a tail relative error of exactly
1.000 for log space at 2, 3, 4 and 6 bits.

Exactly 1.000 is the signature of a broken measurement rather than a broken method. Two defects,
both in the harness:

    D1  the power-of-two PADDING was quantized as data. 5184 states padded to 8192; in log space
        those zeros became -690.8 against real entries in [-19.2, -4.8], and 63% of the log
        vector's variance then came from coordinates that are not states of the model.

    D2  the DC offset was left in. A Walsh-Hadamard transform maps a constant vector onto a
        single coordinate, which then sits far outside the Gaussian marginal the scalar quantizer
        is optimal for and is clipped. Not specific to log space or to padding -- it was also in
        the sampled-block route, worth 10.5x there at 8 bits.

Fixing them is worth 24x-25x on linear and 211x-1685x on log. With one protocol applied to both
spaces and 16 rotation seeds instead of one, the prediction is still LOST but in the opposite
direction to the first run -- linear has the better median at 4 of 5 widths -- and it is not
resolved in that direction either: largest median gap 3.23x, sign changes with bit width, seed
ranges overlap at every width, and the spread WITHIN one space across seeds reaches 191x, larger
than the effect being ranked.

That is the fifth appearance in this session of a single failure class: AN APPROXIMATION RANKED
AT A POINT RATHER THAN OVER A DISTRIBUTION. The earlier forms were ranking at one system size,
one dt, one observable and one zero crossing. Here the point was one rotation seed. The rule has
now been rediscovered often enough to state without qualification: a number that ranks two
methods is not a result until the spread of the thing it is ranked over has been measured too.

One incidental finding worth keeping. Quantization is the first approximation in this session
whose tail error is NOT orders of magnitude worse than its bulk error -- the tail/bulk ratio
stays inside [0.33, 5.59] at every width and in both spaces. The reason is structural: after
centring, a quantizer perturbs every entry by a comparable RELATIVE amount, while truncation,
thinning and history all discard the smallest entries preferentially, and the smallest entries
ARE the tail. If a compression is ever found that changes the exponent, this is the property to
look for in it.


=================================================================================================
THE SIGNED COUNT: REFUTED AT ITS OWN CEILING, AND THE FAMILY IT SITS IN PRICED PROPERLY
=================================================================================================
promoter.py refuted the count summary on 5,790 designed yeast promoters and named the signed count
-- count activators and repressors separately -- as the nearest untested candidate, on the grounds
that activators and repressors cannot be added together. It has now been tested.

THE CEILING GATE, run standalone before any module was written. Give the signed count a
DELIBERATELY LEAKY sign oracle: per-factor effects fitted on all the data, held-out promoters
included, so the number is an upper bound on what any honest sign-learning scheme could reach.

    plain count      RMSE 1.8960     6.29 noise floors from identity
    SIGNED count     RMSE 1.4451     2.41 floors      closes 61.6% of the gap
    identity         RMSE 1.1641     0

The signed count does not reach identity even when handed the answer. That is a refutation that no
cleverness in estimating signs can overturn, and it was available in one probe before a line of
the module existed.

SO THE MODULE ASKS THE QUESTION THE REFUTATION EXPOSES. The signed count is C = 2 in a
one-parameter family: partition the factors into C classes and count within each. C = 1 is the
plain count, C = n_TF is identity, and the useful object is the whole curve priced against the cap
each C buys. Error is in noise floors from identity, cap and coverage are on TRRUST at L = 8, and
every class map is refitted INSIDE each training fold.

    C        floors from identity      cap |C|      edge coverage
    1  (count)              6.29           419              93.3%
    2  (SIGNED)             2.61           419              93.3%
    4                       2.09           419              93.3%
    8                       1.52            25              36.5%
    16                      1.45            15              28.6%
    64                      0.19            13              26.6%
    identity                0                 3              12.8%

BOTH ENDS OF THE FAMILY FAIL, IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS. Everything cheap enough to keep the
419-controller cap -- C <= 4, the signed count among them -- is two or more noise floors from
identity. Everything accurate enough to be inside one floor has already given back nine tenths of
the coverage it was supposed to buy. There is no C that is both cheap and sufficient.

WHAT SURVIVES IS THE MIDDLE, AND IT IS REAL BUT MODEST. C = 64 is the smallest tested class count
inside one noise floor of identity, and it moves the cap from 3 to 13 controllers and edge
coverage from 12.8% to 26.6%. That is a genuine gain over the exact pattern representation. It is
not the polynomial collapse the total count promised, which was cap 419 and 93.3% coverage, and
which the measurements refute.

THREE CONTROLS, because each of them could have produced this answer spuriously.

  LEAKAGE. Refitting the class map in-fold costs at most 0.0554 log2 = 0.48 noise floors against
  the leaky oracle, at every C. So the classes CAN be learned from training data alone and the
  leaky ceiling was a fair upper bound rather than a fantasy.

  ABSTENTION. The identity model wins partly by refusing to predict: 11.1% of held-out promoters
  have no training group and fall back to the context mean, against 3.1% for the signed count. On
  the 5,146 promoters where NO model abstains, the ordering is unchanged and sharper -- signed
  count 3.28 floors, C = 8 1.90, C = 64 0.12, C = 128 0.01. The result is not an artefact of who
  answered which question.

  AVAILABILITY. A signed count needs a sign per edge. Of TRRUST's 8,403 distinct directed pairs,
  42.3% are annotated Unknown, against 34.8% Activation and 22.8% Repression. On the real network
  the representation is not two classes but three, and the largest of the three is the one that
  carries no information. The annotation, not the method, is the binding limit there.

AND A CORRECTION TO AN EARLIER MODULE, which is the more important half. summary.py's K2 concluded
that the per-controller count does no better than the total count, and read that as evidence that
WHICH controllers are active does not matter. That verdict was built into the generator:
block.py's multi_controller drives every target by prod_c (1 + g*b_c)^(1/nC) with ONE g shared by
all controllers, so the drive is a symmetric function of the count and the total count was
count-sufficient BY CONSTRUCTION. No arrangement of that system could have refuted it.

Rebuilt with a per-controller signed gain, and scoring every summary against the FULL PATTERN of
the same construction rather than against zero -- the pattern is not exact either, and charging a
summary for the harness is not a measurement of the summary:

    variant                                   fraction of the span the total count recovers
    A  all activators, equal strength                                              92.9%
    B  half repressors, equal magnitude                                             5.9%
    C  half repressors, heterogeneous magnitude                                     6.6%

With repressors present the total count recovers almost nothing -- it is barely better than not
conditioning on the history at all. The signed count recovers 78.8% in variant C, repairing 77.3%
of the break, against the 61.6% measured on real promoters. The synthetic number is 16 points more
forgiving than the measurement; they are the same order and the same sign, and the synthetic one
flatters the summary.

So summary.py's K2 is withdrawn as a statement about promoters and kept only as a statement about
that generator. This is why the synthetic verdict and the measured verdict disagreed: they were
not testing the same thing, and the synthetic one could not have failed.

WHERE THIS LEAVES THE STATE-COMPLEXITY PROBLEM. Five representations have now been tested against
it. r-balls die on hubs. Bounded width has a 6% floor on hub-mediated dependence. Controller
history is exact only when controllers have no feedback from what they control. The total count is
refuted on real promoter data. The class count -- of which the signed count is the two-class
member -- is the first one that survives contact with data, and it buys 3 -> 13 controllers rather
than 3 -> 419. That is a real result and it does not change the exponent.


=================================================================================================
POSITION AND ORIENTATION IN A CLASS COUNT: AN EXCHANGE RATE, AND A REPRESENTATION FAULT
=================================================================================================
Position and orientation do not add a dimension to a class count. They MULTIPLY the alphabet: a
count over (factor class x position bin x orientation) has K = C*P*O cells, and the engine's cost
depends only on how many cells a summary distinguishes, never on what makes them distinguishable.
So signed.py's cap curve already prices every possible split and the only open question is
accuracy at fixed K. That makes this an exchange rate: is a position bin worth more than the
factor class it displaces?

    budget K       cap |C| at L=8      edge coverage
    pattern                     3              12.8%
    4                         419              93.3%
    8                          25              36.5%
    16                         15              28.6%
    32-128                     13              26.6%

THE ANSWER IS NO, AND THE WAY IT IS NO IS THE INTERESTING PART. Of 54 spatial splits, 4 beat the
pure-identity split of the same budget and 2 also showed any improvement from the spatial axis
over their own class resolution. Both margins are under one noise floor. And the feature carrying
them is not a position feature at all.

A PREDICTION MADE AND LOST. The paper reports a ~10 bp PERIODIC dependence of expression on site
location, and promoter.py's V5 had tested start//10, which is LOCATION rather than PHASE. The
prediction was that phase -- start mod 10 -- is the feature that carries what V5 missed. Measured:
phase 1.3830 against location 1.3430 in the group mean, and +0.12 floors against +0.77 in a ridge.
Phase is worse in both estimators. The design ladder steps by 7 bp, so nothing finer can be
resolved however real it is in the paper.

AND THE FEATURE THAT WON WAS THE CONTROL. A 7 bp feature was included only to tell grid from DNA,
and it beat every biologically motivated position feature: +0.48 floors against location's -0.01.
Two controls say what it is.

    PERMUTATION. Partition the same 49 start values into 4 bins at random, 24 times, bin sizes
    matched. Median gain -0.06 floors, range [-0.26, +0.18]; the ladder sits at the 100th
    percentile. So it is not merely that any partition of position helps.

    RESTRICTION, the decisive one. Restrict to the 3,184 promoters whose every site lies on the
    main ladder, where the feature is CONSTANT and can contribute nothing. Position gain there:
    +0.01 floors at P = 2, -0.33 at P = 4, -0.96 at P = 8. It vanishes.

So the ladder's win is BETWEEN design sub-libraries and not within one -- design provenance
wearing the costume of a position effect. Without the control it would have been this module's
headline. A second finding of the same kind: orientation contributes EXACTLY 0.0000 at every C,
so a split with O = 2 is a class count wearing twice its budget, and the first run's "best spatial
split" was that artefact rather than a spatial result.

THE REPRESENTATION FAULT, which is what this is actually worth. promoter.py's V5 asserted, without
testing, that position fails because a group-mean estimator cannot exploit it at this sample size
-- finer groups are sparser, so held-out promoters abstain. That is now tested, by running a ridge,
which uses position without partitioning by it:

    group mean, class counts + 4 position bins            -0.01 floors
    ridge,      class counts + one term per position      +0.77 floors
    ridge,      per-factor counts + position bins         +0.54 floors  (1.1568, beating the
                                                                        identity group mean's
                                                                        1.1641 outright)
    and within the main ladder, where the confound is constant:
    group mean, class counts + 4 position bins            -0.33 floors
    ridge,      class counts + one term per position      +0.41 floors

The information is real, it is worth about half a noise floor, and a COUNT CANNOT REACH IT. The
reason is structural rather than statistical: a count summary is a PARTITION, and position enters
as a smooth effect shared across all factors. Partitioning by position destroys exactly the
sharing that makes the effect estimable, and it does so while multiplying the alphabet -- paying
the full cost for information it has just made unusable.

WHAT THAT IMPLIES FOR THE ENGINE, stated as a hypothesis and not a result, because it has not been
run. The positional part should enter as a MULTIPLIER on the conditional rather than as extra
cells in it -- P(x_i | class counts) * g(positions) rather than P(x_i | cells). A multiplier costs
ADDITIVELY in the alphabet where a cell costs multiplicatively, so the same information would be
had for P + C cells instead of P * C. Every measurement here is consistent with that form and none
of them tests it. It is the next thing to build.

WHERE THIS LEAVES THE COUNT FAMILY. signed.py found the class count is the first representation to
survive contact with data, worth cap 3 -> 13. This module asked whether spatial information could
push it further and the answer is that it cannot, not because the information is absent -- it is
worth half a floor and a ridge extracts it -- but because a count is the wrong functional form for
it. The cap stays at 13.


=================================================================================================
THE MULTIPLIER FORM: THE HYPOTHESIS poscount LEFT, TESTED ON BOTH HALVES
=================================================================================================
poscount.py ended with a form recorded as untested: since a count is a PARTITION and position
enters as a smooth effect shared across factors, the positional part should be a MULTIPLIER on the
conditional rather than extra cells in it.

    P(x | class counts) * g(positions)      instead of      P(x | cells)

C + P parameters instead of C * P table entries. It has now been built and measured.

THE COST CLAIM IS VERIFIED RATHER THAN ASSUMED, by charging the shared parameters against the same
bar and checking the cap does not move. It does not, for any C tested.

    form                                    alphabet     cap |C| at L=8
    pattern (exact)                       2^(k(L+1))                  3
    CELL form, C = 8 x P = 4                      32                 13
    MULTIPLIER, C = 8, g over P = 4                8                 25

ON REAL PROMOTERS IT WORKS, AND THE CONTRAST WITH THE CELL FORM IS THE RESULT.

    model                                        RMSE   vs pure C=8   fallback   alphabet
    pure class count, C = 8                    1.3419         0.00       3.9%           8
    CELL form, C = 8 x P = 2                   1.3380        +0.03       4.4%          16
    CELL form, C = 8 x P = 8                   1.3622        -0.17       8.4%          64
    MULTIPLIER, g over 8 position bins         1.3183        +0.20       3.9%           8
    MULTIPLIER, g over 49 position bins        1.2660        +0.65       3.9%           8

The multiplier gains +0.65 noise floors where the cell form's best is +0.03 and its error gets
WORSE as P grows. And the abstention column is the structural claim made falsifiable: the
multiplier never splits a group, so its fallback rate is IDENTICAL to the pure class count's at
every P -- 3.9%, to the digit -- while the cell form climbs to 8.4%. It also reduces to the class
count to machine precision when the shared term is switched off, difference 0.000e+00 exactly.

Inside the single main design ladder, where poscount.py showed the apparent position win was
design provenance between sub-libraries, the multiplier gains +0.08 and +0.15 floors where the
cell form loses 0.33. Small, under one floor, and the right sign in the place where the confound
cannot produce it.

THE ENGINE HALF, AND A GATE THIS MODULE HAD TO FAIL ITSELF ON. The analogy proposed was that
position is to a promoter what SLICE TIME is to a controller history: both axes a count aggregates
over, both a smooth modulation shared across the things counted. M5 was predeclared as "the
temporal multiplier must repair a substantial part of the count's break", and as written it fails
at 0.0% to 1.8%.

The bar was mis-specified, and the module's own table says so. The per-slice CELL form holds the
ENTIRE temporal alphabet -- 5,120 parameters, the most a temporal representation can possibly
know -- and repairs only 3.8%. Swept over dt from 0.05 to 16 it never exceeds 8.4%. So more than
nine tenths of the count's break in this system is about WHICH controller and not WHEN, which is
exactly what signed.py measured, and no temporal form of any kind could have cleared that bar.
That is ledger P, a bar unreachable on any evidence, and it is the same error class as
poscount.py's D2: something in the denominator that the thing measured was never responsible for.

Measuring the denominator instead of assuming it -- the cell form's repair IS the temporal share:

    dt      temporal share of the break      multiplier's share of THAT
    0.05                     0.2 - 0.4%      not quoted, the denominator is noise
    0.25                     1.5 - 3.8%      not quoted
    1.0                      6.0 - 7.7%                          69.6 - 74.8%
    4.0 and 16.0             7.4 - 8.4%                          73.8 - 76.5%

Where the temporal axis is live the multiplier captures about three quarters of what the full
temporal alphabet captures, for 5 shared parameters against 5,040.

AND THE COMPOSITION BOTH RESULTS IMPLY, measured because the decomposition above made the need for
it visible. signed.py fixes WHICH, the multiplier fixes three quarters of WHEN, and the axes are
independent -- so the form is a signed count TIMES a shared temporal multiplier, at alphabet
C + (L+1) rather than C * (L+1). At dt = 1.0, where the temporal axis is live:

    L    WHICH contributes    WHEN contributes    total of the count's break repaired
    2                36.8%                4.1%                                  40.9%
    4                21.8%                5.6%                                  27.4%

Both halves contribute what their own measurements said they would, and they ADD in the alphabet
rather than multiplying in it. That is the whole claim and it holds.

WHAT IT IS WORTH, STATED AT ITS SIZE. This is an EFFICIENCY result, not a scaling result. It buys
the same information for C + P instead of C * P, and the axis it addresses carries 8.4% of the
engine's break and about half a noise floor on promoters. Running total across three
representation changes, all measured against real data or an exact joint:

    representation                 cap |C| at L=8      TRRUST edge coverage
    pattern (exact)                             3                     12.8%
    class count (signed.py)                    13                     26.6%
    class count x multiplier                   25                     36.5%

Three controllers to twenty-five, and 12.8% of the network's regulatory edges to 36.5%. That is
real and it is arithmetic on the same cap function throughout. It is still a constant factor on an
exponential, and the whole-cell tail tier remains 15 to 20 orders out of reach.


=================================================================================================
RULING OUT INSTEAD OF ENUMERATING: THE FIRST ATTACK ON STRATA RATHER THAN ON TABLE SIZE
=================================================================================================
Every representation change in this session -- class count, signed count, multiplier -- shrank the
TABLE the engine stores. None touched the number of STRATA it must enumerate, 2^(|C|(L+1)), which
block_joint still builds in full and prunes only exact zeros. This is a different axis.

TWO WAYS TO REDUCE IT, AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME KIND OF THING. block.py's E5 SAMPLED the block:
32 of 512 strata, 3.3e-4 error. But a sample is an estimate with a variance and certifies nothing.
Ruling a branch out with an admissible bound is stronger, and here the bound is free and exact:
block_joint extends a prefix by a stochastic matrix and then masks, so no descendant can carry
more mass than its prefix, and any conjunctive observable is bounded by mass. Cutting a prefix
therefore costs at most its mass -- a number the pruner accumulates as it goes, without seeing the
answer. That is a certificate.

THE CEILING GATE SAID NO AT THE BUILD ORDER'S OWN OPERATING POINT. An oracle that keeps exactly
the top-contributing strata needs 3,059 of 4,096 for 99% of the mass and 2,229 for 99% of the
tail -- 54.4% -- and every prefix node sits above mass 1e-12. The distribution is flat there.
There is nothing to rule out, and a constant FRACTION means a constant factor.

SO THE QUESTION BECAME WHAT MAKES IT FLAT, AND THE ANSWER IS PHYSICS. Sweeping the window depth L
at fixed window length, the nominal count is 4^(L+1) and the kept count is not:

    switches per window   keep99 at L=7   keep99 at L=8   step ratio     regime
                  0.152              91             111        1.220   polynomial
                  0.305             235             301        1.281   polynomial
                  0.609             919           1,346        1.465   transition
                  0.914           2,079           3,417        1.644   transition
                  1.523           6,167          11,887        1.928   transition
                  2.132          12,039          26,448        2.197   exponential
                  3.046          22,495          57,623        2.562   exponential
                  4.569          38,008         115,909        3.050   exponential

A quadratic predicts a step ratio of 1.306 and the nominal count gives 4.000. Below about 0.3
controller switches per window the kept count is POLYNOMIAL in L while the nominal count is
exponential. Above about 2 it is exponential with a smaller base, which is a constant factor.
Monotone over a thirtyfold range with no exceptions.

A PREDICTION MADE AND LOST ON THE WAY THERE. The mechanism was predeclared as switches per
SAMPLING INTERVAL, tested as a collapse, and refuted at R2 = 0.75: switches/slice = 0.1523 occurs
twice with kept fractions of 0.0726 and 0.4531, a sixfold spread at one value of the supposed
controlling parameter. The corrected parameter is switches per WINDOW, and the discriminating fact
was already inside the sweep -- each sweep holds the window fixed, so switches per window is
constant while switches per slice falls fourfold, and the regime never changes inside a sweep,
only between them. The physical reading is that the amount of genuinely distinct history in a
window is set by how often the controllers switch during it; sampling more finely adds slices that
carry no new information. A units check confirming that only rate x time matters is reported
beside it and LABELLED as a units check, because at fixed L the two candidate parameters are
proportional and it discriminates nothing.

THE ORACLE IS NOT AN ALGORITHM, AND THE CERTIFICATE HOLDS. A real pruner expands a prefix tree and
cuts on the bound without seeing the answer. On a peaked system with 16,384 strata:

        tau   nodes touched   strata kept    rel err   certificate   held?
      1e-03             580            52   4.62e-02      2.40e-01     yes
      1e-04           1,720           221   1.25e-02      7.18e-02     yes
      1e-06           5,300         1,196   2.56e-04      1.29e-03     yes
      1e-09          14,952         6,239   4.03e-07      2.36e-06     yes
          0          21,844        16,384   1.28e-15             0     yes

The certificate held at every threshold. At 1e-6 the pruner touches 24% of the tree, keeps 7.3% of
the strata, and is right to 2.6e-4 -- with a bound of 1.3e-3 it computed itself.

AND THE MASS BOUND DOES NOT LOSE THE TAIL HERE, which had to be checked because six modules in
this session found bulk and tail diverging. Keeping the top-k strata by MASS retains slightly MORE
of the tail than of the mass at every k. The strata carrying the conjunctive tail are the same
heavy ones carrying the mass, which is a negative for the usual worry and is exactly what makes a
mass bound usable as a tail certificate at all. It is a property of this system and has to be
rechecked wherever the method is used.

SAMPLING VERSUS RULING OUT, AT EQUAL BUDGET, AND THE PREDECLARED CROSSOVER IS CONFIRMED.

    system                     budget   PRUNE err   certified   SAMPLE err (8 seeds)
    flat   (0.76 sw/window)       128    4.40e-01         yes   7.27e-02 +- 7.2e-02
    peaked (0.05 sw/window)       128    2.78e-02         yes   7.78e-02 +- 6.5e-02

Sampling wins where the distribution is flat, pruning wins where it is peaked, and the two columns
are not the same kind of number: a pruner's error comes with a bound it computed itself, a
sampler's with a spread over seeds and no bound at all.

THE COMBINED FORM IS BETTER THAN EITHER HALF BY ABOUT TENFOLD. Prune with the certificate, then
sample the residue -- whose mass is exactly what the certificate bounded, so the sample is
unbiased for the part that was cut and the bound still holds if the sample is bad.

        tau   strata kept   prune only   + residue sampled   certificate
      1e-03            52     4.62e-02   4.66e-03 +- 2.6e-03    2.40e-01
      1e-04           221     1.25e-02   1.67e-03 +- 1.1e-03    7.18e-02
      1e-06         1,196     2.56e-04   2.92e-05 +- 1.3e-05    1.29e-03

WHAT THIS IS AND IS NOT. It is the first result in this session that changes the SHAPE of a cost
curve rather than its constant -- but along ONE axis only. Sweeping the controller count instead
of the window depth gives kept ~ nominal^0.96, so pruning does not touch the WIDTH explosion at
all. The whole-cell problem is a width problem: 122 controllers, and a cap that three
representation changes moved from 3 to 25. Pruning does not move it further.

What it does buy is that the tail-accuracy refinement history.py demanded -- small dt, where tail
error goes as dt^1.753 -- was previously multiplied by 2^(|C| W / dt) and is now nearly free
whenever the regulators switch slowly relative to the window. Which regime real transcription
factors sit in is an empirical question about their kinetics that this does not answer, and it is
the next thing worth measuring.


=================================================================================================
REAL TF KINETICS AGAINST THE PRUNABLE REGIME, AND THE ASSEMBLY RUN
=================================================================================================
prune.py's transition is dimensionless -- polynomial at or below 0.30 controller switches per
window, exponential at or above 2.13 -- so it can be tested against measurements rather than
argued about. From PubMed, carried as RANGES because absolute residence times are known to depend
on imaging acquisition parameters through sampling bias (Presman 2017, doi:10.1016/j.ymeth.2017.03.014;
Paakinaho 2017, doi:10.1038/ncomms15896):

    Chen et al. 2014, Cell 156:1274-1285, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2014.01.062 -- Sox2/Oct4 in mouse ES
    cells: specific target dwell 12.0-14.6 s, reached after 84-97 sampling events of 3.3-3.7 s
    diffusion and 0.75-0.9 s nonspecific collisions.
    Gebhardt et al. 2013, doi:10.1038/nmeth.2411 -- GR and ER-alpha residence times.
    Loffreda et al. 2017, doi:10.1038/s41467-017-00398-7 -- p53 residence is MODULATED by
    acetylation, so it is not a constant of the protein.
    Schwanhausser et al. 2011, doi:10.1038/nature10098 -- mRNA and protein half-lives measured
    together for >5,000 genes, and UNCORRELATED with each other.

THE ANSWER DEPENDS ENTIRELY ON WHAT A CONTROLLER IS, AND THE TWO NATURAL CHOICES SIT ON OPPOSITE
SIDES OF THE TRANSITION BY FOUR ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE.

  BINDING LEVEL -- a controller is a factor occupying its site. Off-rate 1/13.3 s = 0.075 per
  second. Prunability then needs a window under 4.4 SECONDS. The window the engine needs is set by
  how long a target's mRNA remembers its input, which is hours. FAILS by about 7,400x.

  ACTIVITY LEVEL -- a controller is a factor being present and active, changing on its turnover
  timescale. The group collapses to

      switches per window = ln2 * (target mRNA lifetime) / (TF protein half-life)

  and the criterion becomes a statement about a ratio: PRUNABLE WHEN A TF'S PROTEIN OUTLIVES ITS
  TARGET'S mRNA BY 2.31x OR MORE. That is a criterion and not a verdict -- Schwanhausser found the
  two distributions uncorrelated, so it is a per-edge question and a median of medians would not
  settle it.

WHICH MAKES THE LOAD-BEARING ASSUMPTION A COARSE-GRAINING, NOT A NUMBER, AND IT WAS TESTED. May
the fast binding be averaged away? A two-timescale controller -- slow activity bit, fast binding
bit gated by it, targets reading the BOUND state -- against the activity-only model, sweeping the
separation, required to reproduce the TAIL and not merely the mean.

AND THE FIRST VERSION OF THAT TEST WAS WRONG IN A WAY WORTH KEEPING. The target drive is
exp(sum s m b / nC), which is NONLINEAR in the binding state. Substituting the mean OCCUPANCY
inside it does not converge:

    separation        1       10      100     1000     3000
    err, occupancy  20.0%   26.2%   27.5%   27.7%    27.7%      <- WORSE as timescales separate
    err, drive       5.99%   1.16%   0.11%   0.011%   0.0037%   <- converges as 1/separation

That is not slow convergence, it is convergence to the WRONG LIMIT. Timescale separation makes a
fast variable EQUILIBRATE, it does not make it deterministic, and E[exp(X)] is not exp(E[X])
however fast X is. The correct quasi-steady state averages the DRIVE over the fast variable's
conditional law, not the occupancy. A whole-cell model built the other way would have been wrong
by 28% on every conjunctive tail, permanently, with no amount of separation revealing it.

With the correct averaging the coarse-graining reaches 1% on the tail at a separation of 30. The
real separation -- binding off-rate 0.075/s over TF turnover at a 46 h half-life -- is 1.8e4, a
margin of about 600x. SO THE TEST PASSES, conditionally, and the condition is one line.

THE ASSEMBLY, RUN. Controller block wired by TRRUST (8,403 distinct pairs, controllers by
out-degree: SP1, NFKB1, RELA, TP53, JUN, STAT3, E2F1, SP3 ...), real signs, activity-level rates;
60 real target genes with their real regulators, fixed across every row; strata pruned as PATHS
with an exact certificate; targets carried by the signed class count times the shared temporal
multiplier. One simplification found on the way: in the controller block the state IS the code, so
a stratum is a single path and its mass is the path probability -- enumerating strata is literally
Markov-chain path enumeration and the prefix bound is the path probability.

    |C|  sw/window     regime   nodes touched  % of full   certificate   vs exact
      4       0.20  polynomial         39,200     56.08%      7.45e-04   5.46e-04
      5       0.20  polynomial        381,472     35.24%      7.48e-04   4.27e-04
      6       0.20  polynomial      3,800,640     22.30%      3.00e-03   budget missed
      8       0.20  polynomial     27,252,224      0.63%      1.63e-01   budget missed
      4       1.05  transition         68,592     98.12%      4.97e-04   4.37e-04
      5       1.05  transition      1,026,080     94.80%      7.33e-03   budget missed

At a 1e-3 budget the pruner holds to |C| = 5 in the prunable window and |C| = 4 in the transition
band. The regime is worth about 117x in certificate at |C| = 6 -- 22.3% of the tree against 90.6%
-- which is the demonstration. Where exact enumeration is still affordable the pruned answer is
checked against it and agrees to the fourth decimal.

THREE DEFECTS IN THIS GATE'S OWN FIRST RUNS, ALL RECORDED. A fixed absolute threshold does not
scale, because path probabilities fall like n^-L: tau = 1e-6 was gentle at |C| = 4 and produced a
certificate of 0.92 at |C| = 8, meaning the pruner had silently dropped 92% of the mass. The
target set moved with |C|, so tails at different widths were different observables. And the
retained set grows like n^L whatever the budget says, which exhausted memory until a path cap
turned it into a reportable row instead of a crash.

WHAT BREAKS FIRST, AND IT IS NOT WHAT THE PRUNER FIXES. The pruner touches 2^|C| children per
surviving node, so its cost carries 2^|C| however well it prunes. Pruning removes the exponential
in WINDOW DEPTH and leaves the exponential in WIDTH untouched -- prune.py's own nC sweep already
said so (kept ~ nominal^0.96) and the node column here confirms it. So the binding constraint is
unchanged: 25 controllers and 36.5% of TRRUST's regulatory edges, from the representation work.
Pruning does not move it. What it buys is that the window may now be refined for tail accuracy --
which goes as dt^1.753 -- without paying 2^(|C| W / dt) for it.

SO THE HONEST SUMMARY OF THE WHOLE ARC. Real transcription factors DO sit in the prunable regime,
but only when the engine models them at the ACTIVITY level, and only because a coarse-graining
that must average the drive rather than the occupancy is legitimate by a 600x margin. The engine
assembles, runs on real network wiring, and returns certified answers. It does not scale to a
whole cell, and the reason is the same width exponential that four representation changes and one
pruner have now all failed to remove.

THE WIDTH EXPONENTIAL: IT IS ONE GENE, AND THE CEILING GATE'S VERDICT IS NOT PERMANENT
=================================================================================================
Four representation changes and one pruner all left the same exponential in WIDTH standing. This
attacks it, and the first thing the attack did was refute its own premise.

THE CEILING GATE, RUN FIRST, KILLED THE OBVIOUS TARGET. Under the class-count representation at
C = 64, L = 8, bar 1e12, pricing the controller block at ZERO moves the cap by NOTHING -- 13 with
the class-count block, 13 factored, 13 free. At |C| = 13 the per-gene term is 2.96e11 against a
bar of 1e12 while the block is 6.86e10. Third application of block.py's rule and the first time it
has stopped an attack BEFORE the module was built rather than after one was spent.

The factorisation that would have been built was measured anyway so nobody builds it: the
controller-controller subgraph is sparse (561 edges among the top 122 factors, density 0.042) and
its min-degree elimination width grows sublinearly -- 2, 5, 9, 16, 25, 35, 46, 59, 63 at
|C| = 5 to 420 -- giving 2^36 in place of 2^122, a factor of 7.7e25. Correct, huge, and at that
moment useless.

TWO DEFECTS IN THE PROBES THAT GOT THERE. bounded_elimination returns DROPPED EDGES, not a success
flag, so a first pass "measured" width 2 on a graph of mean degree 10 by silently sacrificing 74%
of its edges; the true width needs an unbounded min-degree pass. And an unconstrained greedy
alignment of the class map reached the theoretical minimum cost with mean occupied classes exactly
1.00 -- by putting every controller in ONE class, which is the plain count that promoter data
refutes at 6.29 noise floors. A cost optimiser with no accuracy constraint always rediscovers the
cheapest refuted representation. Ledger U, in this module's own probe.

THEN THE MEASUREMENT THAT SHOULD HAVE COME FIRST. The per-gene cost is not a sum, it is one gene:

    |C|    max k_i    worst gene cost      total      worst/total   worst gene

From |C| = 20 upward ONE GENE is the entire per-gene cost. CDKN1A, with 52 regulators among the
top 122 transcription factors. The binding term is a HUB -- the same object that killed r-balls
(SP1's 3-ball reaches 2,724 of 2,861 genes) and left bounded width with a 6% floor. Third
appearance, third form. No representation-level fix could ever have worked, which is why the
alignment attack built before this measurement made things WORSE by up to 28x. Its specification
error is recorded too: with C = 64 classes and |C| = 13 controllers the balance cap is 1, so every
controller is FORCED into its own class -- the worst case, not a balanced one.

SO THE ATTACK THAT WORKS IS ON THE GENES, NOT THE REPRESENTATION. Carry the top-m most-regulated

AND THAT FLIPS THE CEILING GATE'S OWN VERDICT, which is the most useful thing here.

At zero demotions the block representation is worth nothing and W0 was right to refuse it. At 100

CEILING GATE PRICES A TERM AGAINST THE CURRENT BINDING CONSTRAINT. Its verdict is correct at the

That is a rule this build order did not previously have, and it was earned by nearly discarding a

BLOCK is worse than the plain pattern at these widths -- one controller per class costs 10 against
the pattern's 2 -- which never mattered while the block was not binding.

THE ACCURACY TRADE, STATED RATHER THAN BURIED. The 100 demoted genes drop from C = 64 (0.19 noise

none on the other 96.5% -- a different KIND of trade from every earlier one, which all degraded

Demoting the hubs buys the cap by giving up on precisely the genes the cap was wanted for, and
that is the result rather than a caveat on it.

Four auditors were run over this ledger and the modules behind it, each through one lens -- cost,
accuracy, biology, defect-record -- and told to separate what has been MEASURED from what has been
ASSERTED. They found five things, all verified independently before being accepted, and one of
their claims was checked and found wrong.

arc is fitted and scored against y = (l1+l2)/2, whose noise is std(l1-l2)/2 = 0.0823. Verified
empirically: std(l1 - y) = 0.0823. signed.py, poscount.py and multiplier.py each recomputed the
same wrong divisor. promoter.py also printed the two figures with their LABELS SWAPPED.

WHAT THIS EPISODE IS WORTH, BEYOND THE FIVE FIXES. Every one of these defects sat inside a module
whose own predeclared gates all PASSED, and four of the five were in work reported as successful
within the last few hours. That is the same lesson twice already recorded here -- adversarial
review catches what a module's own gates cannot -- but with a sharper edge: the gates were not
weak, they were answering the questions their author thought to ask. An auditor reading only the
record, with no stake in it, asked different ones. The rule that follows is that a module's gates
verify its INTERNAL consistency and cannot certify the quantity it reports; that needs a reader
who did not write it.


=================================================================================================
THE WEAKEST LINK, TESTED: DOES THE YEAST RESULT TRANSFER TO HUMAN CELLS?
=================================================================================================
Every accuracy figure in this build order comes from one experiment -- Sharon et al. 2012, ~5,800
designed yeast promoters -- and has been applied throughout to a human network. Four modules flag
that transfer as the weakest link and none of them tests it. This does.

THE MATCHED HUMAN QUESTION is not another reporter assay. It is the same question asked of real
regulatory relationships: for a gene with several known regulators, does it matter WHICH one is
perturbed? ENCODE CRISPRi RNA-seq in K562 answers it -- one factor silenced per experiment, whole
transcriptome, two biological replicates. Restricted to TRRUST regulators that is 48 factors
cached, 153 expressed target genes with two or more of them, 400 (gene, regulator) pairs measured
twice each. One assay in one cell line, deliberately: mixing shRNA, CRISPR and CRISPRi would
confound the comparison being made. No control experiment is needed, because centring each gene
across the panel removes exactly the per-gene constant a control supplies and that constant
cancels anyway in a comparison between regulators of the same gene.

TWO GATES OF THIS MODULE'S OWN WERE MIS-SPECIFIED, AND FINDING THAT OUT WAS THE WHOLE WORK.

H2 failed and COULD NOT HAVE PASSED. The centring that makes the test possible also removes each
gene's own level, so the "knows the gene" baseline was guaranteed to sit near the "knows nothing"
one. Ledger P, built into the preprocessing rather than the threshold. The question it should have
asked -- is there reproducible signal at all -- answers yes: the replicates correlate at r = 0.63.

H3's instrument was too blunt for the design. A held-out comparison of a regulator-blind model
against an identity model conflates signal with PREDICTOR NOISE: the blind model predicts from the
mean of K ~ 2.6 noisy measurements while identity predicts from one, so it is quieter by about
sqrt(K), and with K this small that cancels most of identity's advantage in signal. Taken at face
value it reads 0.11 floors and says the regulators are interchangeable. The yeast experiment never
suffered this because its groups were large.

THE RIGHT INSTRUMENT IS A VARIANCE COMPONENT -- within a grouping unit, how much response variance
is attributable to which regulator, after subtracting the replicate noise the averaging leaves --
bootstrapped over the grouping unit, and applied to BOTH organisms with the same estimator:

    organism   grouping unit       floor    effect sd      in floors        95% CI
    HUMAN      target gene        0.2200       0.2302           1.05    [0.90, 1.18]
    YEAST      sequence context   0.0823       1.3049          15.86    [9.32, 21.36]

    ratio yeast/human: 5.67x in absolute log2, 15.15x in floors

THE ANSWER IS IN TWO PARTS, WHICH IS WHY NO SINGLE GATE COULD HAVE CARRIED IT.

QUALITATIVELY IT TRANSFERS. A human gene's regulators are demonstrably NOT interchangeable: the
between-regulator effect excludes zero with a comfortable margin, and it is LARGER than the
variance between genes (0.0530 against 0.0378). Every count-like summary in this build order
assumes the opposite, and that assumption is refuted on human data as it was on yeast. The main
confound runs the same way -- a knockdown carries indirect effects that are shared across a gene's
regulators, which biases this measurement TOWARD interchangeability -- so the true effect is more
likely larger than measured than smaller.

QUANTITATIVELY IT DOES NOT. The effect is 15 times smaller relative to measurement noise than in
yeast and 5.7 times smaller in absolute log2. Because the entire regulator-identity effect spans
about ONE noise floor here, the yeast bar -- "within one floor of identity" -- is satisfied by
almost any class resolution, C = 1 included, and the class ladder is flat across its whole range
where yeast's separated its ends by 8.6 floors.

SO THE SPECIFIC NUMBER EVERY CAP DEPENDS ON DOES NOT SURVIVE THE TRANSFER. signed.py's C = 64 of
404 -- 15.8% of the alphabet -- is what makes a cap meaningful, because a cap is only a cap at a
stated accuracy. That figure is not reproduced on human data and is not currently testable there:
this panel cannot distinguish class resolutions at all. The caps are not thereby WRONG. Their
status is now precise and worse than it was assumed to be: 3, 13 and 140 are cost arithmetic
carried out at an accuracy standard measured in yeast and ASSUMED for human. Where the ledger has
been reporting them as measured results at a stated accuracy, that stated accuracy is an
extrapolation across a kingdom.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT. Not more of this panel -- the limit is that a single CRISPRi knockdown moves
an individual target by about a quarter of a log2 against a noise floor of the same size. It needs
either many more replicates per factor, or a human assay with the yeast experiment's actual
structure: designed combinations of sites in a fixed context, where the number and identity of
regulators are varied independently and the response is measured with a tight floor. Human MPRAs
of that design exist; none was used here, and that is the next measurement rather than a caveat.


=================================================================================================
LOOKING FOR A HUMAN MPRA WITH THE YEAST DESIGN: WHAT WAS FOUND, AND WHAT IT SAYS
=================================================================================================
humantransfer.py closed by naming what was needed: a human assay with the yeast experiment's
structure -- designed site combinations in a fixed context, number and identity varied
independently, tight floor. This went to find one.

THE RIGHT DATASET EXISTS AND COULD NOT BE READ. Smith et al. 2013, Nat Genet 45:1021-1028,
doi:10.1038/ng.2713, varied "copy number, spacing, combination and order" of 12 liver
transcription-factor sites across ~5,000 synthetic elements in HepG2 -- the Sharon design in human
cells, exactly. Its processed data is not machine-readable from this environment: the article is
not in the PMC open-access subset, the supplementary tables sit behind an interstitial that
defeats both automated retrieval and a real browser driven through the proxy, and the raw reads at
SRA SRP018414 cannot be decoded without the barcode map that lives in those same supplements.
THE COPY-NUMBER AXIS THEREFORE REMAINS UNTESTED ON HUMAN DATA. That is a gap, not a footnote.

WHAT IS OPEN ANSWERS THE OTHER HALF. Kheradpour et al. 2013, Genome Res 23:800-11,
doi:10.1101/gr.144899.112, GEO GSE33367: designed human enhancer constructs in K562 and HepG2 with
selected motif instances directly disrupted -- activators and repressors in both cell types -- ten
barcodes per construct, two biological replicates, matched plasmid input. That FIXES THE COUNT AT
ONE and varies the IDENTITY, which is precisely what a count summary assumes away, and it does so
with a floor far tighter than the CRISPRi panel's. Identity is recovered from the data rather than
looked up: constructs sharing both flanks are one enhancer family, the wild type is the member
minimising total Hamming distance, the disrupted variant differs in a contiguous block, and the
wild-type sequence in that block IS the motif instance. 196 disruptions were recovered.

THREE DEFECTS OF THIS MODULE'S OWN, ALL FOUND AND FIXED BEFORE THE RESULT WAS READ.
  Single linkage is transitive and chained 181 of 196 disruptions into ONE class, leaving exactly
  one group with two or more members -- so the bootstrap resampled a single unit, produced a
  zero-width interval, and the "between-motif" variance was the variance WITHIN one giant class.
  Ledger U. Complete linkage, which cannot chain, gives 136 classes of which 34 have two or more.
  The similarity function accepted a six-base overlap, which can score 1.0 by chance; it now
  requires nine. And M5 asserted its principle while computing nothing -- a dead loop.

THE RESULT, AND IT DOES NOT REPLICATE.

    instrument                                    HepG2                      K562
    M3 motif similarity vs effect similarity   z = -0.47, p = 0.31    z = -5.03, p = 0.0005
    M4 between-motif variance component        1.58 floors [0.91, 2.10]  0.77 floors [0.00, 1.21]

The two instruments point at DIFFERENT cell types. M3 finds the identity effect in K562 and not
HepG2; M4's variance component excludes zero in HepG2 and not K562. M5 was predeclared as "a

instrument. THE COUNT-MATCHED IDENTITY EFFECT IS NOT ESTABLISHED BY THIS MODULE.

AND THE INCIDENTAL FINDING IS THE STRONGEST THING HERE, AND IT WAS NOT PREDECLARED.

    disruption effects, HepG2 against K562, the SAME 196 constructs :  r = +0.02
    within-cell reproducibility, replicate 1 against replicate 2    :  r = +0.73 (HepG2)
                                                                       r = +0.52 (K562)

The same construct, the same disrupted motif, measured in two human cell lines, gives effects
that are UNCORRELATED -- while each cell line reproduces itself well. The effect of removing a
regulatory site is not a property of the site. It is a property of the site AND the cell.

THAT IS A PROBLEM FOR THE ENGINE THAT IS LARGER THAN THE ONE THIS MODULE SET OUT TO TEST. Every
representation in this build order -- pattern, class count, signed count, multiplier -- carries a
per-gene response function P(x_i | summary of its regulators) and assumes it is a fixed object to
be learned once. If a motif's effect does not transfer between two cell lines of the same
organism, that function is not a property of the network at all, and the accuracy figures the caps
rest on are conditional on a cell type that was never named. The yeast measurement was made in one
organism in one condition; so was every human measurement here; and the cross-cell correlation
above says those conditions do not commute.

WHERE THE ACCURACY EVIDENCE NOW STANDS, ALL THREE EXPERIMENTS ON THE SAME ESTIMATOR:

    experiment                                     effect size    replicates?
    yeast promoters, between factor set            15.86 floors   single condition
    human CRISPRi, between regulator                1.05 floors   single cell line
    human MPRA, between motif at fixed count     0.77-1.58 floors NO -- opposite cells

The qualitative claim that regulators are not interchangeable survives in yeast and in the CRISPRi
panel. At fixed count, in designed human constructs, it does not replicate across cell types. And
the copy-number question -- the one the whole count summary is about -- has still never been asked
of human data.


=================================================================================================
CORRECTION AND SYNTHESIS: WHAT THE CELL-SPECIFICITY RESULT ACTUALLY MEANS FOR THE ENGINE
=================================================================================================
The section above closed by saying that if a motif's effect does not transfer between two cell
lines then the per-gene response function "is not a property of the network at all". THAT WAS TOO
STRONG, and the measurement that shows why was not run before it was written.

AN UNCORRELATED CROSS-CELL RESULT HAS TWO EXPLANATIONS AND THEY MATTER OPPOSITELY.
  (a) the per-gene response function itself differs between cells -- a problem for the engine; or
  (b) the cognate factor's ACTIVITY differs between cells -- which is exactly what the engine
      already models as controller state, and under which an uncorrelated cross-cell result is
      what a CORRECT engine predicts, not evidence against one.
The r = +0.02 alone cannot separate them, and reading it as (a) was an error.

WHAT SEPARATES THEM: within ONE cell type, is the same motif's effect consistent across the
different enhancer contexts it appears in?

    cell     motif classes    between motifs    within a motif    identity explains
    HepG2               34       0.83 floors       1.58 floors                21.5%
    K562                34       0.72 floors       0.77 floors                46.8%

So the answer is (b) PLUS something the arc had not measured. Within a single cell, motif identity
explains only a minority of the reproducible effect -- 21.5% and 46.8% -- and the remainder is
CONTEXT: the same motif behaving differently in different enhancers. The caveat runs one way and
is stated with its direction: classes are recovered by sequence similarity rather than from a
curated database, so any misassignment inflates the within-class term, making the context share an
UPPER bound and the identity share a LOWER one.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT DO TO THE ENGINE.

  THE FORM SURVIVES. The engine computes sum_a P(a) prod_i P(x_i | summary(a)), and those
  conditionals are PER GENE already. Context-dependence is therefore inside the model rather than
  outside it, and cross-cell differences in which factors are active are represented as controller
  states, which is what the controller block is for. Nothing measured here contradicts the
  factorisation, the pruning, the certificate, or the cost arithmetic.

  THE COST ARITHMETIC SURVIVES AS COST. The caps -- 3, 13, 140 controllers, 12.8% to 71.7% of
  TRRUST's edges -- are graph and table arithmetic against a fixed bar. They do not depend on what
  the response function IS, only on how many distinguishable values it must carry.

  THE COMPRESSION IS WHAT IS DAMAGED, AND IT IS DAMAGED AT ITS SHARED ASSUMPTION. Every summary
  tested in this build order -- total count, signed count, class count, class count times temporal
  multiplier -- assumes a gene's response depends on its regulators through classes that are
  SHARED ACROSS GENES. That is what makes them cheap: one global alphabet, reused by every gene.
  A globally shared class map can capture at most the BETWEEN-MOTIF share of the signal, which is
  a minority here. That is not a new failure; it is the explanation of one already recorded. It is
  why the human class ladder was flat across its whole range while yeast's separated its ends by
  8.6 floors, and why C = 1 already sat inside one floor of identity on human data.

  AND THE ACCURACY STANDARD BEHIND EVERY CAP IS NOW UNSUPPORTED IN A SPECIFIC WAY. signed.py's
  C = 64 of 404 was measured in yeast, within a handful of fixed sequence contexts. The human data
  says the shared-across-genes component -- the only component a global class map can exploit --
  is a minority of the effect. So the caps are cost statements at an accuracy standard that was
  measured under conditions where context was held fixed, and applied to a network where it is
  not.

WHERE THAT LEAVES THE ENGINE, PLAINLY. It is a correct and well-tested machine for a factorisation
whose per-gene conditionals must be learned per gene. Everything built to make those conditionals
CHEAP by sharing structure across genes is undercut by the same measurement, and the cheapness was
the entire point -- without it the per-gene factor is exponential in k_i and the cap returns to 3.
The honest position is that the engine's mathematics is sound, its cost model is sound, and the
compression that made the cost model interesting rests on an assumption that human data does not
support and that yeast data could not have tested, because yeast held context fixed by design.


=================================================================================================
SHARING CLASSES ACROSS GROUPS OF GENES: THE GROUPS COLLAPSE TO ONE NUMBER
=================================================================================================
The compression's damaged assumption is that classes are SHARED ACROSS GENES. The obvious repair
is to share them across GROUPS instead -- G class maps, interpolating between a global map (cheap,
captures only the between-motif minority) and a per-gene map (adequate, no saving). This tested it.

THE CEILING GATE RAN FIRST AND CHANGED THE MODULE. Grouping is worth building only if the context
variance is not already explained by something cheaper, and it is. ONE per-enhancer scalar -- the
wild-type activity of the context, how strong it is before anything is removed -- absorbs 94.2% of
the context variance in HepG2 and 72.2% in K562. Stronger contexts lose more when a site is
removed. That is saturation, and it is not surprising once stated.

AND THE CONTROL THAT NEARLY KILLED IT. The disruption effect is activity(mutant) minus
activity(wild type), so the covariate sits on both sides of the equation and shared measurement
noise manufactures exactly this correlation. Measured ACROSS replicates, where the two noises are
independent:

    cell     same-replicate      cross-replicate
    HepG2    -0.865, -0.880      -0.758, -0.758
    K562     -0.811, -0.698      -0.546, -0.441

It survives. Shared noise inflates it from about -0.76 to -0.87 in HepG2 but does not create it,
and every coefficient below is cross-fitted.

THE LADDER, FIT ON REPLICATE 1 AND SCORED ON REPLICATE 2, AGAINST THE NOISE LIMIT.

    model                                HepG2    x limit      K562    x limit   parameters
    global class map                    0.6648       1.69    0.9168       1.46          136
    global map x per-gene GAIN          0.5391       1.37    0.9693       1.54          137
    global map + per-gene SCALAR        0.4723       1.20    0.8176       1.30          138
    per-group class maps, G = 2         0.6129       1.56    0.9027       1.43          272
    per-group class maps, G = 8         0.5539       1.41    0.9125       1.45         1088
    per-group class maps, G = 16        0.5514       1.40    0.9124       1.45         2176
    per-group class maps, G = 32        0.5604       1.42    0.9185       1.46         4352
    the NOISE LIMIT                     0.3935       1.00    0.6295       1.00           --

    one scalar closes 71% (HepG2) and 35% (K562) of the gap from the global map to the limit
    the best group model closes 42% and 5% of it, at up to 4,352 parameters against 138

GROUPS DO NOT PAY. In both cell types a single per-gene number beats every group model tested, at
3 to 32 times fewer parameters, and the group ladder is flat from G = 4 upward. The answer to
"share classes across groups of genes" is that the group structure is one-dimensional and
continuous, so the right object is a covariate rather than a partition.

TWO ERRORS OF MY OWN IN THE FIRST RUN, RECORDED RATHER THAN EDITED AWAY.

  THE PREDICTED FORM WAS WRONG. I reasoned to a MULTIPLICATIVE form -- a shared class effect
  scaled by a per-gene gain -- by analogy with multiplier.py, which validated exactly that shape on
  the TIME axis. Measured, the ADDITIVE form wins in both cells, by 0.17 and 0.24 of the floor. The
  reasoning by analogy was good and the analogy was false. A shared modulation that multiplies on
  the time axis adds on the gene axis, and nothing in the earlier result implied otherwise; it was
  read as if it did.

  THE "CEILING" WAS NOT A CEILING. The per-enhancer row -- predicting replicate 2 from replicate 1
  of the same construct -- was labelled the bound no model can pass, and two models passed it. It
  carries BOTH replicates' noise so it sits at sqrt(2)*sigma, while a fitted model averages and can
  approach sigma. That is the SAME error humantransfer's H3 had already recorded, made again in the
  very next module. Twice now, a one-measurement predictor has been mistaken for an upper bound.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE ENGINE. The cap does not move either way: a per-gene covariate adds one
global coefficient and changes no gene's alphabet, and G class maps multiply a stored table that
was never the binding term. So this was never a cost question. What it buys is accuracy, and the
finding is that the context-dependence which damaged the compression is mostly ONE OBSERVABLE
SCALAR PER GENE rather than an irreducible per-gene function. That is the cheapest possible repair
-- cheaper than groups, cheaper than per-gene maps -- and it is available to every representation
in this build order without changing any of their cost arithmetic.

It does not restore the yeast accuracy standard. One scalar closes 71% of the gap in HepG2 and 35%
in K562, so a globally shared class map plus a per-gene strength term is still short of the noise
limit, and the copy-number question that the whole count summary is about remains unasked of human
data. But the shape of the repair is now known and it is additive, which is the only kind of repair
this build order has ever found that does not cost an exponent.


=================================================================================================
THE PER-GENE SCALAR IN THE ENGINE, AND THE CAPS RERUN
=================================================================================================
The request was to put the per-gene scalar into the engine and rerun the caps. It is in, it is
free, and the caps do not move. That is the whole result, and two of this module's three planned
gates had to be withdrawn to reach it honestly.

WHAT WENT IN. genegroups found that the context-dependence damaging every compression here is
mostly ONE OBSERVABLE SCALAR PER GENE -- the gene's own baseline strength -- and that sharing class
maps across groups loses to it at 3-32x the parameters. The scalar costs the engine one global
coefficient plus a functional of state it already carries, so nothing new is stored per gene.

V0 PASSES, IN EXECUTED CODE. genegroups asserted in prose that the scalar cannot move the cap.
block.py's E4 and E7 asserted their arithmetic in prose too, were never executed, and were false by
a wide margin -- so this was run, not argued:

     C classes   cap without scalar   cap WITH scalar   same?
             4                   59                59    True
             8                   30                30    True
            16                   15                15    True
            64                   13                13    True

The reason is structural, not numerical: the per-gene factor is a PRODUCT over occupied classes and
the scalar ADDS one value to one factor, so it cannot multiply anything. Adding a value to a factor
that is already a sum of a class's slots changes that factor by a bounded amount and changes no cap
at any C tested.

V1 IS DEGENERATE AND FAILS AS A GATE -- LEDGER U, ON THIS MODULE'S OWN CRITERION. V1 asked the
question that actually matters, since C is what drives the cap: does the scalar buy a SMALLER class
count on human data? Held out on the human MPRA, fit on replicate 1 and scored on replicate 2,
sweeping C with and without the scalar, and taking signed.py's own criterion -- the smallest C
within one noise floor of the finest model. The ladder is narrower than the floor:

    cell     floor    ladder span, no scalar    ladder span, with scalar
    HepG2    0.3935                    0.0615                      0.0003
    K562     0.6295                    0.1006                      0.0213

Every C sits within one floor of the finest model, C = 1 included. "Smallest C within one floor"
therefore returns 1 whatever the truth is, and NO class count may be derived from this data. This
is the flat ladder mpra_human already reported, meeting a criterion built for yeast's, which
separated its ends by 8.6 floors. The criterion is not wrong; it is being asked of a quantity that
has saturated, which is exactly ledger U and exactly what signed.py's D1/D2 and widthblock's
collapse to C = 1 were.

WHAT IS MEASURABLE IN V1 ANYWAY: the scalar's gain, which is real and FLAT IN C -- +0.64 floors at
C = 1 and +0.49 at C = 136 in HepG2, +0.25 and +0.16 in K562. On this data the per-gene scalar does
essentially all the work and the class map almost none. But the class map's flatness is the DATA's
limitation, not a finding about class maps, and it must not be reported as one.

V2 IS WITHDRAWN. The plan was to take V1's measured class count, carry it to TRRUST as a fraction
of the alphabet, and rerun the caps there. V1 cannot supply that number. Had the degeneracy gone
unnoticed the module would have reported "human data says C = 3 suffices, so the cap is 140 at
71.7% coverage" -- a spectacular claim resting entirely on a statistic that returns 1 or 3 by
construction. It is recorded here as a withdrawn gate rather than deleted.

THE CAPS, THEREFORE, STAND EXACTLY AS THEY WERE:

    representation                              cap   coverage
    pattern (exact)                               3     12.8%
    class count C = 64                           13     26.6%
    + hub demotion + factored block             140     71.7%
    + per-gene scalar                          unchanged, at whichever row it is added to

WHAT THIS SETTLES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT. The scalar is a free ACCURACY gain, not a cap gain. V0 is a
statement about the COST functional only: it says the scalar is free, not that it is sufficient.
The class count that sets the cap remains the yeast-derived C = 64, still unvalidated for human,
exactly as humantransfer left it -- and the human data now in hand is provably incapable of
validating it, which is a stronger and more useful statement than "not yet validated." The fraction
transfer from 136 motif classes in a reporter assay to 404 regulators in a curated network remains
an assumption. And the scalar is fitted and tested on SINGLE-SITE contexts, because that is all the
open human data has; whether one scalar still absorbs the context when several sites are present is
the copy-number question, still unasked of human data.

A NOTE ON THE FIRST RUN, WHICH WAS WRONG IN THREE WAYS, ALL MINE. The first version fixed the block
term to the factored representation alone and reported C = 4 giving cap 59 against signed.py's 419
-- the fix is to take the MINIMUM over all three block representations, which the engine has always
been entitled to do. V1's degeneracy was not detected, and V2 propagated it into a cap table. All
three were repaired before the run whose numbers appear above, and the module now refuses V1
explicitly instead of passing it.


=================================================================================================
WHAT DATA WOULD VALIDATE C? THE QUESTION IS PARTLY MALFORMED, AND HERE IS WHY
=================================================================================================
humantransfer and scalarcap both closed by naming what C needs in PROSE -- "many more replicates,
or a human assay with the yeast experiment's structure". Prose is where this build order has
repeatedly gone wrong: block.py's E4 and E7 were prose arithmetic and false by a wide margin. So
this converted the wish into numbers. It did not run a new experiment; it computed how big one
would have to be, and priced the catalogue that exists against that number. The answer that came
back was not the expected one.

THE CEILING GATE FIRST, AS ALWAYS, AND IT CLOSES THE CHEAP ROUTE. A live census of the ENCODE
K562 CRISPRi RNA-seq catalogue: 77 released experiments, 74 distinct factors, replicate histogram
{2: 66, 3: 8, 4: 3}. Against thresholds predeclared BEFORE the query -- 128 factors and 7
replicates -- it fails on both. Not the 48 factors cached here: the ENTIRE released catalogue
cannot express C = 64 and cannot reach the depth. "Download more of the same" is not the answer.

D1 WAS MINE AND IT WAS WRONG, AND IT IS LEFT IN THE OUTPUT SO THE ERROR IS VISIBLE. D1 set its
bar on the between-regulator sd, 1.05 floors, and returned R(bar = 1) = 2 replicates -- a bar the
current data ALREADY MEETS, which can say nothing about what to fix. The quantity that was
degenerate in scalarcap is not the sd. It is the LADDER SPAN, a difference of held-out RMSEs, and
RMSE differences compress a signal. Superseded, not deleted.

THE POWER CALCULATION THAT REPLACED IT, AND THE RESULT NOBODY WAS LOOKING FOR. Simulate a world
in which the class hypothesis is EXACTLY TRUE with C = 64 classes shared across genes, at the
measured between-regulator variance and the measured floor, with TRRUST's real depth
distribution. Build the ladder the way signed.py and humantransfer build it. Sweep panel size N
and replicate depth R.

    THE KNEE NEVER REACHES 64 ANYWHERE IN THE SWEEP. It climbs 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 as R goes 2 to 128,
    with the truth held fixed at 64 the whole time. At N = 48 it is 1 to 8 at every depth,
    because a panel of 48 cannot express 64 classes however often it is replicated -- depth
    cannot buy alphabet.

AND THE CONTROL THAT DECIDES WHAT THAT MEANS. Two incompatible readings: either the estimator is
resolution-limited whatever the truth is, or 64 levels drawn from a continuous spread simply are
not separable at these depths. So: rerun with the 64 class effects EQUALLY SPACED ONE FLOOR
APART, separable by construction. If the estimator can ever return 64, it returns it here.

    IT RETURNS 16. At every panel size and every depth, on a ladder spanning 17.7 floors.

    knee ~ R^0.57 -- the signature of a RESOLUTION statistic, the number of distinguishable
    levels growing as the square root of the precision. A structural count would be FLAT in R:
    it would find 64 and stop.

SO "SMALLEST C WITHIN ONE FLOOR OF THE FINEST MODEL" CANNOT RETURN A STRUCTURAL CLASS COUNT AT
ANY DEPTH, ON ANY PANEL. Pooled across both simulation families -- continuously spread effects
and separated ones -- the law is

    knee  =  0.88 * (signal in floors) ^ 1.05        R^2 = 0.947

C is a QUANTISATION COUNT: the regulator effect spread divided by the accuracy demanded of it.
Demand less accuracy and C falls; demand more and C rises. That makes C a design parameter of the
engine, not a fact about human cells waiting to be measured -- which is why no dataset validates
it, and why the search for one was the wrong search.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH, STATED BECAUSE THE DISCREPANCY IS REAL. The law is measured INSIDE
the simulation. It licenses the statement that the estimator returns a resolution -- it could not
return 64 even when told 64 was true, and signed.py uses that estimator. It does NOT license
reading 64 off the law. The one crude yeast signal-to-floor figure available is about 16 floors,
which the law maps to a knee near 16, not 64. That 4x discrepancy is unexplained here and is
itself the reason not to over-read the reframing. Reconciling it needs signed.py's ladder rebuilt
in floors, which was not done.

THE CAP IS A CURVE, AND scalarcap's 59 WAS ITS OWN LOOP CEILING
=================================================================================================
Pushing the law through the cap functional required evaluating it at low C, and that exposed a
defect in the module shipped one turn earlier. scalarcap called cap_with_scalar with maxC = 60
and reported a cap of 59 at C = 4. 59 is 60 - 1: the functional walks the controller count upward
until the bar is breached and stops at its own ceiling, so that row WAS THE CEILING AND NOT A CAP.
Recomputed with the ceiling raised to 2000:

    C classes    cap, controllers      as previously reported
            1              >= 1999      not reported
            2              >= 1999      not reported
            4                  442      59      <- wrong by 7.5x, and it was a ceiling
            8                   30      30
           16                   15      15
           64                   13      13

scalarcap's V0 VERDICT SURVIVES UNCHANGED: with and without the per-gene scalar both hit the same
ceiling, so the scalar still changes no cap, and that was what V0 measured. But the NUMBER 59 was
a saturated statistic reported as a measurement -- ledger U -- in the very module that withdrew
its own V1 for exactly that. Two gates in one module, one caught and one missed, and the one that
was missed had already been pushed. The C = 8, 16 and 64 rows broke on the bar rather than the
ceiling and are unaffected.

READ AS A CURVE, THE ENGINE'S POSITION IS SHARPER THAN ANY SINGLE CAP EVER MADE IT. The cost of
accuracy is not gradual: between C = 4 and C = 8 the cap falls 442 to 30, a factor of 15 for one
doubling of resolution. Every cap this build order has reported -- 3, 13, 140 -- is ONE ROW of
that curve, and the row was chosen in yeast.

=================================================================================================
THE REST OF THE SPECIFICATION, FOR THE PART OF THE QUESTION THAT IS WELL POSED
=================================================================================================
C is not validatable as a structural constant. The signal-to-floor ratio that SETS it is, and so
are the other three axes, so the spec still has content.

THE ALPHABET AXIS is a hard ceiling, not a power calculation: a panel of N regulators cannot
return C > N. The coverage scaling law, bracketed between the best panel of size N (highest
out-degree first) and the worst (drawn at random, mean of 40 draws), counting TRRUST targets
reached at each regulator depth:

        N          k>=2         k>=4         k>=8        k>=20
       48      857/64       303/7         64/1          6/0
       74      987/108      349/16        89/2         11/0
      128     1128/238      433/50       132/9         20/0
      200     1225/398      509/99       167/21        27/2
      300     1296/613      561/183      187/46        34/5
      794     1416/1416     639/639      221/221       42/42

THE CEILING OF THE IDEAL EXPERIMENT -- every one of TRRUST's 794 regulators perturbed -- is 42
targets at k >= 20. That is the entire population available to test the regime the 140 cap
actually lives in, and no panel can do better, because TRRUST does not know of more regulators
for those genes.

THE DEPTH AXIS REFUSED ITSELF, AS PREDECLARED. Does the identity margin grow with the number of
regulators a gene has? k >= 2: 1.05 floors, CI [0.91, 1.18]. k >= 3: 1.08, CI [0.89, 1.24].
k >= 4: 1.19, CI [0.92, 1.42]. The CIs overlap across every bin, the deepest holds 26 genes, and
the gate's predeclared response to overlapping CIs is that the scaling is UNMEASURABLE and no
extrapolation to k = 20 is permitted. The spec carries the depth requirement as an unknown.

THE COMBINATORIAL AXIS IS WHAT NO AMOUNT OF SINGLE KNOCKDOWNS SUPPLIES. The per-gene factor is a
PRODUCT over occupied classes of (k_ij (L+1) + 1) -- a statement about JOINT states. Every
measurement used here is a MARGINAL. Two regulators can have identical marginals and different
joint behaviour, and the class hypothesis asserts they do not. TRRUST offers 46,712 same-target
regulator pairs and 615,193 triples; the pair count is the size of the smallest design that could
ask the question. This is the copy-number question, still unasked of human data.

APPLICABILITY DOMAIN, because a number without one is ledger S. A C measured on CRISPRi knockdown
log-ratios is a C for that response function. The engine's C is for a steady-state transcription
rate. The two coincide only if the knockdown response is monotone in the same latent quantity the
rate depends on -- an assumption no module here has tested.

TWO NAMED CANDIDATES, NEITHER DOWNLOADED NOR TESTED, AND FLAGGED AS SUCH. Genome-scale Perturb-seq
(Replogle et al., Cell 2022, PMID 35688146, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2022.05.013; via PubMed) targets
all expressed genes with CRISPRi across more than 2.5 million human cells, the only published
design plausibly meeting the alphabet and depth criteria. For the combinatorial criterion the
nearest published design is the CRISPRa genetic-interaction map of Norman et al. (Science 2019,
PMID 31395745, doi:10.1126/science.aax4438; via PubMed). Per-perturbation depth is not stated in
either abstract, so whether either clears the bar is unknown. This is exactly the status
humantransfer gave the human MPRA before it was run -- and when that one WAS run, it failed. A
named candidate is not a validated one.

THE HONEST BOTTOM LINE. The question "what data would validate C" has no answer, because C is not
the kind of thing data validates: it is the resolution at which one chooses to represent regulator
effects, and the criterion this build order used to measure it returns that resolution and never a
structural count. What data CAN settle is the signal-to-floor ratio that sets C, and the joint
behaviour the cost functional actually reads -- and for those the requirement is a
multi-perturbation panel over at least 128 regulators, which no released dataset in this cell line
provides. Until then the caps are not wrong; they are one row of a curve, chosen in yeast.

=================================================================================================
WHAT ACCURACY SHOULD THE ENGINE TARGET? THE WRONG TERM WAS BEING OPTIMISED
=================================================================================================
whatdata left C as a CHOICE rather than a constant, which raises the question of what to choose it
by. The ground has to be an error budget: an approximation should not be polished far below the
largest error the engine already carries, because precision under the binding term buys nothing
and costs an exponent. This priced them against each other, and the answer is that the class map
-- the thing this build order has spent modules optimising -- is not the binding term.

FIRST, A CORRECTION TO THE MODULE THAT ASKED THE QUESTION. whatdata derived an accuracy per class
count by inverting a law fitted INSIDE its own simulation and reported it as the useful new
object. It was not new. signed.py MEASURED it on held-out yeast promoters, and that column has
been sitting beside the cap column this ledger has quoted throughout:

       C   MEASURED floors from identity    whatdata SIMULATED
       1                            6.29                 0.930
       2                            2.61                 0.480
       4                            2.09                 0.247
       8                            1.52                 0.127
      16                            1.45                 0.066
      64                            0.19                 0.017

The simulated column is 7 to 11 times too small at every point and is WITHDRAWN. whatdata's claim
that C = 64 demands 1.7% of a noise floor is wrong; the measured demand is 0.19 floors, 19%. What
whatdata's simulation DID establish -- that the ladder criterion returns a resolution and not a
structural count -- stands, because that came from the estimator's behaviour rather than from the
fitted constant.

AND THE SATURATION DEFECT HAS A THIRD INSTANCE, IN THE FOUNDATIONAL MODULE. signed.py's cap column
reports 419 at C = 1, 2 AND 4. Its class_caps walks nC over range(1, maxC) with maxC = 420, so 419
is maxC - 1 and three identical values at exactly the ceiling is the signature of a loop that
never breached the bar. Recomputed with the ceiling raised to 2500:

    representation        as reported     recomputed
    count                         419        >= 2499
    class C = 1                   419        >= 2499
    class C = 2                   419        >= 2499
    class C = 4                   419            442
    class C = 8                    25             25
    class C = 16                   15             15

So "the total count promised cap 419 and 93.3% coverage" UNDERSTATES the promise -- the count
summary is cheaper than the ledger has been saying, by at least a factor of six. It does not
rescue the count summary, which was refuted on ACCURACY at 6.29 floors and not on cost. Two
independent cap functionals now agree at 442 for C = 4, which is the cross-check that the
recomputation is real. This is the third instance of the same defect after scalarcap's 59 and
whatdata's 199, and all three were the same mistake: reading a loop ceiling as a measurement.

=================================================================================================
THE UNIT PROBLEM, WHICH IS WHY THE RANKING WAS NEVER DONE BEFORE
=================================================================================================
"0.19 noise floors from identity" is an error in a promoter's log2 expression. THE ENGINE DOES NOT
REPORT LOG2 EXPRESSION. It reports a rare-event tail -- the probability that a whole target set is
simultaneously ON across a window -- and a small error in a per-gene response can be enormous or
negligible in that tail depending on how it propagates. Every module in this build order has
compared its error terms in floors, which is the wrong unit for all of them. This one converts:
it substitutes the coarser representation into the ASSEMBLED engine and reads the error off the
tail itself, at 10 controllers, 200 real target rows, window L = 6.

MY OWN FIRST GATE WAS DEGENERATE AND IS SUPERSEDED IN PLACE. A1 swept the pruning BUDGET across
five decades. Two of the five rows retained no paths at all -- a tail of exactly zero is an absent
number, not a small one -- and the other three returned the IDENTICAL tail from the IDENTICAL
retained set, because engine_budget carries a hard cap of 2e7/n retained paths and that cap, not
the budget, decided every row. The "infinity orders of magnitude" it produced was manufactured
from two absent rows. Ledger U, in this module's own gate, in the module that OPENED by correcting
another module for the same class of error.

AND THE SECOND ATTEMPT WAS CONFOUNDED TOO, WHICH IS WORTH RECORDING BECAUSE THE REASON IS REAL.
Sweeping the retained-path cap at a fixed budget of 1e-3 wiped out four of six rows. Not a crash:
the per-level budget is ABSOLUTE, so a smaller cap retains less mass, the retained mass falls below
the budget at the next level, and the pruner then drops everything. Cap and budget were confounded
and the sweep measured their interaction. With the budget set to zero the cap alone decides, and
the sweep finally measures what it claims to.

=================================================================================================
THE RANKING, AND WHY IT HAD TO BE DONE AS A SCALING LAW
=================================================================================================
Compared AT A POINT the pruner's last increment is 0.14 orders against the class ladder's 5.08,
and the class map looks like the binding term. That comparison is wrong, in the way this build
order has been wrong three times before -- prune.py's N2, realkinetics' fixed tau, the grouping
law -- and the rule earned from those is that an approximation is ranked by a SCALING LAW and
never at a point.

The pruner's last step is not its error. Its error is how far the tail still has to travel, and
the tail is RISING MONOTONICALLY and not levelling off. It rises for a structural reason: retaining
more paths can only ADD mass, so every tail this engine reports is a LOWER BOUND.

    log10 tail  =  -100.01 + 0.608 * log10(retained paths)      R^2 = 0.997

    pruning error reaches the class ladder's whole span of 5.08 orders   at 10^12.7 paths
    the full path set the engine is approximating                            10^21.1 paths

The crossing lies BELOW the full set by 8.4 decades. PRUNING IS THE BINDING TERM, and by a wide
margin. The honest size of the extrapolation is stated with it: the slope is measured over about
two decades of retained paths and the full set is nineteen decades further out, so this is NOT a
prediction of the true tail. The claim does not need the far end. The tail is still rising at the
largest set that can be enumerated, and it only has to keep rising for a few more decades -- a
tiny fraction of the way -- before the pruner's error exceeds every representation choice inside
the engine. Nothing measured here suggests it stops.

=================================================================================================
THE ANSWER
=================================================================================================
1. TARGET A BOUND ON THE OBSERVABLE, NOT ON THE DROPPED MASS. The pruner certifies the mass it
   discards, and the tail is not a function of mass -- realkinetics measured that gap at 1.9e29
   and this module measures its consequence: the reported tail is a lower bound still rising at
   0.6 orders per decade of retained paths. This was already named in the record as "the sharpest
   open problem the pruning route leaves behind". It is now also the measured binding term, which
   promotes it from an open problem to THE problem.

2. THEN THE CLASS MAP, set at whatever that bound turns out to allow. Its entire ladder is worth
   5.08 orders, and at the C = 64 operating point it costs zero accuracy because each controller
   is alone in its class.

CHOOSING C FIRST OPTIMISES THE SMALLER TERM. That is what this build order has been doing for
several modules -- signed, poscount, multiplier, genegroups, scalarcap, whatdata -- and the
measurement that would have said so was never made, because every one of those modules compared
its error in noise floors of a log2 expression rather than in the observable the engine reports.
The modules were not wrong in their own terms. They were answering a question that was not the
binding one, and nothing in their gates could have told them, because the unit conversion lives
outside all of them.
