=================================================================================================
THE TWO OPEN PROBLEMS, MEASURED
  1. does a real controller make the important parameters explode?
  2. can grouping keep the joint state small at realistic scale?
Sources: rem/atlas/RESULTS_controller.txt, rem/atlas/RESULTS_statedim.txt
=================================================================================================

THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE. They come apart. A controller is CHEAP IN PARAMETERS and EXPENSIVE IN
STATE: closing the loop leaves the set of rates worth measuring at about five, no matter how big
the system gets, but a single global regulator takes the joint state from 2^4 per variable to 2^N.
Regulation does not increase what must be MEASURED. It increases what must be SIMULATED JOINTLY.

=================================================================================================
PROBLEM 1  --  NO EXPLOSION, AND THE IMPORTANT SET MOVES ONTO THE CONTROLLER
=================================================================================================

    sweep                             P range     N90 range          exponent b
    A  add controllers, fixed plant    45 -> 141   4.90 -> 8.55       +0.4688 +- 0.0503
    B  grow the whole regulated system 37 -> 133   6.75 -> 4.27       -0.2518 +- 0.1251
    predeclared bands: b > 0.7 EXPLOSION, b < 0.3 SPARSE

Sweep B is the one that answers the scaling question, and it is NEGATIVE: N90 does not grow with
the system, it shrinks slightly. Seven-fold more parameters, the same five that matter. Sweep A is
intermediate at +0.47, but even there N90/P falls from 0.109 to 0.061 -- adding regulators adds
important parameters sublinearly, never in proportion.

TRANSFER IS REAL AND IT IS ABRUPT.

    K       controller share of ||C||^2     rank correlation with the open-loop plant ranking
    0                            0.0000                                              1.0000
    1                            0.0926                                              0.4478
    8                            0.1466                                              0.4458
   32                            0.6432                                              0.4536

ONE controller edge is enough to halve the rank correlation. The plant ranking computed open-loop
is not merely incomplete under feedback, it is reordered, and it reorders as soon as the loop
closes at all. At K = 32, seven of the ten largest control coefficients are controller parameters.
So the answer is not "sparse", it is TRANSFER: the same small number of things matter, and they
are different things.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY, per gate C6 as predeclared. Shuffling the regulatory wiring at random --
random target, random sensor, random sign -- gives the same scaling (b = -0.32 +- 0.32 against
-0.19 +- 0.21). The sparsity is a property of closed-loop systems of this class, NOT of the
canonical end-product architecture. C3 therefore says nothing about which biological wiring is
special. It does say the sparsity is robust to getting the wiring wrong, which is the more useful
half for a measurement programme.

GATES:  C1 PASS (adjoint to 1e-9 scaled, both FD directions)   C2 PASS (summation theorem exactly
1.0000000000 in five configurations and correctly BROKEN to 0.8726 for a proteome-sensing
controller)   C5 PASS (||C|| does not collapse, so this is not homeostasis)   C6 NOT SEPARATED
C7 FAIL -- the acceptance bars were six orders too loose and caught only 1 of 3 planted runaways.
Contained: C3 runs canonical wiring only, so no rejected configuration enters the measurement.
The bars were deliberately not retuned.

=================================================================================================
PROBLEM 2  --  THE STATE DOES NOT STAY SMALL, AND THE REASON IS TOPOLOGY, NOT DECAY
=================================================================================================

Dependence decays extremely fast: xi = 0.2486 +- 0.0005, R^2 = 0.999976, a factor of 55 per step.
That is not enough, and the reason is worth stating precisely.

STEP 1 -- THE THRESHOLD IS NOT FREE. From the measured law tail_err = 20.23 sqrt(MI), a 1% tail
tolerance forces MI < 2.44e-07. A bulk threshold of 1e-3, which keeps means fine, would admit
tail errors of 0.64.

STEP 2 -- THE RULE AS WRITTEN RETURNS THE WHOLE NETWORK. MI at distance 1 is 6.3e-03, four orders
above the tail-legal threshold, so every directly regulated pair is joined; and a regulatory
network is connected. Thresholding therefore gives ONE component of N for any connected topology
at any tail tolerance, and costs 2^N. This is not a decay problem. Decay is fast.

STEP 3 -- THE RIGHT COST IS TREEWIDTH. A path is one component but has treewidth 1.

    topology              N = 20,000    treewidth    state cost
    cascade                                      3     3.20e+05      tractable
    random degree 2                            >40     2.69e+14      not
    random degree 3                       too dense to compute       not
    scale-free m = 2                      too dense to compute       not

Only a pure cascade survives. Anything with mean degree 2 or more is past treewidth 40 by N = 512.
Robustness (S8b): at r = 1, the BARE regulatory graph and the most optimistic dependence graph
there could be, the answer is unchanged -- random degree 2 and scale-free are already past 40 at
N = 512. The obstruction is the topology of gene regulation, not the range of the dependence.

STEP 4 -- A GLOBAL CONTROLLER MAKES IT WORSE, AND CONDITIONING ONLY SOMETIMES SAVES IT. One hub
makes the dependence graph COMPLETE: treewidth N. Putting the controller into every bag drops it
back to 4 -- but only when the controller's timescale is far from its targets':

    tau_hub / tau_gene      r_cond       cost per gene
              2000             3              2^5        affordable
               500             4              2^6        affordable
               333            12            2^14         marginal
        200 down to 0.01      >=15           2^N         no grouping helps
              0.0025           5              2^7        affordable
              0.0014           3              2^5        affordable

THE WINDOW: the controller must be at least ~500x SLOWER than what it controls, so that its state
IS its history, or at least ~400x FASTER, where it correlates nothing to begin with. Within a
few hundred either way, conditioning fails and the cost is 2^N. Kinase signalling at seconds
against transcription at tens of minutes is a ratio of roughly 100 to 1000 -- ON that boundary,
not comfortably inside it.

THE MECHANISM, and it is the reason a prediction was lost. The original S4 predicted conditioning
on a controller would restore decay. It does not: the correlation is carried by the controller's
TRAJECTORY, not by its current value, so conditioning on the instantaneous state leaves it. A slow
controller is quasi-static and its state is its history; a fast one is white noise and correlates
nothing. In between, its history is genuinely extra state.

GATES:  S1 PASS (residual < 1e-14; MI exactly 0 on independent variables)   S2 PASS   S5 PASS (the
dependence graph IS path^3, which is what licenses extrapolating past N = 16)   S6 bracketed
S8 PASS   S3 FAIL -- xi scattered 0.0045 against a 0.0043 bar. Non-monotone in N and surviving a
common fit range, so wobble rather than drift; and the quantity used downstream is the INTEGER r,
which a 1.7% wobble cannot move. The gate failed; the conclusion does not rest on it.

=================================================================================================
WHAT THIS CHANGES
=================================================================================================

FOR MEASUREMENT. Problem 1's answer is good news and it redirects the programme. The rates worth
measuring under feedback are the REGULATORY ones -- gains, thresholds, cooperativities -- not the
catalytic ones, and an open-loop ranking of catalytic rates is reordered by the first controller
edge you add. This is consistent with what the corrected Recon3D run already said from the other
direction: with a sealed medium, zero enzyme capacities have a realised sensitivity.

FOR THE ENGINE. Problem 2's answer is bad news and it is structural. Section 14's split criterion,
applied at a tail-legal threshold, returns the whole network on any connected topology. The repair
is a tree decomposition rather than a threshold, and then the engine is tractable exactly on
low-treewidth topologies -- which real gene regulation is not. Three routes remain and none is
tested here: exploit that the tail-legal graph is a bounded graph POWER (structure a generic
treewidth bound ignores), accept bounded error from a fixed-width decomposition and measure what
it costs in tail accuracy, or carry controller history explicitly so conditioning works at matched
timescales.

FOR HONESTY. Two gates failed in this pair of modules and both are recorded rather than retuned:
controller C7 (bars six orders too loose, contained) and statedim S3 (a real miss that does not
propagate). One prediction of mine was made and lost before commit, and is kept as made.

=================================================================================================
ROUTE 1 OF 3 TESTED: THE BOUNDED GRAPH POWER.  IT FAILS, AND THE FAILURE IS SPECIFIC
Source: rem/atlas/RESULTS_localclosure.txt
=================================================================================================

WHAT WAS TRIED. Not lifting a tree decomposition from G to G^r -- S8(b) already killed that, since
treewidth is past 40 at r = 1. Instead, giving up exact marginalisation: if every dependence above
tau is confined to distance r, factorise on r-balls,

    P_hat(x) = prod_i P( x_i | x_{B_r(i) and earlier} )

a Bayesian network whose cost is sum_i 2^(1+|pa_i|) -- linear in N, no treewidth term anywhere.

THE APPROXIMATION WORKS. On a cascade, tested at three tail depths down to P = 1.3e-13:

    r   max|pa|   tail rel err at P=1.3e-13    max error in any mean
    1         1                    1.063e+00                5.0e-16
    2         2                    4.604e-02                4.4e-16
    3         3                    5.509e-03                6.1e-16
    4         4                    1.190e-03                5.6e-16

This is the sharpest instance of the bulk/tail split in this build order, and the cleanest,
because here the bulk error is EXACTLY ZERO by construction -- the factorisation reproduces every
marginal in the ball -- while the conjunctive event is wrong by 106% at r = 1. Six modules have
now measured a bulk quantity right while the tail was wrong; this one measures it at machine
precision against a 100% tail error in the same row.

THE COST DOES NOT WORK, ON REAL TOPOLOGY. TRRUST v2 human, 2,861 genes, 8,403 edges:

    r    median ball    90th    99th     max      gene at the max
    1              3      12      62     480      SP1
    2            118     615    1177    1813      CDKN1A
    3           1381    2169    2532    2724      SP1

The 3-ball around SP1 is 2,724 of 2,861 genes -- 95% of the network in one factor. Cost is set by
the WORST ball, so the median of 1,381 is not a reprieve, and quoting it would be ledger S. The
closure costs 2^2724 against a junction tree's 2^40+. The route does not merely fail to help; on
real topology it is worse than what it replaces.

HUB CONDITIONING DOES NOT RESCUE IT. Deleting the h highest-degree genes and conditioning:

    h        max|B_2|   treewidth    total cost = 2^h x N x 2^max|B_2|
    0            1813         >40                            4.6e+63
   20             682         >40                            4.8e+69
  200              44          23                            7.5e+76
  400              14           3                            6.5e+67

The ball sizes fall exactly as hoped -- 400 deletions take treewidth to 3 -- but 2^400 eats the
saving many times over. There is no h that wins.

THE ASSUMPTION UNDERNEATH WAS CHECKED, AND IT HELD. statedim's S5 verified "dependence graph =
G^r" on a cascade and a random graph, never on a hub, and the whole cost verdict depends on two of
SP1's 484 targets really being dependent above tau. Measured exactly (L7):

    out-degree 4, 8, 14  ->  sibling MI 1.35e-4, 1.55e-4, 1.36e-4     FLAT
    in-degree 1 .. 12    ->  sibling MI 1.50e-4 down to 4.89e-7       307x, and still above tau

Out-degree does not dilute AT ALL: a hub with fourteen targets correlates them exactly as strongly
as one with four, because its state is shared by all of them equally. In-degree does dilute, as
roughly its square, but is still above tau at in-degree 12 -- and TRRUST's mean in-degree is 2.9,
where sibling dependence sits ~80x above tau. So the ball sizes are not overestimates.

WHAT THIS LEAVES. The failure is now specific rather than general, which is worth more than the
route would have been:

  - the r-ball FACTORISATION is sound and cheap in accuracy: r = 3 buys 0.55% error on a 1e-13
    event with three-variable factors
  - the r-ball GEOMETRY is what fails, and only because of hubs
  - hubs cannot be conditioned away arithmetically (2^h), cannot be diluted away by out-degree,
    and are not diluted enough by biological in-degree

So the obstruction has been localised to one thing: a small number of very high out-degree
regulators, whose targets are mutually dependent no matter how many of them there are. Any route
that survives has to treat those specific vertices differently -- not by conditioning on their
STATE, which costs 2^h and which statedim's S6 says is only legitimate at a 500x timescale
separation anyway. The two remaining routes from statedim are untouched: bounded-width
decomposition with measured error, and carrying controller history explicitly.

ONE BOUND DID NOT SURVIVE. tail_err <= 20.23 sqrt(MI) fails in 5 of 16 rows where the MI is above
its own numerical floor, so it cannot be used to CHOOSE r. r has to be chosen from a measured
error curve. (L2 as first written compared against the law at every r including rows where the MI
was below its floor -- a guard statedim's S2 had and this gate did not; both floors are now
measured, the tail statistic's by solving the stationary equation twice from independent starts
and differencing, which gives 3.8e-16.)
