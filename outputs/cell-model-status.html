What this cell model can answer, what it cannot, and how each was found out

The goal was a model that answers anything in the cell — any mutation, any protein change, any chromosome fold, drug effect, side effect, cancer. As stated that cannot fail, so it was turned into axes that can. Every number here is read out of a run that declared its thresholds before it executed.

Three numbers, not one

Asked means the perturbation is addressable — there's a slot you can hand it to. Got wrong means one module both consumes that slot and records a result scored against a named control; a number that cannot be wrong is not an answer. Answered well means every gate in that pipeline came back positive. The third is the honest one — the second counts questions that are now falsifiable, which is real progress and is not the same as knowing the answer.

The single most informative pair of results

Same predictor. Same confound. Opposite outcome.

Mean essentiality of a drug's targets, with target count partialled out identically both times:

Side-effect count → partial +0.0515, inside the null. Failed.
Cytotoxic class → partial 0.7233, far outside it. Passed.

The reason was written down before either number existed. An adverse event happens in a patient, after distribution, metabolism and a tissue's response — none of which this model represents. Cytotoxicity happens in a dividing cell, which is the one thing it does. The model succeeds where its scope reaches and fails where it doesn't, which is a better shape than both passing.

29

Was the cancer inversion real?

Loop 14 reported essentiality anti-predicting drivers at 0.2990 after partialling out citations — which predict the label at 0.8259. Regressing out a confound that strong is how reversed residuals get manufactured. Matched pairs instead of regression: 0.4949, inside the null. The inversion was over-control and the claim is withdrawn. What survives is sharper: the two driver roles point opposite ways — loss-of-function 0.4717, activating 0.5248 — and cancel. A tumour suppressor must be deletable so it cannot be essential; an oncogene is switched on rather than lost. Thin margin, one test.

31

Chain a point mutation in DNA through the fold, to a residue, into nexus.

Four links joined end to end on real variants. The middle one is computed, not looked up: genomic coordinate → walk the transcript's coding exons → CDS offset → codon → substitute the base (complemented on a minus-strand gene) → translate. Nothing about the protein consequence is read from an annotation, because a chain whose middle link is a lookup is a join and not a model.

It is verified against a source it never saw. Reference base matches the CDS at 100.00% of 966,223 variants; the computed consequence agrees with ClinVar's held-back field at 99.99%. TP53 R175H, KRAS G12D and BRAF V600E all land on the right residue from raw coordinates. 56,258 mutations carry all four links.

Then the part that fails. Scored within gene — so the chain cannot pass by rediscovering which genes are pathogenic — torsion at the variant's own base gives 0.5054 against a null of 0.5063, and BLOSUM62, a substitution table from 1992, beats every structural layer at 0.6623.

32

That test of nexus was unfair. Do it properly.

Loop 31 called nexus_activity(ddg, 0.0) — binding pinned at zero, because an arbitrary ClinVar gene has no interface partner — then reported near-chance as a verdict on nexus. But nexus had already published that exact configuration at 0.562, already measured it blind to 94% of binding-breakers, and already stated that the hop to whole-cell phenotype "does not compose". Its task is not pathogenicity at all; it is whether a mutation breaks a protein–protein interface.

Rebuilt on its own task, its claim holds: intrinsic 0.625, dual 0.770, fusion gain +0.145 against a recorded +0.193. The absolute numbers miss the gate (621 mutations here vs 808 there) so F1 stays failed — but the difference, which is what nexus actually asserts, reproduces.

And my excuse for loop 31's inverted sensor was falsified by my own test. Restricting to confident structure moves it 0.4806 → 0.4902 — toward chance, not toward correct — and burial makes it worse (0.4803 buried vs 0.5104 exposed). Struck. The real limit is reach: only 30.4% of these variants sit in a gene with any multi-chain experimental structure, so for seven in ten, nexus's second sensor cannot fire at all.

–41

Build a 4D chromatin model that behaves like DNA in a nucleus, on four CPUs.

The engine is diagonalised, not integrated. A chromosome held by harmonic bonds — backbone plus cohesin loops — is a Gaussian network, so one eigendecomposition of its Laplacian yields the contact map and the whole time axis in closed form. No timesteps anywhere. 0.42 s per configuration at 1,926 bins.

Validated first against physics fixed before the code existed: the ideal-chain identity to 5e-11, the Gaussian exponent at exactly −1.5000, the Rouse exponent 0.4939. And exactly wrong for chromatin — real chr21 measures −0.964, so the bare polymer is the null, not the answer.

Then the mechanism. Loop extrusion on GM12878's own CTCF, with motif direction scanned from the sequence, using literature parameters only. P(s) went −1.262 → −1.010 against a measured −0.964. The orientation signature came out +0.2278 against a measured +0.3788 and inverted to −0.042 when orientations were shuffled — that inversion is what makes it extrusion and not coincidence.

The fourth dimension. Two clocks: relaxation inside a fixed loop set, and rewiring of the set itself. MSD exponent 0.354; loop lifetime 14.8 min out for 15.0 min in; and single configurations reach r = 0.17 where their average reaches 0.22 — TADs are a population object here, exactly as single-cell Hi-C reports.

Held out by chromosome, mechanism beat fitting. On chr22, unseen: physics +0.2852 with zero fitted parameters, a trained CNN +0.0893, smoothed CTCF density +0.1110. The network's orientation-shuffled twin scored higher (+0.1290) than the real one — it learned where CTCF binds, never which way it points.

And the ceiling is the same fact as the speed. Insulation stalled at 0.297 against a 0.40 gate, and two attempts at compartments both collapsed P(s) — the second exposed by its own control, where shuffled GC scored higher than real GC. A long-range harmonic bond is a permanent shortcut; an attraction is not. The linearity that makes one eigendecomposition deliver everything is the same property that forbids distance-dependent attraction. Stopping rules were declared before each run and honoured.

Five limits on the rows that pass

  • Effects are modest. ρ ≈ 0.18 for torsion; AUC ≈ 0.72 for cytotoxic class; R² ≈ 0.43 for protein abundance. Real, controlled, and not large.
  • Three of this page's own earlier claims have been withdrawn. It used to say the model predicts non-drivers at 0.7010 (loop 29: 0.4949, inside the null); that nothing here predicts a quantity (loop 22: R² 0.4332); and that nexus's sensor "contributes nothing" (loop 32: that was one of its two sensors, run against a target it disclaims). Struck through above rather than quietly deleted.
  • The feedback that started this still does not earn its place. On a model that cannot eat ATP, with the reference growth rate fixed, its deficit went −0.0074 → −0.0013. The sign never flipped.
  • Recall is the binding limit and its obvious fix is a measured dead end. The model finds a third of real dependencies; the layer that separates the misses makes the predictor worse when you add it.
  • There is now a real 4D model, and its ceiling is the same fact as its speed. Loops 33–41 built a Gaussian-network chromosome with loop extrusion that reproduces P(s), the CTCF orientation signature, sub-diffusive motion and correct loop turnover, at 0.42 s per configuration — and beats a trained CNN on an unseen chromosome. But insulation stalls at 0.30 against a 0.40 gate and compartments fail twice, because a long-range harmonic bond is a permanent shortcut and an attraction is not. The linearity that makes it cheap is what caps it. Separately, the earlier 1D chromatin line stands as it was: what once looked like a fold was a retrieval model's top-8 list at 0.99% recall, and the torsion track that replaced it works at gene scale, carries nothing at variant scale, and contributes −0.0012 to the integrated model.

84 gates passed, 32 failed, across forty-one loops. Both numbers are reported because a project where every gate passes was not gating anything. Ten results have been retracted or corrected after being measured more carefully — most of them faults in the instrument rather than the data: an audit that credited itself, a search through its own vocabulary, reachability through a shared 38 MB hub, a control that was an algebraic identity, a control that stratified by its own target, a partialled confound strong enough to manufacture the anti-correlation it was meant to remove, a verdict on a dual-sensor model that only ran one of its sensors, a data ledger whose paths pointed at a directory the files had never been in, a demonstration constant carried into a physical run, and a consistency check that was wrong twice before becoming an identity. Each correction is recorded in the module that made the original claim, and the capability count has gone down twice as a result.
